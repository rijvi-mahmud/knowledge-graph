---
title: Patient records
description: 'Who the patient is, kept as one trustworthy record: registration, demographics, identifiers, duplicates and merges, death, personal representatives and the patient''s privacy rights.'
module: patient
version: 0.6.1
status: draft
industry: healthcare
purpose: 'Keep one accurate record of who each patient is, so every clinical and administrative module attaches its data to the right person: register patients without creating duplicates, keep demographics and identifiers with their history, find and resolve duplicate records, record death, record who may act for the patient, and honour the patient''s rights over their data. Works in the United States and the European Union.'
scope:
- Searching for a patient, and registering one only when no existing record matches
- Names, birth date, administrative gender, addresses, phone numbers and email, with their history
- Identifiers, such as medical record numbers and national health identifiers
- Optional demographics a deployment chooses to collect, such as race and ethnicity or gender identity
- Contacts, including emergency contacts
- Personal representatives and proxies, and whether one may act for the patient
- Confidential communication preferences
- Finding duplicate records, merging them, and undoing a wrong merge
- Recording and retracting death
- Restricted records, such as for staff or public figures
- Requests to correct, restrict or erase patient data
- A log of who viewed or changed each record
- Validating every field a patient record holds
- How identity was checked at registration, and confirming details on return visits
- Patient photographs for identification, where the deployment collects them
- Warnings for patients with the same or similar names
- Representatives of minors ending at the age of majority or emancipation
- Patients updating their own contact details
- Requests for a copy of patient data
- Legal holds that stop a record being erased
- Bulk imports, such as from a legacy system
- Names in more than one script or language
- Recorded sex, kept apart from administrative gender
- Limits and alerts on patient searches
- Passing accepted corrections on to those who received the data
- Address confidentiality program addresses
- Patients' immediate electronic access to their own record
nonGoals:
- Clinical facts about the patient, such as allergies, problems, sex for clinical use or care plans. Clinical modules own them.
- Insurance and coverage. They belong to the Coverage module, which refers to the patient by id.
- Consent to treatment, to sharing and to research, and EU opt-outs and access restrictions on health data. They belong to the Consent module.
- Logins, passwords and patient portal accounts. They belong to Identity, which links an account to a patient id.
- Printing documents and sending messages. Other modules ask this one where to send them.
- Biometric identification, such as palm or face scans.
- Exchanging records with other organisations or national services. An Exchange module does that, using the patient id and identifiers.
- Moving entries recorded on the wrong patient (overlays). Each clinical module moves its own entries; this module only reports merges and unmerges.
- Accounting of disclosures and copies of the whole medical record. They belong to the Release of information module.
- The Notice of Privacy Practices and its acknowledgment. They belong to the Consent module.
actors:
- name: Registrar
  description: Staff who register patients and keep their details up to date, such as front desk or admissions staff.
- name: Health information management (HIM) specialist
  description: Resolves duplicate records, merges and unmerges them, and decides correction requests.
- name: Clinician
  description: Reads patient details to provide care.
- name: Patient
  description: The person the record is about.
- name: Personal representative
  description: 'A person allowed to act for the patient: a parent, guardian or other legal representative, or a proxy the patient chose.'
- name: Privacy officer
  description: Reviews who accessed patient records, and handles requests about patient data.
- name: System
  description: Another module, or this one acting on its own, such as when it proposes duplicates.
assumptions:
- id: AS-H1
  text: Identity authenticates every caller and says which permissions they hold.
  rationale: Who someone is as a user is a different question from who a patient is. Keeping them apart lets a patient exist with no account.
- id: AS-H2
  text: 'The deployment has a lawful basis to process health data for care: treatment under HIPAA in the United States, and GDPR Article 9(2)(h) in the European Union.'
  rationale: Without it, no patient record could be held at all. The legal basis is the deployment's responsibility, not this module's.
- id: AS-H3
  text: The jurisdiction, record_retention, identifier_systems and collected_fields settings are set with legal advice before go-live.
  rationale: Retention, national identifiers and which demographics may be collected differ by US state and EU member state.
- id: AS-H4
  text: Modules that record care tell this one when they do, so it knows when a patient was last cared for.
  rationale: Retention runs from the last care (BR-H18), and a record with care on it must be merged, not discarded (BR-H11).
- id: AS-H5
  text: The Admission, discharge and transfer module can say whether a patient has an active stay.
  rationale: A merge during a stay needs extra care (BR-H31), and only that module knows who is in hospital.
glossary:
- term: Patient record
  definition: The one record of who a patient is. Clinical data lives in other modules and points to it by id.
- term: Duplicate record
  definition: A second record for a person who already has one. Around 2% of registrations are in error, mostly duplicates (HL7 FHIR).
- term: Merge
  definition: Retiring a duplicate (the source) in favour of the record to keep (the target). The source stays, marked merged, and points to the target.
- term: Unmerge
  definition: Undoing a merge that joined two different people.
- term: Identifier
  definition: A number assigned to the patient by an organisation or government, such as a medical record number or a national health identifier, with the system that issued it.
- term: Administrative gender
  definition: The gender used for administration and record keeping (FHIR Patient.gender). It has no clinical meaning.
- term: Personal representative
  definition: Someone the law treats as the patient for their health information, such as a parent or guardian (45 CFR 164.502(g)), or a proxy the patient authorised (EHDS Article 4(2)).
- term: Restricted record
  definition: A record that needs extra protection, such as a staff member's or a public figure's (HL7 confidentiality R or V).
- term: Statement of disagreement
  definition: A patient's written statement disagreeing with a denied correction. In the United States it is kept with the record (45 CFR 164.526(d)).
concepts:
- name: One record per person
  description: Every other module refers to a patient only by id. When two records turn out to be the same person, the merge tells every module to point at the one that remains.
- name: History, not overwrite
  description: Names, addresses, phone numbers and identifiers change. The old values are kept and marked old, because documents and other systems still use them.

businessRules:
- id: BR-H1
  text: Before a new patient is registered, existing records are searched with at least the name and birth date. If the search finds a probable match, the registrar either picks that record or registers a new one with a reason.
  rationale: HL7 FHIR notes that around 2% of registrations are in error, mostly duplicate records. A duplicate splits one person's history in two, so allergies and results on one are missed on the other.
- id: BR-H2
  text: A record's id never changes and is never reused. Numbers people use, such as medical record numbers, are identifiers on the record, not its id.
  rationale: Other modules hold the id for years. FHIR warns that medical record numbers change, for example when duplicates are merged, so they must not be the id.
- id: BR-H3
  text: An identifier's system and value belong to at most one record that is active or inactive.
  rationale: Two people with the same medical record number means a scan or a lookup can open the wrong person's record.
- id: BR-H4
  text: A record has at least one usual or official name, a birth date and an administrative gender. The birth date can be partial or estimated, and the gender can be unknown, when that is all anyone knows.
  rationale: Name and birth date are what every search and every two-identifier check rely on (BR-H1). Allowing partial and estimated values records what is known instead of inventing it.
- id: BR-H5
  text: A change to a name, address, phone number, email or identifier keeps the old value, marked old, with the period it was in use. Nothing is overwritten.
  rationale: USCDI includes previous name and previous address because documents and other systems still use them. Matching a referral letter in a maiden name needs the old name.
- id: BR-H6
  text: Fields listed in collected_fields are optional for the patient. Race and ethnicity and gender identity are recorded as the patient reports them, with declined as an answer, and are never inferred by staff.
  rationale: OMB SPD 15 says race and ethnicity data should be self-reported wherever possible, and allows several categories. USCDI v3.1 removed sexual orientation and gender identity in 2025 while EU law treats racial or ethnic origin as special category data (GDPR Article 9), so each deployment decides what it collects.
- id: BR-H7
  text: 'A merge retires the source record in favour of the target: the source becomes merged and points to the target with replaced_by, its identifiers are copied to the target and marked old, and patient.merged tells every module to move its data to the target. A merge always records who did it, why, and how the two records differed.'
  rationale: This is the FHIR $merge behaviour. Keeping the source, rather than deleting it, lets anyone holding the old id find the right record, and makes a wrong merge reversible.
- id: BR-H8
  text: Two records are merged only by an HIM specialist. Matching proposes duplicates; it never merges on its own.
  rationale: A wrong merge puts two people's care in one record, which is a worse failure than a duplicate. A person confirms every merge.
- id: BR-H9
  text: Reading a merged record returns it with status merged and replaced_by, following the chain to the record in use. A change to a merged record is rejected.
  rationale: FHIR notes the record pointed to may itself have been merged later. Writing to a retired record would put new data where nobody looks.
- id: BR-H10
  text: An unmerge restores the source record to active with the identifiers it had, and emits patient.unmerged. Data recorded on the target since the merge stays there, and every module is told to review it.
  rationale: A wrong merge mixes two people's care. Only the people who recorded care since the merge can tell whose it is, so nothing is moved back automatically.
- id: BR-H11
  text: A record can be marked entered in error only while no care has been recorded for it (last_care_at unset). Once care exists, the record is merged instead.
  rationale: FHIR marks a record created in error as not in use. If care was recorded, discarding the record would hide that care; merging keeps it with the right person.
- id: BR-H12
  text: Recording a death sets deceased, deceased_at and death_source, and emits patient.deceased. The record stays active. A death recorded in error can be retracted only by an HIM specialist, with a reason, which emits patient.death_retracted.
  rationale: FHIR notes a deceased patient's record may stay in use for some time, for results, billing and family. A wrong death record stops care and messages, so retracting it is a controlled correction.
- id: BR-H13
  text: 'A deceased patient''s record keeps the same protection as a living patient''s for as long as the law requires: 50 years after death in the United States.'
  rationale: HIPAA protects a deceased person's health information for 50 years after death (45 CFR 164.502(f)). GDPR does not apply to the dead, but member states may set rules (Recital 27), and medical confidentiality continues.
- id: BR-H14
  text: A representative is recorded with their kind (parent, guardian, other legal representative, or a proxy the patient chose), the scope of their authority, any exclusions, a period, and the evidence seen. They may act for the patient only within that scope and period, and not for an excluded service.
  rationale: HIPAA treats a personal representative as the patient (45 CFR 164.502(g)), and the EHDS lets patients authorise proxies for a limited period and purpose (Article 4(2)). Recording the scope makes every module's question "may this person act?" answerable the same way.
- id: BR-H15
  text: Staff can exclude services or episodes from a representative's authority where the law lets the patient act alone, or where treating someone as the representative could endanger the patient. The exclusion is not shown to that representative.
  rationale: Under HIPAA a parent is not the representative for care a minor could consent to themselves (164.502(g)(3)), and a provider may refuse to treat someone as the representative where there is abuse or danger (164.502(g)(5)). Which care qualifies is set by state or member state law, so staff record it.
- id: BR-H16
  text: A patient can ask to be contacted by other means or at another address, without giving a reason. Every module that contacts the patient asks this one where to.
  rationale: US providers must accommodate reasonable requests for confidential communications and may not ask why (45 CFR 164.522(b)). For some patients an ordinary letter home could be unsafe.
- id: BR-H17
  text: A patient can ask for their data to be corrected. The request is decided within correction_deadline, extendable once by correction_extension with a written reason. An accepted correction is made and linked to the request. A denied one gives the reason in writing, and the patient can add a statement of disagreement, which stays with the record. While the patient contests accuracy, they can ask for the data to be restricted, which sets accuracy_contested. The patient can name people who received their data and need the correction.
  rationale: HIPAA gives 60 days with one 30-day extension, and requires a written denial and a right to disagree (45 CFR 164.526). GDPR gives a right to rectification (Article 16) answered within one month, extendable by two (Article 12(3)), and to restriction while accuracy is checked (Article 18(1)(a)). The EHDS adds online requests (Article 6).
- id: BR-H18
  text: A request to erase a patient record is refused while record_retention applies, counted from last_care_at or the date of death, and for a patient who was a minor at their last care, not before they reach minor_retention_until_age. When it ends, and no legal hold applies, identifying fields are removed and the id is kept.
  rationale: 'HIPAA gives no right to erasure, and US hospitals must keep records for at least 5 years (42 CFR 482.24(b)(1)), longer in many states. Some states count minors'' records from a birthday: Texas lets hospitals dispose of them only after the patient''s 20th birthday or 10 years after last treatment, whichever is later (Health and Safety Code 241.103). GDPR Article 17(3)(c) lets health data be kept for care. Keeping the id keeps every other module''s references valid.'
- id: BR-H19
  text: Every view and every change of a patient record is logged with who, when, what, and the reason given. Patients can get a report of who accessed their record where the jurisdiction gives that right.
  rationale: HIPAA requires audit controls (45 CFR 164.312(b)). The EHDS gives EU patients information on every access to their health data, kept for at least three years (Article 9).
- id: BR-H20
  text: Opening a restricted or very restricted record needs a reason, coded as HL7 v3 ActReason (such as TREAT treatment, ETREAT emergency treatment or BTG break the glass), which is logged. Every opening of a very restricted record is reported to the privacy officer with patient.restricted_access.
  rationale: Staff and public figures' records are the ones colleagues are tempted to look at. A stated reason and a report deter snooping without stopping care.
- id: BR-H21
  text: Every change sends the revision it read, and a stale revision is rejected.
  rationale: Registrars and HIM specialists edit the same record at once, such as during a merge. Without this, one silently undoes the other.
- id: BR-H22
  text: Search and matching compare every name, address, phone number and identifier the patient has had, including old ones.
  rationale: A patient returning after years, or a referral in a former name, must still find the existing record (BR-H1).
- id: BR-H23
  text: Social security numbers are not used as patient identifiers. Where one is stored, such as for a payer that needs it, it and any national identifier are shown in full only to roles that need them, and otherwise masked.
  rationale: US Core says social security numbers SHOULD NOT be used as patient identifiers because of identity theft. GDPR lets member states set conditions for national identification numbers (Article 87), and HIPAA limits use to the minimum necessary (45 CFR 164.502(b)).
- id: BR-H24
  text: A record under legal hold is never erased or anonymised, even after record_retention ends. Other modules are told when a hold starts or ends.
  rationale: Texas forbids destroying records involved in unresolved litigation (Health and Safety Code 241.103(c)), and GDPR lets data be kept for legal claims (Article 17(3)(e)).
- id: BR-H25
  text: A parent's or guardian's authority over a minor ends when the patient reaches age_of_majority, or earlier when emancipation is recorded. patient.representative_changed is emitted when it ends.
  rationale: 'HIPAA treats a parent as the personal representative of an unemancipated minor, and an adult or emancipated minor as acting for themselves (45 CFR 164.502(g)(2) and (3)). The age differs by state: 19 in Nebraska (Revised Statutes 43-2101). A parent who keeps access to an adult''s record is a privacy breach.'
- id: BR-H26
  text: Every field is validated as listed in Validations before it is saved. A request with several invalid fields reports all of them at once.
  rationale: Bad data at registration is what creates duplicates later. Reporting every problem at once saves registrars a round trip per field.
- id: BR-H27
  text: At a new patient's first registration, identity is checked against a government photo ID where the patient has one, and the kind of evidence is recorded. A patient without ID, such as a child or someone who lost it, is still registered, with the evidence recorded as none available.
  rationale: The ONC SAFER Guide recommends a picture ID for new patients, with alternatives for minors and others without one. Refusing care for lack of ID is never acceptable.
- id: BR-H28
  text: Returning patients are asked to confirm their details at registration or check-in, and demographics_confirmed_at is recorded.
  rationale: The SAFER Guide recommends asking returning patients to verify their demographic data. Stale phone numbers and addresses make matching and contact fail.
- id: BR-H29
  text: When collect_photo is on, a colour photograph is taken at registration with the patient's agreement, and shown wherever the patient is identified. A patient can decline.
  rationale: The SAFER Guide strongly recommends collecting photographs at registration and showing them across the EHR, and an emergency department study found fewer wrong-patient orders when a photo was shown. GDPR treats a photo as biometric data only when processed by specific technical means to identify someone (Recital 51).
- id: BR-H30
  text: When another record in use has the same given and family name, both records carry similar_name_alert. Search results flag patients with the same or similar names.
  rationale: The SAFER Guide recommends warning users when a new record shares a name with another patient, or a search returns several patients with the same or similar names.
- id: BR-H31
  text: A merge is allowed while either record has an active stay, but it is marked during_care so the care team is told, and bands and labels are reprinted with the record in use.
  rationale: The SAFER Guide says that when temporary or duplicate ids are merged during care, the name must be updated in every system with safeguards against confusion.
- id: BR-H32
  text: Unknown values are left blank, partial or marked estimated. Placeholders such as UNKNOWN, a made-up birth date or a dummy phone number are never stored, and matching ignores blank and estimated values.
  rationale: Project US@ says unknown address parts should be left blank and matching should not match on UNKNOWN. A placeholder makes unrelated patients look alike.
- id: BR-H33
  text: Two records both marked as part of a multiple birth, with the same birth date, are proposed as possible duplicates only with a warning that they may be siblings. A pair marked not a duplicate is not proposed again unless one of the compared fields changes.
  rationale: The SAFER Guide recommends a multiple birth indicator to prevent merging twins' charts because their details are so similar.
- id: BR-H34
  text: Bulk imports go through matching like any registration. An imported record with a probable match is held for review, never merged automatically, and every record is tagged with its import_batch.
  rationale: Imports and health system mergers bring large numbers of duplicates at once. The SAFER Guide asks for such charts to be flagged and resolved on a time-bound plan.
- id: BR-H35
  text: An inactive record is still found by search and can be read. Any change to it, or care reported for it, makes it active again and emits patient.reactivated.
  rationale: A patient returning after years must find their old record, not get a new one (BR-H1).
- id: BR-H36
  text: A patient, or their representative, can change their own phone numbers, email, addresses, languages and contacts directly. A change to their name, birth date, administrative gender or identifiers goes through a correction request (BR-H17).
  rationale: The EHDS lets EU patients request rectification online (Article 6). Contact details change often and only the patient knows them. Names, birth dates and identifiers drive identification, so staff check them against evidence.
- id: BR-H37
  text: A patient, or their representative, can ask for a copy of the data this module holds about them. The request is answered within access_request_deadline. A copy of the whole medical record is the Release of information module's job.
  rationale: HIPAA gives a right of access answered within 30 days (45 CFR 164.524), and GDPR a right to a copy (Article 15) answered within one month (Article 12(3)).
- id: BR-H38
  text: A merge request states which value to keep for every field where the two records differ. A merge that leaves a difference unresolved is rejected. Names, addresses, phone numbers and identifiers not kept become old values on the target; any other value not kept is stored with the merge record.
  rationale: FHIR $merge lets the caller send the expected result patient, because only a person comparing the records can tell which birth date or address is right. Picking silently, such as newest wins, can keep a typo and drop the true value.
- id: BR-H39
  text: A very restricted record appears in search results only when the search includes one of its identifiers, or its full name and birth date. It is marked restricted, and shows nothing more until opened with a reason (BR-H20).
  rationale: A broad search such as a surname should not reveal that a staff member or public figure is a patient here. Someone who already has the exact details can still find the record for care.
- id: BR-H40
  text: A search returns at most search_result_limit records, and asks for more detail when there are more. Every search is logged with its criteria. A user whose searches in search_alert_window exceed search_alert_threshold is reported to the privacy officer.
  rationale: HIPAA requires mechanisms that record and examine activity in systems holding health information (45 CFR 164.312(b)). Without limits, one account can list every patient.
- id: BR-H41
  text: A name can be recorded in more than one script or language, such as Arabic and its Latin spelling, each tagged with its BCP 47 language and whether it is alphabetic, ideographic or syllabic. Search and matching compare every form.
  rationale: FHIR tags a name with its language and its representation (ISO 21090 alphabetic, ideographic or syllabic). Staff in different places type different spellings of the same name, and each one must still find the patient.
- id: BR-H42
  text: 'Sex is recorded apart from administrative gender, each value with its kind: assigned at birth, legal, or other recorded. It is recorded as the patient or their documents report it, never inferred from name or appearance. Sex for clinical use belongs to clinical modules.'
  rationale: FHIR separates administrative gender from recorded sex, legal sex and sex assigned at birth, and a patient can have different legal sex values in different jurisdictions. USCDI v3 names the element Sex (Assigned at Birth), and US certification rules for it changed in 2025, so the values come from the sex_codes setting.
- id: BR-H43
  text: When a correction is accepted, or data is erased or restricted at the patient's request, patient.corrected is emitted. The Release of information and Exchange modules pass the change to everyone the patient named and everyone they know received the data. The patient can ask who was told.
  rationale: HIPAA requires reasonable efforts to inform people who received the data and may rely on it (45 CFR 164.526(c)(3)). GDPR requires telling each recipient of a rectification, erasure or restriction, and telling the patient who they are on request (Article 19).
- id: BR-H44
  text: A denied correction, and any statement of disagreement, is given to the Release of information and Exchange modules to include, or summarise, with every later disclosure of that data.
  rationale: HIPAA requires the statement of disagreement, or an accurate summary, with any later disclosure of the information it relates to (45 CFR 164.526(d)(5)).
- id: BR-H45
  text: When a patient dies, their existing representatives' authority ends. After death, a person acts for the patient only as an estate representative (an executor, administrator or other person with authority over the estate), recorded with evidence.
  rationale: HIPAA treats the executor, administrator or other person with authority over the deceased person or their estate as the personal representative (45 CFR 164.502(g)(4)).
- id: BR-H46
  text: An address confidentiality program substitute address is recorded as the patient's address with its program and participant number, and is never used for matching. Whether the hospital must accept it is set by acp_acceptance. The real address is not required.
  rationale: States such as Washington and California give survivors of abuse a substitute address that state and local agencies must accept, while private businesses may choose to. Many participants share one substitute address, so matching on it would link strangers.
- id: BR-H47
  text: Fields the service sets, such as replaced_by, last_care_at, revision, similar_name_alert, import_batch, external_links, legal_hold, accuracy_contested and demographics_confirmed_at, cannot be written by clients. Each changes only through its own operation.
  rationale: A client writing replaced_by could hide a record behind another one, and a forged last_care_at would change retention.
- id: BR-H48
  text: A patient, or their representative within their scope, can read their own record electronically as soon as it is saved. No policy holds data back for review first. A patient can ask for their own access to be delayed (patient_requested_delay). access_request_deadline applies only to copies in another form.
  rationale: In the United States, patient demographics in the record are electronic health information (45 CFR 171.102), and ONC says a delay in making it available through a portal, for any period, is likely an interference under the information blocking rule (45 CFR 171.103), while following a patient's own request to delay is not.
- id: BR-H49
  text: In the European Union, from 26 March 2029, the Exchange module gives the patient's personal details to the national electronic health data access service whenever they change, so the patient can see them at once.
  rationale: The EHDS gives patients access to their patient summary, which includes personal details and contact information, immediately after it is registered, through the access services (Articles 3 and 4, Annex I), applying to patient summaries from 26 March 2029 (Article 105).
- id: BR-H50
  text: A record is not inactivated automatically while the patient has an appointment that has not started or an encounter that is open.
  rationale: inactive_after looks back at care. A patient booked for next month, or in hospital now, is in active use whatever their history.

workflows:
- name: Register a patient
  trigger: A person needs care and may not have a record
  actor: Registrar
  steps:
  - Search with name and birth date, plus any identifier, phone or address the patient gives
  - If a record matches, use it, and ask the patient to confirm their details
  - Otherwise check identity against a government photo ID where the patient has one, and record the kind of evidence
  - Register a new record, with a reason if the search found a probable match, after every field passes validation
  - Take a photograph if collect_photo is on and the patient agrees
  - Emit patient.registered
  - Propose duplicates for review if matching finds any after saving
  outcome: The patient has exactly one record, or a possible duplicate is waiting for review.
- name: Review a possible duplicate
  trigger: Matching proposes two records that may be the same person
  actor: Health information management (HIM) specialist
  steps:
  - Compare the two records side by side, including old names and identifiers
  - 'Decide: the same person, or not'
  - If the same, preview the merge, then merge the newer or less complete record into the other
  - If not, mark the pair not a duplicate so it is not proposed again
  outcome: The pair is resolved, and the decision is recorded.
- name: Merge two records
  trigger: An HIM specialist confirms a duplicate
  actor: Health information management (HIM) specialist
  steps:
  - Check the revisions and that both records are in use
  - Ask Admission, discharge and transfer whether either record has an active stay, and mark the merge during_care if so
  - For every field where the records differ, record the value to keep
  - Mark the source merged with replaced_by, and copy its identifiers to the target marked old
  - Record who merged, why and how the records differed
  - Emit patient.merged; each module moves its data to the target
  outcome: One record remains, and anyone holding the old id is pointed to it.
- name: Undo a wrong merge
  trigger: An HIM specialist finds a merge joined two different people
  actor: Health information management (HIM) specialist
  steps:
  - Restore the source to active with its identifiers, and remove them from the target
  - Emit patient.unmerged
  - Each module reviews data recorded on the target since the merge and moves what belongs to the source
  outcome: Two records again, with care recorded since the merge checked by the people who recorded it.
- name: Record a death
  trigger: A patient dies in hospital, or a death is reported
  actor: Registrar
  steps:
  - Record deceased_at as precisely as known, and death_source
  - End every existing representative's authority
  - Emit patient.deceased
  - Other modules stop reminders and review future plans
  outcome: Nobody is contacted or booked as if the patient were alive.
- name: Decide a correction request
  trigger: A patient asks for their data to be corrected
  actor: Health information management (HIM) specialist
  steps:
  - Record the request and its due date from correction_deadline
  - If the patient asks, set accuracy_contested while it is checked
  - 'Accept: make the change, link it to the request, tell the patient, and emit patient.corrected so recipients are told'
  - 'Deny: give the reason in writing, and record any statement of disagreement, which goes with later disclosures'
  - Clear accuracy_contested
  outcome: Every request is answered in time, and a disagreement stays with the record.
- name: End a representative at majority
  trigger: A minor patient reaches age_of_majority, or emancipation is recorded
  actor: System
  steps:
  - Find parent and guardian representatives of the patient
  - End each one's period today
  - Emit patient.representative_changed for each
  outcome: The new adult controls their own record.
- name: Import patients
  trigger: A legacy system or acquired hospital sends its patient records
  actor: Health information management (HIM) specialist
  steps:
  - Validate every record
  - Match each against existing records
  - Create records with no probable match, tagged with the import batch
  - Hold the rest for duplicate review
  - 'Report the batch: created, held and rejected'
  outcome: An import adds no unreviewed duplicates.
- name: End retention
  trigger: record_retention ends for a record
  actor: System
  steps:
  - Skip the record if it has a legal hold
  - Remove identifying fields and keep the id
  - Emit patient.updated
  outcome: Data is kept as long as the law requires and no longer.

constraints:
- id: CON-H1
  text: Patient data is encrypted in transit and at rest.
  rationale: GDPR Article 32 lists encryption as a security measure, and HIPAA treats it as an addressable safeguard (45 CFR 164.312).
- id: CON-H2
  text: The access log cannot be changed or deleted by the people it records.
  rationale: A log its subjects can edit cannot show who accessed what.
- id: CON-H3
  text: Language tags follow BCP 47, and dates follow ISO 8601, allowing year or year and month alone.
  rationale: FHIR uses both, and a partial birth date must not be padded into a false full date.
- id: CON-H4
  text: Coded fields use the code systems named in the data model. A deployment's local codes are mapped to them before data leaves this module.
  rationale: Exchange with other systems, reporting and certification all assume these code systems. A local code with no mapping is lost at the boundary.
- id: CON-H5
  text: Patients are exchanged as FHIR R5 Patient resources conforming to US Core Patient (9.0.0) in the United States and to the HL7 Europe Base and Core guide (2.0.1) in the European Union.
  rationale: US Core is what ONC certification tests, and US Core requires Project US@ addresses for certifying systems. HL7 Europe Base is the shared EU starting point.

apiConventions:
- topic: Authentication
  text: Every request carries the caller's identity from Identity. Permissions are checked against it (Permission matrix).
- topic: Errors
  text: Errors use problem details with the media type application/problem+json, with a code member carrying the error code, such as PATIENT_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER.
  reference: RFC 9457
- topic: Pagination
  text: 'Lists are cursor-based: a request sends limit and an optional cursor, and the response returns next_cursor until there are no more results.'
- topic: Concurrency
  text: Every change sends the revision it read. A stale revision fails with PATIENT_REVISION_CONFLICT (BR-H21).
- topic: Retries
  text: Every change accepts an Idempotency-Key header. A retry with the same key returns the original result (FR-H1).
  reference: IETF draft-ietf-httpapi-idempotency-key-header
- topic: Rate limits
  text: A caller over its limit gets 429 Too Many Requests with a Retry-After header.
  reference: RFC 6585, RFC 9110
- topic: Dates
  text: Dates are ISO 8601 and may be partial (year, or year and month). Instants are RFC 3339 timestamps in UTC (CON-H3).
  reference: RFC 3339
- topic: FHIR
  text: GET /patients/{id} returns an HL7 FHIR R5 Patient resource when the request asks for application/fhir+json (FR-H12).
- topic: Personal data in URLs
  text: Names, birth dates and identifiers are sent in request bodies or query parameters over TLS, never in paths, and are never written to request logs.
- topic: Versioning
  text: Additive changes don't change the version. A breaking change ships as a new major version, announced with Deprecation and Sunset headers.
  reference: RFC 9745, RFC 8594

deliveryGuarantees:
- id: DG-H1
  text: Events are published at least once. Consumers treat an event with a source and id they have already seen as a duplicate.
  rationale: A relay can publish twice if it crashes after publishing. CloudEvents defines source plus id as the duplicate check.
- id: DG-H2
  text: Events for one patient can arrive out of order. Each carries the revision, and consumers ignore one lower than a revision they have applied.
  rationale: Brokers and retries don't guarantee order. The revision orders every change to a record.
- id: DG-H3
  text: Events carry ids and the names of changed fields, never names, birth dates, addresses or identifiers. Consumers read the record for details.
  rationale: Events fan out to many modules and logs. Keeping demographics out of them follows the minimum necessary standard (45 CFR 164.502(b)) and GDPR data minimisation (Article 5(1)(c)).
- id: DG-H4

risks:
- id: RISK-H1
  risk: Duplicate records split one person's history.
  impact: Allergies, results and medicines on one record are missed on the other.
  mitigation: Search before registering (BR-H1), matching on old values (BR-H22), and a duplicate review queue (FR-H4).
- id: RISK-H2
  risk: Two different people are merged.
  impact: One person's care is given or recorded against another.
  mitigation: Only a person merges (BR-H8), with a preview, and merges can be undone (BR-H10). Track unmerges (PERF-H2).
- id: RISK-H3
  risk: A wrong death record.
  impact: Care, reminders and benefits stop for a living patient.
  mitigation: Death needs a source, and can be retracted with a reason (BR-H12).
- id: RISK-H4
  risk: Staff look up colleagues or public figures.
  impact: A privacy breach and loss of trust.
  mitigation: Restricted records need a reason and are reported (BR-H20), and every access is logged (BR-H19).
- id: RISK-H5
  risk: Someone acts for a patient without authority.
  impact: Health information reaches the wrong person, possibly an abuser.
  mitigation: Representatives have a scope, period and exclusions (BR-H14, BR-H15).
- id: RISK-H6
  risk: Demographics leak through events and logs.
  impact: Personal data spreads beyond the systems that need it.
  mitigation: Events carry ids only (DG-H3), and personal data is kept out of URLs.
- id: RISK-H7
  risk: A parent keeps access after the patient becomes an adult.
  impact: An adult's health information reaches someone without the right to it.
  mitigation: Authority ends at age_of_majority or emancipation (BR-H25).
- id: RISK-H8
  risk: A legacy import floods the record with duplicates.
  impact: Split histories for thousands of patients at once.
  mitigation: Imports go through matching and review (BR-H34).
- id: RISK-H9
  risk: Someone receives care under another person's identity.
  impact: False data in the real patient's record, and bills in their name.
  mitigation: Photo ID at first registration (BR-H27), confirmation on return (BR-H28) and photos (BR-H29).
decisions:
- id: ADR-H1
  decision: Patients start at the healthcare level, with no core page.
  rationale: 'Patient identity in healthcare carries rules no other industry needs: two-identifier checks, merges of clinical records, personal representatives and medical record retention.'
  alternatives: A neutral core Person module. Not needed until another industry has a person module to share it with.
  date: '2026-10-06'
- id: ADR-H2
  decision: Merging keeps the source record, marked merged with replaced_by, instead of deleting it.
  rationale: It matches FHIR $merge and Patient.link, lets anyone holding the old id find the right record, and makes a wrong merge reversible.
  alternatives: Delete the source. Rejected because old ids in other systems and documents would point nowhere.
  date: '2026-10-06'
- id: ADR-H3
  decision: Matching only proposes; a person merges.
  rationale: Matching algorithms and their thresholds differ by deployment, and a false merge costs more than a duplicate.
  alternatives: Merge automatically above a threshold. Rejected for now; a deployment that wants it can override BR-H8.
  date: '2026-10-06'
- id: ADR-H4
  decision: Optional demographics are switched on per deployment with collected_fields, with none collected by default.
  rationale: US federal standards removed sexual orientation and gender identity in 2025, while EU law treats racial or ethnic origin as special category data. One default would be wrong somewhere.
  date: '2026-10-06'
- id: ADR-H5
  decision: Whether a person may act for a patient is answered here, from the representatives recorded on the patient.
  rationale: Every module asks the same question. Answering it in one place keeps the answer consistent and the exclusions private.
  date: '2026-10-06'
- id: ADR-H6
  decision: The matching algorithm is not specified.
  rationale: 'Deterministic, probabilistic and referential matching all exist, and they change with the data. The requirement is the behaviour: grades, review and measurement (FR-H3, PERF-H1).'
  date: '2026-10-06'
- id: ADR-H7
  decision: 'Names are validated loosely: any letters, marks, spaces, hyphens, apostrophes and periods, with case kept as given.'
  rationale: The W3C warns that strict name rules reject real names, such as single names and names with particles. A rejected real name forces a registrar to invent one, which breaks matching.
  date: '2026-10-06'
- id: ADR-H8
  decision: Photographs and identity checks are deployment settings, on the recommended side, never conditions for care.
  rationale: The SAFER Guide recommends both, but a patient without ID or who declines a photo must still be treated.
  date: '2026-10-06'
- id: ADR-H9
  decision: Very restricted records are hidden from broad searches but found by exact details.
  rationale: Hiding them completely would block care for staff and public figures; showing them in every surname search would reveal they are patients.
  date: '2026-10-06'
- id: ADR-H10
  decision: Merge conflicts are resolved by a person field by field, never by a rule such as newest wins.
  rationale: The newest value is often the duplicate's typo. FHIR $merge leaves the result to the caller.
  date: '2026-10-06'
- id: ADR-H11
  decision: Patients announces corrections; Release of information and Exchange tell recipients.
  rationale: Only the modules that disclose data know who received it. This module knows what changed.
  date: '2026-10-06'

technicalNotes:
- id: TN-H1
  title: Match in two steps
  text: Find candidates cheaply first (blocking), for example records sharing a birth date or a phonetic code of the family name, then score each candidate on name, birth date, gender, address, phone and identifiers. Normalise addresses to Project US@ in the United States before comparing. Tune thresholds on the deployment's own data and map scores to certain, probable and possible.
- id: TN-H2
  title: Run large merges in the background
  text: FHIR $merge allows 202 Accepted with a task to track. Mark the source merged and publish patient.merged in the same transaction, then let each module move its own data. Until consumers finish, reads of their data may show either record.
- id: TN-H3
  title: Map to FHIR
  text: status active and inactive map to Patient.active true and false. merged maps to active false with link type replaced-by, and the target gets link type replaces. entered_in_error maps to active false with no link. identifiers marked old use Identifier.use old.
- id: TN-H4
  title: Validate phone numbers with libphonenumber
  text: Parse numbers with the caller's default region, reject ones that are not possible numbers, and store the E.164 form. Google's libphonenumber has ports for most languages.
- id: TN-H5
  title: Normalise before comparing names
  text: Store names exactly as given, but compare them after Unicode NFC normalisation and case folding, so composed and decomposed accents match. For cross-script matching, compare a transliteration, such as from ICU transforms, alongside the recorded Latin form.
- id: TN-H6
  title: Standardise US addresses before matching
  text: Apply Project US@ (uppercase, standard suffix and state abbreviations, no punctuation except the ZIP+4 hyphen) to a comparison copy, and keep the address as entered for display.

Every clinical module refers to a patient by id. This module keeps that id
pointing at one person, and keeps who that person is accurate over the years.

## One person, one record

A duplicate record splits a patient's history: an allergy on one record isn't
seen on the other. So registration starts with a search (BR-H1), and search
looks at old names and addresses as well as current ones (BR-H22). When a
duplicate still gets through, matching proposes it and an HIM specialist
decides (BR-H8).

## A merge can be undone

Merging keeps the retired record and points it at the one that remains
(BR-H7), so old ids still lead somewhere. If the merge joined two different
people, it can be undone, and every module is told to check what was recorded
since (BR-H10).

## Nothing about a person is overwritten

Names, addresses and identifiers change. The old values stay, marked old,
because referral letters, insurers and other hospitals still use them (BR-H5).

## Death is recorded, not assumed

A death needs a date and a source, and a wrong one can be retracted (BR-H12).
The record stays in use, and stays protected for as long as the law requires
(BR-H13).

## Who may act for the patient is answered in one place

Parents, guardians and proxies are recorded with what they may do and until
when (BR-H14). Where the law lets a patient act alone, such as a minor
consenting to their own care, staff exclude that care from the parent's
authority (BR-H15). Every module asks this one, so the answer is the same
everywhere.

## Validation is strict about format, loose about names

Phone numbers, emails, postal codes and identifiers follow their standards
exactly, because a wrong one fails silently later. Names don't: single names,
particles such as "van der", and unusual capitals are all real, and a rejected
real name forces a registrar to invent one (ADR-H7). Unknown values stay
blank rather than filled with placeholders, so they never make two patients
look alike (BR-H32).
