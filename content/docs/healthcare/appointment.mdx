---
title: Clinical appointments
description: 'What every healthcare application adds to appointments: patients and practitioners, check-in, protected health data and the record it belongs to.'
module: appointment
version: 0.1.0
status: draft
industry: healthcare
extends: core/appointment
purpose: 'Add what every healthcare application needs on top of the core appointment: patients and practitioners, check-in with identity verification, protected health data, communication patients can rely on, and the medical record an appointment becomes part of. Works in the United States and the European Union.'
scope:
- Patients, practitioners and personal representatives as participants
- Check-in with patient identity verification
- Reminders and messages that protect health information
- Access logging, record retention and the limits of erasure
- Language assistance and virtual appointments
- Exchange with other systems as HL7 FHIR Appointment
- Jurisdiction settings for the United States and the European Union
nonGoals:
- The clinical record of the visit itself. That belongs to the Encounter module.
- Insurance eligibility and billing, which differ by country and payer.
- Patient registration and identifiers. Identity owns them; this layer only uses them.
actors:
- name: Patient
  description: The person receiving care. A participant with the role patient.
- name: Practitioner
  description: A clinician providing care. A participant with the role practitioner.
- name: Personal representative
  description: A person allowed to act for the patient, such as the parent of a minor or a legal guardian.
- name: Front desk
  description: Staff who check patients in. A Scheduler with the check-in permission.
- name: Privacy officer
  description: Reviews who accessed appointment data.
assumptions:
- id: AS-H1
  text: Identity holds each patient's identifiers, such as name and date of birth, and whether they asked for confidential communications.
  rationale: Check-in verifies against these identifiers (BR-H2), and reminders follow the communication preference (BR-H4).
- id: AS-H2
  text: 'The deployment has a lawful basis to process health data for care: treatment under HIPAA in the United States, and GDPR Article 9(2)(h) in the European Union.'
  rationale: Without it, no appointment data could be held at all. The legal basis is the deployment's responsibility, not this module's.
- id: AS-H3
  text: The jurisdiction setting and record_retention are set with legal advice before go-live.
  rationale: Retention and erasure rules differ by US state and EU member state. Guessing them risks both unlawful deletion and unlawful retention.
glossary:
- term: Active appointment
  definition: 'An appointment that holds its slot: booked, checked in, or pending while pending_holds_slot is on.'
- term: Protected health information
  definition: Health information that identifies a person (HIPAA, 45 CFR 160.103). In the EU, data concerning health is a special category of personal data (GDPR Article 9).
- term: Personal representative
  definition: Someone the law treats as the patient for privacy purposes, such as a parent or guardian (45 CFR 164.502(g)).
- term: Check-in
  definition: Recording that the patient has arrived and their identity is verified.
- term: Entered in error
  definition: An appointment recorded by mistake. It is kept, but never counted as a real appointment.
concepts:
- name: Medical record
  description: The appointment and its history are part of the patient's record, which is kept for a legal minimum period.
dataModel:
- name: service_type
  type: code
  required: false
  description: The service to be performed, such as a cardiology consultation (FHIR Appointment.serviceType).
- name: reason
  type: code or text
  required: false
  description: Why the appointment was booked. Clinical, so it is health information (FHIR Appointment.reason).
- name: priority
  type: integer
  required: false
  description: From 1 (highest) to 9 (lowest), as in FHIR and iCalendar.
  constraints: 1 to 9
- name: patient_instruction
  type: text
  required: false
  description: What the patient needs to know or do, such as fasting from 8 pm the night before.
- name: virtual_service
  type: connection details
  required: false
  description: How to join a virtual appointment (FHIR Appointment.virtualService).
- name: interpreter_language
  type: BCP 47 tag
  required: false
  description: The language the patient needs an interpreter for.
- name: acting_representative
  type: string
  required: false
  description: Identity id of the personal representative who booked or changed the appointment, if one did.
- name: checked_in_at
  type: instant
  required: false
  constraints: set when status = checked_in
- name: identity_verified_with
  type: string[]
  required: false
  description: The identifier types used at check-in, such as name and date_of_birth.
  constraints: at least 2 when checked in
businessRules:
- id: BR-H1
  text: Every appointment has at least one participant with the role patient, and at least one with the role practitioner.
  rationale: A clinical appointment is care given to a patient by a practitioner. FHIR models both as participants of an Appointment.
- id: BR-H2
  text: Check-in verifies the patient's identity with at least two identifiers, such as name and date of birth. A room or location is never one of them.
  rationale: Wrong-patient errors happen at every stage of care. The Joint Commission requires two identifiers (NPSG.01.01.01).
- id: BR-H3
  text: 'Reminders and other messages to patients contain only what''s needed to attend: the patient''s name, the date, time and place, and the provider''s name and number. Never the reason, diagnosis or a service that reveals a condition.'
  rationale: 'Messages are seen by others: postcards, shared voicemail, lock screens. HHS guidance allows reminders but warns against disclosing treatment or health details.'
- id: BR-H4
  text: If a patient asked to be contacted by another means or at another address, every appointment message uses it.
  rationale: Under 45 CFR 164.522(b) providers must accommodate reasonable requests without asking why. Applying it everywhere protects patients for whom an ordinary reminder could be unsafe.
- id: BR-H5
  text: A personal representative can book and manage appointments for the patient they represent. Every action they take records them as acting_representative.
  rationale: HIPAA treats a personal representative as the patient (45 CFR 164.502(g)). Recording who acted keeps the record accurate.
- id: BR-H6
  text: When a patient needs an interpreter, the appointment records the language so a qualified interpreter can be arranged at no cost to the patient.
  rationale: US covered entities must offer qualified interpreters free of charge (45 CFR 92.201). Recording the need at booking is what makes that possible on the day.
- id: BR-H7
  text: Every view and every change of an appointment is logged with who, when and what.
  rationale: HIPAA requires audit controls that record activity in systems holding electronic health information (45 CFR 164.312(b)), and GDPR Article 32 requires security appropriate to the risk. Core history covers changes, not views.
- id: BR-H8
  text: An appointment recorded by mistake is marked entered in error. It is kept, hidden from schedules, and excluded from statistics.
  rationale: Records are never deleted in a medical record. FHIR has the same entered-in-error status.
- id: BR-H9
  text: Connection details for a virtual appointment are shared only with its participants.
  rationale: The link gives access to a session where health information is discussed.
- id: BR-H10
  text: Check-in is allowed from check_in_window before the start until the end.
  rationale: Checking in much earlier would mark someone as present who has not arrived for this appointment.
- id: BR-11
  text: A request to erase a patient's appointment data is refused while record_retention applies, because the appointment and its history are part of the medical record. When retention ends, personal fields are erased as in core.
  rationale: HIPAA gives no right to erasure, and US hospitals must keep records for at least 5 years (42 CFR 482.24(b)(1)), longer in many states. In the EU, GDPR Article 17(3)(c) lets health data be kept for care under Article 9(2)(h). Erasing on request would break the record.
validations:
- field: participants
  rule: at least one participant with the role patient and one with the role practitioner
  error: APPOINTMENT_PATIENT_AND_PRACTITIONER_REQUIRED
- field: identity_verified_with
  rule: at least two identifier types at check-in, none of them a room or location
  error: APPOINTMENT_IDENTITY_NOT_VERIFIED
- field: priority
  rule: an integer from 1 to 9
  error: APPOINTMENT_INVALID_PRIORITY
stateMachine:
  initial: booked
  states:
  - pending
  - booked
  - checked_in
  - cancelled
  - completed
  - no_show
  - entered_in_error
  transitions:
  - from: pending
    to: booked
    trigger: confirm
    guard: confirm_by has not passed, revision matches, and the slot is still free when pending_holds_slot is off
  - from: pending
    to: pending
    trigger: reschedule or update
    guard: before the end, revision matches
  - from: pending
    to: cancelled
    trigger: cancel
    guard: cancelled_reason provided, revision matches
  - from: pending
    to: cancelled
    trigger: expire
    guard: confirm_by has passed
  - from: booked
    to: booked
    trigger: reschedule or update
    guard: before the end, new slot is free and in the future, revision matches
  - from: booked
    to: checked_in
    trigger: check_in
    guard: within check_in_window before the start and before the end, identity verified with two identifiers
  - from: booked
    to: cancelled
    trigger: cancel
    guard: before the end, cancelled_reason provided, revision matches
  - from: booked
    to: completed
    trigger: complete
    guard: start_at has passed
  - from: booked
    to: no_show
    trigger: record_no_show
    guard: start_at has passed, at least one absent participant named
  - from: checked_in
    to: completed
    trigger: complete
    guard: the patient was seen
  - from: checked_in
    to: cancelled
    trigger: cancel
    guard: the patient left without being seen, with that as the reason
  - from: pending
    to: entered_in_error
    trigger: mark_entered_in_error
    guard: actor is a Scheduler
  - from: booked
    to: entered_in_error
    trigger: mark_entered_in_error
    guard: actor is a Scheduler
  - from: no_show
    to: completed
    trigger: correct_attendance
    guard: actor is a Scheduler
workflows:
- name: Check in
  trigger: The patient arrives
  actor: Front desk
  steps:
  - Find the appointment
  - Verify the patient with two identifiers, such as name and date of birth
  - Record checked_in_at and identity_verified_with, set status to checked_in and increase revision
  - Emit appointment.checked_in
  outcome: The patient is recorded as present and verified, ready to be seen.
- name: Mark entered in error
  trigger: An appointment was created by mistake, for example for the wrong patient
  actor: Scheduler
  steps:
  - Set status to entered_in_error with a reason, and increase revision
  - Withdraw pending reminders and release the slot
  - Emit appointment.entered_in_error
  outcome: The mistake is kept for the record but no longer affects schedules or statistics.
edgeCases:
- id: EC-H1
  situation: Two patients with the same name arrive for appointments at the same time.
  behaviour: Check-in needs a second identifier, such as date of birth, so the wrong appointment is not checked in.
  covers:
  - BR-H2
  - AC-H2
- id: EC-H2
  situation: A reminder is sent to a phone shared by the patient's family.
  behaviour: It says only the name, date, time, place and provider number, never the reason or service.
  covers:
  - BR-H3
  - AC-H3
- id: EC-H3
  situation: A patient asked to be contacted only at a work address.
  behaviour: All appointment messages go to the work address.
  covers:
  - BR-H4
  - AC-H4
- id: EC-H4
  situation: A patient asks for their appointment history to be erased two years after a visit to a US hospital.
  behaviour: Refused with APPOINTMENT_RETENTION_REQUIRED, because record_retention has not ended.
  covers:
  - BR-11
  - AC-H5
- id: EC-H5
  situation: An appointment was booked for the wrong patient.
  behaviour: It is marked entered in error, not deleted, and disappears from schedules and statistics.
  covers:
  - BR-H8
  - AC-H6
- id: EC-H6
  situation: A checked-in patient leaves before being seen.
  behaviour: The appointment is cancelled with the reason "left without being seen".
  covers:
  - FR-H1
- id: EC-H7
  situation: A parent books an appointment for their child.
  behaviour: The appointment records the parent as acting_representative.
  covers:
  - BR-H5
  - AC-H7

constraints:
- id: CON-H1
  text: Appointment data is encrypted in transit and at rest.
  rationale: GDPR Article 32 lists encryption as a security measure, and appointment data is health data.
- id: CON-H2
  text: The access log cannot be changed or deleted by the people it records.
  rationale: A log its subjects can edit cannot show who accessed what.

risks:
- id: RISK-H1
  risk: A reminder reveals a sensitive condition to someone else.
  impact: A privacy breach that can harm the patient.
  mitigation: Reminders carry no clinical detail (BR-H3) and follow confidential preferences (BR-H4).
- id: RISK-H2
  risk: The wrong patient is checked in.
  impact: Care, results and billing attached to the wrong person.
  mitigation: Two identifiers at check-in (BR-H2).
decisions:
- id: ADR-H1
  decision: Check-in is a status between booked and completed.
  rationale: FHIR has checked-in and arrived statuses, and front desks work from the list of who is here.
  alternatives: Record arrival only on the encounter. Rejected because scheduling needs to know who has arrived before an encounter starts.
  date: '2026-10-05'
- id: ADR-H2
  decision: Retention overrides erasure (BR-11) while the medical record must be kept.
  rationale: Both US and EU law protect the medical record from erasure on request.
  date: '2026-10-05'
- id: ADR-H3
  decision: One jurisdiction setting, with explicit settings for anything that varies inside a jurisdiction.
  rationale: Retention differs by US state and EU member state, so the jurisdiction alone cannot decide it.
  date: '2026-10-05'

A clinical appointment is still an appointment: everything in the
[core specification](/docs/core/appointment) applies. This layer adds what
changes when the participants are a patient and a practitioner and the data is
health information.

## The patient is verified at the desk, not assumed

Check-in uses two identifiers, such as name and date of birth, never a room
number (BR-H2). Two patients with the same name in the same waiting room is an
ordinary day in a hospital, and checking in the wrong one attaches care and
results to the wrong person.

## A reminder says when, never why

A reminder lands on a shared phone, a postcard, a lock screen. So it carries
only what the patient needs to turn up: name, date, time, place and a number to
call (BR-H3). If a patient asked to be contacted somewhere else, every message
goes there (BR-H4).

## The appointment is part of the medical record

Appointments are not erased on request while the record must be kept (BR-11).
US hospitals keep records for at least 5 years, and GDPR lets health data be
kept for care. Mistakes are marked entered in error, never deleted (BR-H8), and
every view and change is logged (BR-H7).

## The United States and Europe, from one setting

The `jurisdiction` setting chooses the legal regime, and `record_retention` is
set per state or member state, with legal advice (AS-H3).
