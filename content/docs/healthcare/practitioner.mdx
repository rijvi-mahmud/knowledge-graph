---
title: Practitioners
description: 'The people who provide care: their identifiers, licences and other qualifications, how they are verified and credentialed, and whether they may practise where and when care happens.'
module: practitioner
version: 0.3.4
status: draft
industry: healthcare
purpose: 'Keep one trustworthy record of each practitioner: who they are, the identifiers and qualifications they hold, and whether each has been verified at its source. Decide, through a person, whether they are credentialed; watch what expires and who is excluded; and answer every other module whether a practitioner may practise, where and on what date. Works in the United States and the European Union.'
scope:
- 'Practitioner records: names, identifiers such as the NPI, contact details, languages, specialties and qualifications'
- Applications, primary-source verification and credentialing decisions, and recredentialing
- Expiring licences, registrations and certifications
- Exclusion and sanction screening in the United States, and national register checks in the EU
- Whether a practitioner is licensed to practise in a place on a date, and DEA registration by state
- Suspension, reinstatement and leaving
- Trainees who need supervision
- Practitioners' access to and correction of their own data
nonGoals:
- Departments and which practitioners work in which department. Organization owns roles.
- Medical staff appointment and clinical privileges, which hospitals grant on top of these rules.
- User accounts, sign-in and permissions. Identity owns them and links an account to a practitioner.
- Payer enrollment, such as Medicare enrollment, and billing numbers. Billing owns them.
- Employment, payroll and rostering. Human resources and scheduling systems own them.
- Issuing licences or deciding on professional conduct. Licensing boards and competent authorities do.
actors:
- name: Credentialing specialist
  description: Collects applications, verifies qualifications at their source, and prepares files for decision.
- name: Credentials committee
  description: The people the organization authorizes to decide credentialing, such as a committee or medical director.
- name: Practitioner
  description: A person who provides care, applying, keeping their details current, and seeing their own file.
- name: Privacy officer
  description: Reviews access to credential files.
- name: System
  description: Other modules asking whether a practitioner may practise, and screening sources.
assumptions:
- id: AS-H1
  text: Licensing boards, the NPPES registry, DEA, certification boards and exclusion lists can be checked online or through a verification service.
  rationale: Primary-source verification means checking with the issuer or an approved source, not a copy the applicant provides.
- id: AS-H2
  text: Organization owns which practitioners hold which roles in which departments, and asks this module whether a practitioner may practise.
  rationale: A role is about the organization; a licence is about the person.
- id: AS-H3
  text: Identity links each user account to at most one practitioner record.
  rationale: Signatures and access decisions in other modules depend on knowing which practitioner is acting.
glossary:
- term: Practitioner
  definition: A person with a formal responsibility in providing care, such as a physician, nurse or therapist (FHIR Practitioner).
- term: Qualification
  definition: A licence, registration, certification or degree, with its issuer and period (FHIR Practitioner.qualification).
- term: Primary-source verification
  definition: Confirming a qualification with the body that issued it, or an approved source, rather than from a copy.
- term: Credentialing
  definition: Collecting and verifying a practitioner's qualifications and deciding whether they meet the organization's requirements.
- term: Recredentialing
  definition: Repeating credentialing at a set interval.
- term: Exclusion
  definition: In the United States, a ban on payment from federal health care programs for anything an excluded person furnishes, orders or prescribes (42 CFR 1001.1901).
- term: Expirable
  definition: A qualification with an end date, such as a licence or DEA registration.
concepts:
- name: Verified, not declared
  description: An applicant's copy of a licence is a lead, not proof. Each qualification counts only once verified at its source, and only for as long as its verification is current.
- name: The person, not the job
  description: This module holds what belongs to the person wherever they work. Organization holds the roles they hold in each department.

businessRules:
- id: BR-H1
  text: One practitioner has one record. Before a record is created, existing records are searched by identifier, such as the NPI or a registration number, and by name with birth date; a probable match is picked or a new record is created with a reason. Duplicates found later are merged by a credentialing specialist, keeping every identifier and qualification.
  rationale: Two records for one person split their qualifications and screening history, so an exclusion found on one is missed on the other.
- id: BR-H2
  text: 'In the United States, a practitioner who is a covered health care provider has an NPI: ten digits whose check digit passes the Luhn check with the prefix 80840, and which NPPES shows as active.'
  rationale: HIPAA requires covered providers to obtain and use an NPI (45 CFR 162.410). The check digit is computed as if the 80840 prefix were present, and NPPES says whether a number is assigned and active.
  jurisdiction: us
- id: BR-H3
  text: A qualification counts only when verified at the primary source primary_sources names for its type, such as the licensing board, NPPES, DEA or the certification board, with the source and verified_at recorded. A copy provided by the applicant is never treated as verification.
  rationale: Accreditors require primary-source verification because copies can be altered and licences can be restricted after they are issued.
- id: BR-H4
  text: A credentialing review is decided by the credentials committee, never by the system. It can be approved only when every qualification the practitioner's type requires is verified, and each verification is no older than verification_window. A denial gives a reason, and the practitioner is told.
  rationale: Credentialing is a judgment the organization is accountable for. A stale verification may miss a recent restriction.
- id: BR-H5
  text: Approving the initial review makes the practitioner active and sets credentialed_until from recredential_interval. A recredentialing review opens recredential_lead_time before that date; if it isn't approved by then, the practitioner is suspended with reason credentialing_lapsed.
  rationale: Credentials must be renewed at a fixed interval; letting them lapse silently means care by someone nobody has rechecked.
- id: BR-H6
  text: Qualifications with an end date are listed expiry_warning before they end, and practitioner.credential_expiring is emitted. When one ends unrenewed, practitioner.credential_expired is emitted; if it was the practitioner's only valid licence, they are suspended with reason licence_expired.
  rationale: An expired licence means the person may no longer practise; catching it before the date keeps care going without breaking the law.
- id: BR-H7
  text: In the United States, every practitioner is screened against each list in screening_sources, including the OIG LEIE and SAM, when they apply and every screening_interval after. A possible match is resolved by a person using identifiers such as the NPI and birth date, never automatically. A confirmed match suspends the practitioner with reason excluded, and Billing is told.
  rationale: Federal health care programs pay nothing for items or services furnished, ordered or prescribed by an excluded individual (42 CFR 1001.1901(b)). OIG recommends screening at hire and monthly, because the LEIE is updated monthly; name matches alone are often false.
  jurisdiction: us
- id: BR-H8
  text: In the European Union, a practitioner's registration with the national competent authority is verified when they apply and every screening_interval after, and a qualification obtained in another member state counts only once that authority has recognised it.
  rationale: Member states regulate health professions through competent authorities and recognise qualifications from other member states under Directive 2005/36/EC; a restriction is recorded by the authority, not the employer.
  jurisdiction: eu
- id: BR-H9
  text: A practitioner is licensed in a jurisdiction on a date when an active, verified licence or legal permission for that jurisdiction covers the date, such as a state licence, a licence issued through an interstate compact, or a national registration. Other modules ask this before a virtual visit with a patient in another place.
  rationale: In the United States a practitioner must be licensed or permitted where the patient is located, as the Clinical appointments rules state; licences are issued per state.
- id: BR-H10
  text: In the United States, a practitioner may prescribe controlled substances in a state only with a DEA registration for that state, active on the date.
  rationale: DEA requires a separate registration for each principal place of practice where controlled substances are dispensed (21 CFR 1301.12).
  jurisdiction: us
- id: BR-H11
  text: A suspended practitioner can't be booked for new appointments, start encounters, or sign or cosign notes, and practitioner.suspended tells the modules that check. Records they made before stay as they are.
  rationale: Care by someone without a valid licence or while excluded is unlawful and unpaid; the earlier record of care stays accurate.
- id: BR-H12
  text: A suspended practitioner is reinstated only when the reason is resolved, such as a renewed licence verified at its source or an exclusion lifted, with a reason, and practitioner.reinstated is emitted.
  rationale: Reinstatement on the practitioner's word would undo the protection the suspension gave.
- id: BR-H13
  text: A practitioner who leaves is made inactive with the date. Their record, qualifications, reviews and screening history are kept for credential_retention.
  rationale: Records they made remain, and auditors and courts may need to know what was verified when they practised.
- id: BR-H14
  text: A trainee, such as a resident or student, has requires_supervision set; other modules use it to require a cosignature or a supervising practitioner.
  rationale: Trainees practise under supervision as training programmes and medical staff bylaws require.
- id: BR-H15
  text: A practitioner can see their own record, reviews and screening results, update their contact details and languages, and upload documents. Changes to names, identifiers or qualifications are made by a credentialing specialist after verification.
  rationale: Practitioners have a right of access and rectification over their personal data (GDPR Articles 15 and 16), but qualifications must be verified, not self-declared.
- id: BR-H16
  text: Every view and change of a credential file is logged with who, when and why, and credential files are shown only to credentialing staff, the credentials committee and the practitioner.
  rationale: Credential files hold malpractice history, sanctions and sometimes health information about the practitioner; peer review is sensitive.
- id: BR-H17
  text: In the European Union, criminal record checks on practitioners are made only where member-state law authorizes them, and their results are kept only as that law allows.
  rationale: Personal data about criminal convictions can be processed only under official authority or when authorized by Union or member-state law (GDPR Article 10).
  jurisdiction: eu
- id: BR-H18
  text: When a primary source can't be reached, the verification stays pending; it is never marked verified from cached or applicant data. Answers to other modules use the last verified state.
  rationale: A verification nobody completed is not a verification, but care must not stop because a board's website is down.
- id: BR-H19
  text: Every change sends the revision it read; a stale revision fails.
  rationale: Two specialists updating one file must not overwrite each other.
- id: BR-H20
  text: Names and identifiers keep their history with periods, so a search by a former name or number still finds the practitioner.
  rationale: Licences and screening lists may show a name or number the practitioner no longer uses.
- id: BR-H21
  text: A qualification, privilege or registration with an end date is valid through the whole of that date, and "active on a date" is decided on the local calendar date where the care is given, never a date converted to UTC.
  rationale: Licences and registrations are issued with end dates that include the last day, as FHIR periods do; a UTC date would end an evening shift's licence early or late depending on the time zone.
- id: BR-H22
  text: A request to erase a practitioner's data is refused while credential_retention applies after they leave, or while a legal hold or an open review needs it. When it ends, identifying fields are removed and the id is kept, so records that name the practitioner stay linked.
  rationale: Credential files show the organization checked who it let practise, which law and accreditation require and which may be needed to defend a malpractice claim; GDPR Article 17(3)(b) and (e) exempt that processing from erasure.

workflows:
- name: Credential a new practitioner
  trigger: A practitioner applies
  actor: Credentialing specialist
  steps:
  - Search for an existing record, then create one as applicant
  - Record each qualification from the application
  - Verify each at its primary source, recording the source and date
  - Screen against each list in screening_sources
  - Open an initial review and send it to the credentials committee
  - The committee approves or denies with a reason
  outcome: An approved practitioner is active and can be given roles in Organization.
- name: Watch expiring credentials
  trigger: A qualification nears its end date
  actor: System
  steps:
  - List it expiry_warning ahead and emit practitioner.credential_expiring
  - The specialist verifies the renewal at its source
  - If it ends unrenewed, emit practitioner.credential_expired and suspend when it was the only valid licence
  outcome: No one practises on an expired licence.
- name: Screen monthly
  trigger: screening_interval passes
  actor: System
  steps:
  - Check each active practitioner against each source
  - Record each result
  - Send possible matches to a specialist, who confirms or rejects them using identifiers
  - Suspend on a confirmed exclusion and tell Billing
  outcome: Exclusions are found within a month of being listed.
- name: Recredential
  trigger: recredential_lead_time before credentialed_until
  actor: Credentialing specialist
  steps:
  - Open a recredentialing review
  - Re-verify the required qualifications and screen again
  - The committee decides; approval moves credentialed_until on
  outcome: Credentials never lapse unnoticed.

apiConventions:
- topic: Authentication
  text: Every request carries the caller's identity from Identity. Permissions are checked against it (Permission matrix).
- topic: Errors
  text: Errors use problem details (application/problem+json) with a code member, such as PRACTITIONER_NOT_VERIFIED.
  reference: RFC 9457
- topic: Pagination
  text: 'Lists are cursor-based: limit and an optional cursor in, next_cursor out.'
- topic: Concurrency
  text: Every change sends the revision it read. A stale revision fails with PRACTITIONER_REVISION_CONFLICT (BR-H19).
- topic: Retries
  text: Every create accepts an Idempotency-Key header; a retry returns the original result.
  reference: IETF draft-ietf-httpapi-idempotency-key-header
- topic: FHIR
  text: Practitioners are returned as HL7 FHIR R5 Practitioner, with the US Core Practitioner R4.0.1 API in the United States, when the request asks for application/fhir+json (FR-H9).
- topic: Dates
  text: Dates are ISO 8601 calendar dates, read as the local date where care is given, never a date converted to UTC.
  reference: ISO 8601
- topic: Times
  text: Instants are RFC 3339 timestamps with an offset, stored in UTC; time zones are IANA names.
  reference: RFC 3339
- topic: Personal data in URLs
  text: Names, birth dates and identifiers are sent in request bodies or query parameters over TLS, never in paths, and are never written to request logs.
- topic: Rate limits
  text: A caller over its limit gets 429 Too Many Requests with a Retry-After header.
  reference: RFC 6585, RFC 9110
- topic: Versioning
  text: Additive changes don't change the version. A breaking change ships as a new major version with Deprecation and Sunset headers.
  reference: RFC 9745, RFC 8594

deliveryGuarantees:
- id: DG-H1
  text: Events are published at least once; consumers drop one with a source and id they have seen.
  rationale: A relay can publish twice after a crash.
- id: DG-H2
  text: Events about one practitioner can arrive out of order; each carries the revision, and consumers ignore a lower one.
  rationale: Brokers and retries don't guarantee order.
- id: DG-H3
  text: Events carry ids and reasons, never verification details, screening entries or review notes.
  rationale: Credential files are sensitive; consumers need only whether the practitioner may practise.

Every module that books, treats or signs asks one question about a
practitioner: may this person practise here, now? This module answers it, from
a record of who they are and what they hold, each item verified at its
source.

## A copy is a lead, not proof

An applicant's licence copy says where to look. The licensing board's record
is what counts, and it can show a restriction the copy doesn't (BR-H3). A
review can't be approved on anything less, or on a verification gone stale
(BR-H4).

## Licences expire on a date, not when someone notices

Every licence and registration with an end date is listed months ahead, and a
practitioner whose only licence lapses is suspended that day, so they can't be
booked or sign until the renewal is verified (BR-H6, BR-H11).

## In the United States, exclusion is checked every month

Federal programs pay nothing for care an excluded person gives. Practitioners
are screened when they apply and monthly after, and a person, not a name
match, decides whether a hit is really them (BR-H7).

## The person, not the job

A licence follows the practitioner everywhere. Which department they work in,
and in what role, belongs to Organization (ADR-H2).
