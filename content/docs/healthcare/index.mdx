Healthcare applications share obligations that general software doesn't have,
above all around the data they hold. Rules every healthcare domain needs are
written once at this level, and each domain below inherits them.

## Health data is protected almost everywhere, under different laws

In the United States, the HIPAA Privacy Rule protects health information that
identifies a person, which 45 CFR 160.103 calls protected health information.
It applies to health plans, healthcare clearinghouses and providers that
transmit health information electronically. In the European Union, GDPR
Article 9 makes data concerning health a special category of personal data,
which can be processed only under one of the exceptions in Article 9(2).

The laws differ in scope, so a healthcare spec states the protection every
jurisdiction needs, and models the parts that differ by jurisdiction as
settings, never as one country's rule.

## Modules

| Module | What this industry adds |
|---|---|
| [Patient records](/docs/healthcare/patient) | One record per patient: registration, identifiers, duplicates and merges, death, representatives and privacy rights |
| [Clinical appointments](/docs/healthcare/appointment) | Patients and practitioners, check-in, reminders that protect health data, and the medical record |
| [Clinical encounters](/docs/healthcare/encounter) | Each visit or interaction: lifecycle, participants, locations, diagnoses, record completion and locking, and patients' access |
| [Clinical notes and observations](/docs/healthcare/clinical-documentation) | Notes, signing and cosigning, corrections, release to patients, vital signs and assessments |
| [Practitioners](/docs/healthcare/practitioner) | Who may practise: identifiers, licences, primary-source verification, credentialing, exclusion screening and licensure by place and date |
| [Terminology](/docs/healthcare/terminology) | Code systems such as ICD-10-CM, ICD-11, SNOMED CT, LOINC and UCUM: releases by date, value sets, maps and licences |
