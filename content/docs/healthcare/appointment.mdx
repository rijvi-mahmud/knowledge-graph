---
title: Clinical appointments
description: 'What every healthcare application adds to appointments: patients and practitioners, check-in, protected health data and the record it belongs to.'
module: appointment
version: 0.13.2
status: draft
industry: healthcare
extends: core/appointment
purpose: 'Add what every healthcare application needs on top of the core Appointment specification: patients and practitioners, check-in with identity verification, protected health data, communication patients can rely on, and the medical record an appointment becomes part of. Works in the United States and the European Union.'
scope:
- Patients, practitioners and personal representatives as participants
- Check-in with patient identity verification
- Reminders and messages that protect health information
- Access logging, record retention and the limits of erasure
- Language assistance and virtual appointments
- Exchange with other systems as HL7 FHIR Appointment
- Jurisdiction settings for the United States and the European Union
- Disability accommodations
- A patient's right to a copy of their appointment data
- Automated reminder texts and calls in the United States
- Where a virtual appointment may be held
nonGoals:
- The clinical record of the visit itself. That belongs to the Encounter module.
- Insurance eligibility and billing, which differ by country and payer.
- Patient registration, identifiers, contact details and representatives. The Patients module owns them; this layer only asks for them.
- Charging fees for missed or late-cancelled appointments. Billing decides, using appointment.no_show and the late cancellation flag on appointment.cancelled.
- Detecting and reporting data breaches, which belongs to the Security module.
- Maintaining code sets. This layer only stores codes and checks them against the configured code system.
actors:
- name: Patient
  description: The person receiving care. A participant with the role patient.
- name: Practitioner
  description: A clinician providing care. A participant with the role practitioner.
- name: Personal representative
  description: A person allowed to act for the patient, such as the parent of a minor or a legal guardian.
- name: Front desk
  description: Staff who check patients in. A Scheduler with the check-in permission.
- name: Privacy officer
  description: Reviews who accessed appointment data.
- name: Clinic manager
  description: Sets a department's scheduling rules, such as overbooking and referral requirements.
assumptions:
- id: AS-H1
  text: The Patients module holds each patient's identifiers, such as name and date of birth, where to contact them, and who may act for them.
  rationale: Check-in verifies against these identifiers (BR-H2), reminders follow the communication preference (BR-H4), and representatives act within what Patients allows (BR-H5).
- id: AS-H2
  text: 'The deployment has a lawful basis to process health data for care: treatment under HIPAA in the United States, and GDPR Article 9(2)(h) in the European Union.'
  rationale: Without it, no appointment data could be held at all. The legal basis is the deployment's responsibility, not this module's.
- id: AS-H3
  text: The jurisdiction setting is set with legal advice before go-live. Record retention is the Patients module's setting.
  rationale: Retention and erasure rules differ by US state and EU member state. Guessing them risks both unlawful deletion and unlawful retention.
- id: AS-H4
  text: The Encounter module opens an encounter when it receives appointment.checked_in, and reports its id back.
  rationale: FHIR links an encounter to the appointment that scheduled it (Encounter.appointment). The encounter is the clinical record of the visit.
- id: AS-H5
  text: A Referral service can confirm that a referral is valid for a department.
  rationale: Some departments only accept referred patients (BR-H28).
- id: AS-H6
  text: Departments, rooms and equipment are owned by the Organization module. This module only refers to them by id.
  rationale: Opening a clinic or renaming a room is not a scheduling decision.
- id: AS-H7
  text: The Organization module knows which practitioners hold a role in a department on a given date, and which rooms and equipment belong to it.
  rationale: FHIR models a practitioner's roles, locations and services at an organization for a period of time as PractitionerRole. Staffing a department is not a scheduling decision.
- id: AS-H8
  text: Where payers require prior authorization, an Authorization module knows whether a service needs it for this patient and reports its status.
  rationale: Which services need authorization depends on the payer and the patient's coverage, and changes often. Only the module that talks to payers can know it.
glossary:
- term: Active appointment
  definition: 'An appointment that holds its slot: booked, checked in, or pending while pending_holds_slot is on.'
- term: Protected health information
  definition: Health information that identifies a person (HIPAA, 45 CFR 160.103). In the EU, data concerning health is a special category of personal data (GDPR Article 9).
- term: Personal representative
  definition: 'Someone the law lets act for the patient, such as a parent or guardian: in the United States, a personal representative under HIPAA (45 CFR 164.502(g)); in the European Union, a legal representative under member state law.'
- term: Check-in
  definition: Recording that the patient has arrived and their identity is verified.
- term: Entered in error
  definition: An appointment recorded by mistake. It is kept, but never counted as a real appointment.
- term: Outpatient
  definition: A patient who receives care without being formally admitted, even if they stay overnight; in the United States, as Medicare defines it, and in the European Union, as the member state defines it. HL7 class AMB.
- term: Virtual appointment
  definition: Care where the patient and practitioner are not in the same place, such as a video visit. HL7 class VR.
- term: Prior authorization
  definition: A payer's approval, required by some plans before a service is given, for the payer to cover it.
concepts:
- name: Medical record
  description: The appointment and its history are part of the patient's record, which is kept for a legal minimum period.

businessRules:
- id: BR-H1
  text: Every appointment has at least one participant with the role patient, and at least one with the role practitioner.
  rationale: A clinical appointment is care given to a patient by a practitioner. FHIR models both as participants of an Appointment.
- id: BR-H2
  text: At check-in, the patient's identity is verified with at least two identifiers, such as name and date of birth. A room or location is never one of them.
  rationale: The Joint Commission requires at least two identifiers when providing care, treatment and services, and never the room number (NPG.01.01.01 for hospitals from 2026, NPSG.01.01.01 for its other programs). WHO Patient Safety Solution 2 recommends the same before care is given. Verifying at check-in matches the visit, and everything later recorded on it, to the right person.
- id: BR-H3
  text: 'Reminders and other messages to patients contain only what''s needed to attend: the patient''s name, the date, time and place, and the provider''s name and number. Never the reason, diagnosis or a service that reveals a condition.'
  rationale: HHS guidance permits reminders but says providers "should take care to limit the amount of information disclosed", for example only "name and number and other information necessary to confirm an appointment" (FAQ 198). GDPR requires data to be "limited to what is necessary" (Article 5(1)(c)). Messages reach shared phones, postcards and lock screens.
- id: BR-H4
  text: If a patient asked to be contacted by another means or at another address, every appointment message uses it.
  rationale: In the United States, providers must accommodate reasonable requests to communicate by alternative means or at alternative locations, without asking why (45 CFR 164.522(b)). In the EU, GDPR requires appropriate security and confidentiality of personal data (Article 5(1)(f)). For some patients an ordinary reminder could be unsafe.
- id: BR-H5
  text: A personal representative can book and manage appointments for the patient they represent, when the Patients module confirms they may act for the patient for this appointment. Every action they take records them as acting_representative.
  rationale: HIPAA treats a personal representative as the patient (45 CFR 164.502(g)), but not for a minor's care the minor could consent to themselves or that a parent agreed to keep confidential (164.502(g)(3)), and state and member state law add their own rules. That is a judgment about the person, so Identity makes it and this module applies the answer.
- id: BR-H6
  text: When a patient needs an interpreter, the appointment records the language so a qualified interpreter can be arranged at no cost to the patient.
  rationale: In the United States, covered entities must offer qualified interpreters free of charge (45 CFR 92.201). In the EU, language assistance depends on member state law. Recording the need at booking is what makes it possible on the day, everywhere.
- id: BR-H7
  text: Every view and every change of an appointment is logged with who, when and what.
  rationale: HIPAA requires audit controls that record activity in systems holding electronic health information (45 CFR 164.312(b)), and GDPR Article 32 requires security appropriate to the risk. Core history covers changes, not views.
- id: BR-H8
  text: An appointment recorded by mistake is marked entered in error. It is kept, hidden from schedules, and excluded from statistics.
  rationale: Records are never deleted in a medical record. FHIR has the same entered-in-error status.
- id: BR-H9
  text: Connection details for a virtual appointment are shared only with its participants.
  rationale: The link gives access to a session where health information is discussed.
- id: BR-H10
  text: Check-in is allowed from check_in_window before the start until the end.
  rationale: Checking in much earlier would mark someone as present who has not arrived for this appointment.
- id: BR-11
  text: A request to erase a patient's appointment data is refused while the Patients module's record retention for the patient applies, because the appointment and its history are part of the medical record. When retention ends, personal fields are erased as the core Appointment specification describes.
  rationale: HIPAA gives no right to erasure, and US hospitals must keep records for at least 5 years (42 CFR 482.24(b)(1)), longer in many states. In the EU, GDPR Article 17(3)(c) lets health data be kept for care under Article 9(2)(h). Erasing on request would break the record.
- id: BR-H11
  text: When a patient needs a disability accommodation, such as a sign-language interpreter or an accessible room, the appointment records it so it can be arranged at no cost to the patient.
  rationale: In the United States, covered entities must provide auxiliary aids and services at no cost so communication with people with disabilities is as effective as with others (45 CFR 92.202). In the EU this depends on member state law.
- id: BR-H12
  text: 'A patient, or their personal representative, can obtain a copy of their appointment data. The request is answered within the Patients module''s access_request_deadline: 30 days in the United States, extendable once by 30 days with written notice; one month in the EU, extendable by two months. In the EU the first copy is free.'
  rationale: HIPAA gives individuals the right to inspect and obtain a copy of their health information (45 CFR 164.524). GDPR gives a right of access and a free first copy (Article 15) within one month (Article 12(3)).
- id: BR-H13
  text: In the United States, automated reminder texts and calls go only to the number the patient provided, name the provider and how to contact them, are limited to one a day and three a week per patient, keep texts to 160 characters and calls to one minute, and offer an opt-out in every message, honoured immediately.
  rationale: These are the conditions under which healthcare messages to mobile phones are exempt from prior consent (47 CFR 64.1200(a)(9)(iv)). Breaking any of them turns a reminder into an unlawful call or text.
  jurisdiction: us
- id: BR-H14
  text: A virtual appointment records where the patient will be. In the United States, the practitioner must be licensed or legally permitted to practise in the state where the patient is located. In the European Union, telemedicine is provided in the member state where the provider is established, whose rules apply.
  rationale: 'HHS: providers "must ... be licensed or legally permitted to practice in the state where the patient is located". In the EU, telemedicine is considered provided in the member state where the provider is established (Directive 2011/24/EU, Article 3(d)), so the patient''s location is recorded but not checked against a licence.'
- id: BR-H15
  text: reason and service_type are coded with the configured code systems, and a code must be valid in the release in effect on the appointment date.
  rationale: 'HIPAA adopts ICD-10-CM for diagnoses and HCPCS with CPT-4 for services (45 CFR 162.1002). In Europe, coding is set nationally: WHO''s ICD-11 has been the global standard since 1 January 2022, but countries are still moving from ICD-10. Codes are revised over time, so a code is checked against the release in force on the day.'
- id: BR-H16
  text: 'When the Patients module merges two patient records, every appointment of the retired record is moved to the record that remains: the patient participant changes, the appointment keeps its id, and the change is recorded in its history. The overlap rule (BR-4) is not applied to the move: appointments that now overlap are kept, and appointment.patient_review_needed is emitted for each so a Scheduler decides.'
  rationale: After a merge, the retired record is never used again. An appointment left on it would disappear from the patient's schedule and from check-in. Duplicate records often hold the same visit booked twice; refusing the merge would keep the duplicate, and silently cancelling one would lose a real booking.
- id: BR-H17
  text: When a patient dies, their appointments that have not started are cancelled with the reason patient_deceased, and no message about them is sent to the patient. If the death is retracted, the cancelled appointments are listed for a Scheduler to rebook.
  rationale: Reminders and appointment letters to a person who has died distress their family. A death recorded in error is corrected by a person, so rebooking is a person's decision too.
- id: BR-H18
  text: When a merge is undone, appointments moved by it return to the restored record. Appointments booked on the remaining record since the merge stay there, and appointment.patient_review_needed is emitted for each so a Scheduler checks whose they are.
  rationale: Only the appointments the merge moved are known to belong to the restored record. Those booked since could belong to either person, so a person checks.
- id: BR-H19
  text: When the Patients module marks a patient entered in error, their appointments that have not started are cancelled with the reason patient_entered_in_error, and no message is sent.
  rationale: The record was created by mistake, so there is nobody to remind. Keeping the bookings holds slots other patients need.
- id: BR-H20
  text: A checked-in appointment can return to booked, by undoing the check-in, until care has started. Undoing it emits appointment.check_in_undone.
  rationale: Checking a patient in to the wrong appointment is a common front-desk mistake. Without a way back, the right appointment can't be checked in and the wrong visit stays on record.
- id: BR-H21
  text: In the United States, when an appointment is booked or rescheduled at least 3 business days ahead, Billing is told the booking time and the scheduled date, so a good faith estimate can be given to an uninsured or self-pay patient in time.
  rationale: The No Surprises Act requires a good faith estimate for uninsured or self-pay patients within 1 business day of scheduling when the service is 3 to 9 business days away, and within 3 business days when it is 10 or more (45 CFR 149.610). Scheduling starts the clock, so Billing must learn of it at once.
  jurisdiction: us
- id: BR-H22
  text: 'A cancellation records a code from the HL7 appointment cancellation reasons. Reasons the service sets itself keep their own code, mapped to one HL7 code for exchange: patient_deceased to pat-dec and patient_entered_in_error to oth-err.'
  rationale: FHIR Appointment.cancellationReason uses the HL7 appointment-cancellation-reason code system, so other systems and reports can tell a patient cancelling from a provider or an error. A local free-text reason can't be counted or exchanged.
- id: BR-H23
  text: A new appointment cannot be booked, rebooked or rescheduled for a patient the Patients module records as deceased.
  rationale: BR-H17 cancels a deceased patient's appointments, but a booking made afterwards, such as from an old referral, would send reminders to a grieving family and hold a slot nobody will use.
- id: BR-H24
  text: Where no_show_limit is set, a patient, or their representative, cannot book for themselves once the patient has had that many no-shows within no_show_lookback; staff can still book for them. The refusal tells them to contact the organization.
  rationale: Many hospitals restrict self-booking after repeated missed appointments, so staff can talk to the patient first. No-show rates are higher for patients facing transport, cost or language barriers, so a blanket refusal can cut off care; the limit is off by default and never stops staff booking.
- id: BR-H25
  text: A participant's response is exchanged as FHIR participant.status (accepted, declined, tentative, needs-action), and whether they are required as participant.required.
  rationale: FHIR R5 requires a status for every appointment participant, with the same four values as iCalendar, so the core response maps one to one.
- id: BR-4
  text: A patient cannot have two active appointments whose slots overlap. A practitioner can, only when their department has allow_overbooking on, and with no more than max_overbooked_per_slot appointments overlapping one slot. Slots are half-open.
  rationale: No-show rates differ by clinic and specialty, and a 2023 systematic review found the effect of overbooking on no-shows uncertain. So each department decides, and it is off by default. A patient, though, can only be in one place.
- id: BR-H26
  text: A room, or an item of equipment, cannot hold two active appointments whose slots overlap.
  rationale: Two patients cannot be examined in one room, or scanned with one portable machine, at once. The core Appointment specification leaves resources to the layers that need them (ADR-11), and FHIR books both as appointment participants.
- id: BR-H27
  text: For an outpatient or virtual appointment, check-in opens an encounter and the appointment records its encounter_id. A checked-in appointment of either kind completes only once its encounter exists.
  rationale: In FHIR, the appointment is fulfilled and linked to the newly created encounter at check-in. The encounter is where care is documented.
- id: BR-H28
  text: A department with referral_required on accepts a booking only with a valid referral.
  rationale: Many health plans pay for specialist care only with a referral from the primary care doctor (HealthCare.gov), and 31 of 38 OECD countries have GP gatekeeping, 24 of them mandatory. Checking before the slot is taken avoids a visit the clinic will not be paid for, or must turn away on the day.
- id: BR-H29
  text: An outpatient walk-in gets an appointment that starts now, marked walk_in, and is checked in straight away. Emergency arrivals are not walk-ins.
  rationale: Every visit needs an appointment to link its encounter and keep statistics complete.
- id: BR-H30
  text: Every practitioner on an appointment holds a role in its department on the appointment date.
  rationale: A practitioner's privileges and schedule are per department and per period (FHIR PractitionerRole). Booking them into a clinic they do not work in fills a slot nobody will staff.
- id: BR-H31
  text: An appointment's room and equipment belong to its department.
  rationale: A department schedules only what it controls. Booking another department's room takes it without that department knowing.
- id: BR-H32
  text: An appointment with previous_appointment_id is for the same patient as that appointment, and starts no earlier than it ends. A booking or reschedule that would break the order is rejected, whichever appointment moves.
  rationale: 'Some care must happen in order: the blood test before the consultation that reviews it, or imaging before the surgeon sees the patient. FHIR links such appointments with previousAppointment. Checking on every move stops a reschedule from silently reversing them.'
- id: BR-H33
  text: When an appointment is cancelled or marked no_show, every active appointment whose previous_appointment_id names it is flagged needs_review with review_reason previous_cancelled. Nothing is cancelled automatically.
  rationale: The later appointment may now be pointless, as with a consultation with no results to review, or still be needed. A person decides.
- id: BR-H34
  text: Booking never waits for prior authorization. The appointment records the authorization_status and authorization_id the Authorization module reports, and the department worklist shows them. When the status becomes denied, the appointment is flagged needs_review with review_reason authorization_denied.
  rationale: 'Payer decisions take time: from January 1, 2026, CMS requires impacted payers to decide within 72 hours for expedited and 7 calendar days for standard requests (CMS-0057-F). Holding the slot while that runs would lose it, and a denial does not always mean the care should not happen, so a person decides.'
- id: BR-H35
  text: needs_review clears when the appointment is rescheduled, cancelled, or when a Scheduler marks it reviewed.
  rationale: A flag nobody can clear stops meaning anything. Each of these is a decision about the appointment.
- id: BR-H36
  text: A virtual appointment has care_setting virtual, and an appointment with care_setting virtual has virtual_service.
  rationale: HL7 classes remote care as VR, not AMB. Keeping the setting and the connection details in step keeps encounters and statistics right.
- id: BR-H37
  text: When the encounter for a checked-in appointment starts, the appointment completes. When that encounter is discontinued or cancelled before care, the appointment is cancelled with the reason left_without_being_seen or encounter_cancelled.
  rationale: FHIR marks an appointment fulfilled and linked to its encounter; once the encounter decides what happened, the appointment follows. Without this, checked-in appointments never close and worklists fill with old visits.
- id: BR-H38
  text: Undoing a check-in (the Clinical appointments rule) tells Encounters to cancel the planned encounter opened for it. It is refused once that encounter has started.
  rationale: The wrong visit's encounter must not stay on record, and once care has started the check-in was real.
- id: BR-H39
  text: An appointment in a department Organization marks as sensitive, such as a substance use disorder program, carries that department's sensitivity codes. It is shown only to the department's staff and the patient, and its messages name neither the department nor the service.
  rationale: Records that identify someone as a patient of a US federally assisted substance use disorder program are protected by 42 CFR Part 2, compliance date February 16, 2026. An appointment at that program identifies the patient as much as the encounter does.
- id: BR-H40
  text: After a patient merge, appointments that now overlap are flagged needs_review with review_reason patient_merged.
  rationale: Duplicate records often hold the same visit booked twice. A Scheduler decides which booking is real.
- id: BR-H41
  text: Charges for care come from encounters. Appointments give Billing only no-shows and the late cancellation flag, for missed-appointment fees where the jurisdiction allows them.
  rationale: The encounter is what was done and is coded; the appointment is the plan. Charging from both would bill one visit twice.
- id: BR-H42
  text: When the encounter opened at check-in is marked entered in error, a checked-in appointment that has not ended returns to booked, as an undone check-in, so it can be checked in correctly. One that has ended is flagged needs_review with review_reason encounter_entered_in_error.
  rationale: The encounter was opened by mistake, often for the wrong patient. Leaving the appointment checked in would keep it out of every worklist, and only a person can tell what happened at a visit that has already ended.
- id: BR-H43
  text: An appointment booked to carry out an order records it, and Orders learns when the appointment is booked, rescheduled, cancelled or completed.
  rationale: IHE Scheduled Workflow books imaging and procedures against the order that asked for them, and FHIR records it as Appointment.basedOn. Without the link an order can sit unscheduled with nobody noticing.
- id: BR-H44
  text: Booking never waits for financial clearance. The appointment records the status Coverage reports, and the department worklist shows it, so staff can verify insurance and explain costs before the day.
  rationale: HFMA patient access practice verifies insurance and estimates the patient's cost before scheduled services. Holding the slot for it would lose it; showing it lets staff act in time.
- id: BR-H45
  text: An appointment is pre-registered when the patient has confirmed their details with the Patients module and Coverage reports financial clearance, both after booking. Check-in for a pre-registered appointment only verifies identity.
  rationale: HFMA calls registration before the service the best way to speed up access. Asking again at the desk what the patient already gave wastes their time and the front desk's.
- id: BR-H46
  text: 'The cancellation reasons this layer sets map to HL7 appointment cancellation reasons for exchange: left_without_being_seen to pat, because the patient left, and encounter_cancelled to other.'
  rationale: BR-H22 requires every service-set reason to map to one HL7 code. HL7 has no code for leaving before being seen, and an encounter cancelled before care has no single cause, so these are the closest codes that don't misstate who cancelled.
- id: BR-H47
  text: When Organization reports that a practitioner no longer holds a role in a department from a date, or that a room or item of equipment is out of service for a period, each active appointment that relies on it in that period is flagged needs_review with review_reason resource_unavailable. Nothing is cancelled automatically.
  rationale: BR-H30 and BR-H31 are checked at booking, but people leave and rooms close afterwards. Without a flag, patients arrive for a doctor who has gone or a room that is shut. A Scheduler moves or cancels each one, because another practitioner or room may suit.
- id: BR-H48
  text: When Encounters merges a duplicate encounter into another, appointments linked to the duplicate are linked to the remaining one. When Encounters moves an encounter to another patient, appointments linked to it are flagged needs_review with review_reason encounter_moved.
  rationale: HL7 merges (A42) and moves (A45) visits. A link to a retired encounter loses the visit; an appointment whose encounter now belongs to someone else was probably booked for the wrong patient too, which only a person can decide.
- id: BR-H49
  text: During an EHR downtime, each department's schedule stays readable from a read-only copy refreshed at least hourly. Bookings, check-ins and cancellations made on paper are entered afterwards with their original times and downtime_entry set. Two paper bookings for the same participant or room at the same time are both kept and flagged needs_review for a person, rather than one being refused.
  rationale: The SAFER Contingency Planning guide expects a read-only backup updated at least hourly and paper records entered and reconciled after recovery (REF-H40). Refusing a paper booking that already happened would hide care that took place.
- id: BR-H50
  text: When the Practitioners module reports a practitioner suspended or inactivated, each future appointment with them is marked needs_review with review_reason practitioner_unavailable and appointment.review_needed is emitted, so a scheduler rebooks or cancels it; nothing is cancelled automatically. When it reports two practitioners merged, appointments with the source practitioner are changed to the remaining one, recorded in history.
  rationale: A patient booked with a clinician who can no longer practise here would arrive to no one, and a merged-away practitioner id would break the link to their schedule; who rebooks and when is a person's decision.

workflows:
- name: Check in
  trigger: The patient arrives
  actor: Front desk
  steps:
  - Find the appointment
  - Verify the patient with two identifiers, such as name and date of birth
  - Ask the patient to confirm their details, and record it in the Patients module
  - Record checked_in_at and identity_verified_with, set status to checked_in and increase revision
  - Emit appointment.checked_in
  - Report care to the Patients module
  outcome: The patient is recorded as present and verified, ready to be seen.
- name: Mark entered in error
  trigger: An appointment was created by mistake, for example for the wrong patient
  actor: Scheduler
  steps:
  - Set status to entered_in_error with a reason, and increase revision
  - Withdraw pending reminders and release the slot
  - Emit appointment.entered_in_error
  outcome: The mistake is kept for the record but no longer affects schedules or statistics.
- name: Follow a patient merge
  trigger: Patients emits patient.merged
  actor: System
  steps:
  - Find the appointments whose patient participant is the retired record
  - Change the participant to the remaining record, record it in history, and increase revision
  - Emit appointment.updated for each
  outcome: Every appointment shows on the one record in use.
- name: Follow a death
  trigger: Patients emits patient.deceased
  actor: System
  steps:
  - Find the patient's appointments that have not started
  - Cancel each with the reason patient_deceased, without messaging the patient
  - Emit appointment.cancelled for each
  outcome: No one is booked or reminded after their death.
- name: Follow a patient entered in error
  trigger: Patients emits patient.entered_in_error
  actor: System
  steps:
  - Find the patient's appointments that have not started
  - Cancel each with the reason patient_entered_in_error, without messaging anyone
  - Emit appointment.cancelled for each
  outcome: No slot is held for a record that never existed.
- name: Register a walk-in
  trigger: A patient arrives without an appointment
  actor: Front desk
  steps:
  - Verify the patient with two identifiers
  - Book an appointment starting now in the department, marked walk_in
  - Check the patient in, which opens an encounter
  - Emit appointment.booked and appointment.checked_in
  outcome: The patient is in the queue like any other, with an encounter to document the visit.
- name: Link the encounter
  trigger: The Encounter module reports the encounter it opened
  actor: System
  steps:
  - Record encounter_id and increase revision
  - Emit appointment.encounter_linked
  outcome: The appointment and its encounter point to each other.
- name: Review a linked appointment
  trigger: An appointment is cancelled or marked no_show
  actor: System
  steps:
  - Find the active appointments whose previous_appointment_id names it
  - Flag each one needs_review with review_reason previous_cancelled, and increase its revision
  - Emit appointment.review_needed for each
  outcome: The next step in the sequence is looked at by a person, not left waiting on care that will not happen.
- name: Record prior authorization
  trigger: Authorization reports a status for an appointment
  actor: System
  steps:
  - Record authorization_status and authorization_id, and increase revision
  - If the status is denied, flag needs_review with review_reason authorization_denied and emit appointment.review_needed
  - Emit appointment.authorization_updated
  outcome: The worklist shows which appointments still need authorization before the day.

decisions:
- id: ADR-H1
  decision: Check-in is a status between booked and completed.
  rationale: FHIR has checked-in and arrived statuses, and front desks work from the list of who is here.
  alternatives: Record arrival only on the encounter. Rejected because scheduling needs to know who has arrived before an encounter starts.
  date: '2026-10-05'
- id: ADR-H2
  decision: Retention overrides erasure (BR-11) while the medical record must be kept.
  rationale: Both US and EU law protect the medical record from erasure on request.
  date: '2026-10-05'
- id: ADR-H3
  decision: One jurisdiction setting, with explicit settings for anything that varies inside a jurisdiction.
  rationale: Retention differs by US state and EU member state, so the jurisdiction alone cannot decide it.
  date: '2026-10-05'
- id: ADR-H4
  decision: Whether someone may act for a patient, including for minors, is the Patients module's decision; this module asks for each appointment.
  rationale: The rules depend on the patient's age, the kind of care and US state or EU member state law. Deciding them here would copy another module's job.
  date: '2026-10-06'
- id: ADR-H5
  decision: Overbooking is a per-department setting with a limit and no default, and it never applies to patients.
  rationale: No-show rates differ by clinic, and the evidence on overbooking is uncertain, so each department decides and sets its own limit. A patient is never in two places.
  alternatives: Never overbook. Rejected because clinics with high no-show rates rely on it.
  date: '2026-10-05'
- id: ADR-H6
  decision: The Encounter module opens the encounter; this layer only links it.
  rationale: Keeps the clinical record in one place, as FHIR separates Appointment from Encounter.
  date: '2026-10-05'
- id: ADR-H7
  decision: Prior authorization is shown on the appointment but never blocks booking or check-in.
  rationale: Payers may take up to 7 calendar days to decide (CMS-0057-F), and whether to proceed without authorization is a clinical and financial decision, not a scheduling rule.
  alternatives: Book as pending until authorized. Rejected because the slot would be lost or held for days, and the core pending expiry does not fit payer timeframes.
  date: '2026-10-06'
- id: ADR-H8
  decision: Appointments that must happen in order are linked one to the next with previous_appointment_id, not grouped.
  rationale: It matches FHIR Appointment.previousAppointment, and a chain says what must come first, which a group alone does not.
  alternatives: A visit group with sequence numbers. Rejected as more than ordering needs.
  date: '2026-10-06'

A clinical appointment is still an appointment: everything in the
[core Appointment specification](/docs/core/appointment) applies. This layer adds what
changes when the participants are a patient and a practitioner and the data is
health information.

## The patient is verified at the desk, not assumed

Check-in uses two identifiers, such as name and date of birth, never a room
number (BR-H2). Two patients with the same name in the same waiting room is an
ordinary day in a hospital, and checking in the wrong one attaches care and
results to the wrong person.

## A reminder says when, never why

A reminder lands on a shared phone, a postcard, a lock screen. So it carries
only what the patient needs to turn up: name, date, time, place and a number to
call (BR-H3). If a patient asked to be contacted somewhere else, every message
goes there (BR-H4).

## The appointment is part of the medical record

Appointments are not erased on request while the record must be kept (BR-11).
US hospitals keep records for at least 5 years, and GDPR lets health data be
kept for care. Mistakes are marked entered in error, never deleted (BR-H8), and
every view and change is logged (BR-H7).

## The United States and Europe, from one setting

The `jurisdiction` setting chooses the legal regime, and the Patients module's record retention is
set per state or member state, with legal advice (AS-H3). Rules that apply in only one
region, such as the US limits on automated reminder texts (BR-H13), are labelled "US only"
or "EU only", and a project in one region can hide the other's.

## A department books only what it controls

A practitioner is booked only into a department they work in on that date
(BR-H30), and the room and equipment must belong to the department (BR-H31).
Two patients can't share a room or a portable scanner at the same time
(BR-H26).

## A clinic can overbook a doctor, never a patient

No-show rates differ by clinic, and the evidence on overbooking is uncertain.
So each department decides whether to overbook and sets its own limit, with no
default (BR-4). A patient can still only be in one place.

## Care that must happen in order stays in order

A blood test before the consultation that reviews it is a chain, not two
separate bookings. Neither can be moved out of order (BR-H32), and if the first
is cancelled or missed, the second is flagged rather than kept or cancelled
blindly (BR-H33).

## Prior authorization never holds a slot hostage

Payers can take days to decide, so booking never waits. The worklist shows the
status Authorization reports, and a denial flags the appointment for a person
to decide (BR-H34).
