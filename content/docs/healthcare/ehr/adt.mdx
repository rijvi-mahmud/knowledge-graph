---
title: Admission, discharge and transfer
description: Admitting patients to a hospital stay, assigning and moving beds, leave, discharge, and the notices and notifications each step requires.
module: adt
version: 0.2.0
status: draft
industry: healthcare
domain: ehr
purpose: 'Manage each hospital stay from the decision to admit to the moment the patient leaves: admission requests and orders, beds and the bed board, transfers, leave, changes between observation and inpatient, discharge, and the notices and notifications the law ties to each step. Every step is reported to Encounters, which keeps the clinical record of the stay.'
scope:
- Pending admissions, admissions and cancelled admissions
- Admission orders and the practitioners allowed to give them
- 'The bed board: bed status, occupancy and reservations'
- Bed assignment, including patients admitted and waiting for a bed
- Pending and actual transfers inside the hospital, and cancelled transfers
- Leave of absence
- Changes between observation and inpatient
- Pending discharge, discharge, and cancelled discharge
- The Medicare discharge-rights notice and the observation notice
- Admission, discharge and transfer notifications to the patient's practitioners
- The census of patients in beds
nonGoals:
- The clinical record of the stay. Encounters owns it; this module reports each step to it.
- Emergency department arrival, triage and emergency transfers to other hospitals under EMTALA. The Emergency department module owns them.
- 'Bed and room definitions: which beds exist, where, and what they can be used for. The Organization module owns them.'
- Discharge planning content, care coordination and the discharge summary. Care management and Clinical documentation own them.
- Infection control decisions, such as who needs isolation. Clinical modules decide; this module only places patients accordingly.
- Visitation rights and visitor policies. A Visitors module owns them.
- Admission and discharge orders themselves. Orders owns them; this module checks one exists.
- Insurance, utilization review decisions and billing. Coverage and Billing own them.

assumptions:
- id: AS-E1
  text: Orders holds admission, observation and discharge orders, with who gave them and when.
  rationale: Medicare requires an inpatient admission order from a qualified practitioner (42 CFR 412.3); this module needs only to know it exists.
- id: AS-E2
  text: Organization defines beds, their unit and features, and which practitioners have admitting privileges.
  rationale: Admitting privileges are granted by the hospital to a practitioner, not by this module.
- id: AS-E3
  text: Coverage says whether a patient is a Medicare beneficiary.
  rationale: The discharge-rights and observation notices apply to Medicare beneficiaries.
- id: AS-E4
  text: The jurisdiction and notice settings are set with legal advice before go-live.
  rationale: Notices and notification duties differ by country and US state.
glossary:
- term: Stay
  definition: A patient's time in a hospital bed as an inpatient or under observation, from admission to discharge.
- term: Pending admission
  definition: A decision to admit that hasn't happened yet, such as an elective surgery next week or an emergency patient waiting for a bed (IHE pending admit, HL7 A14).
- term: Boarding
  definition: An admitted patient still waiting in the emergency department, or elsewhere, for an inpatient bed.
- term: Bed board
  definition: The live view of every bed's status and occupant.
- term: Important Message from Medicare
  definition: The standardized notice of a Medicare inpatient's rights, including discharge appeal rights (42 CFR 405.1205).
- term: MOON
  definition: The Medicare Outpatient Observation Notice, given to patients in observation for more than 24 hours (42 CFR 489.20(y)).
- term: Census
  definition: The patients in beds at a point in time, such as midnight, by unit.
concepts:
- name: This module moves the patient; Encounters records it
  description: Every admission, transfer, leave and discharge happens here and is reported to Encounters, which keeps it on the clinical record of the stay (FHIR keeps one Encounter for the stay).

businessRules:
- id: BR-E1
  text: A patient is admitted only with an admission order, or an observation order for observation, from a practitioner with admitting privileges. The order and practitioner are recorded on the stay.
  rationale: Medicare treats a patient as an inpatient only if formally admitted under an order from a qualified practitioner with admitting privileges (42 CFR 412.3(a) and (b)).
- id: BR-E2
  text: A patient has at most one stay that is pending or admitted. A newborn's stay is its own, linked to the mother's through the Patients module.
  rationale: A patient is in one hospital bed at a time; two stays send orders and charges to the wrong one.
- id: BR-E3
  text: A pending admission opens a planned encounter through Encounters. Admission starts it. Cancelling the pending admission cancels it.
  rationale: IHE pending admit (A14) and its cancel (A27) let beds and staff be prepared; Encounters keeps one record from the start.
- id: BR-E4
  text: A patient can be admitted before a bed is ready. Until one is assigned, the stay records where they wait, such as the emergency department, and is listed as boarding.
  rationale: Admitted patients often wait for beds. Recording them as admitted, with their real location, keeps orders, staff and the census correct.
- id: BR-E5
  text: A bed takes a patient only if it is unoccupied, not reserved for another stay, and on a unit that provides the stay's level of care. One bed holds one patient, except a newborn rooming in with their mother. An isolated bed takes only a patient needing the same isolation; closed and contaminated beds take nobody. Where room_sharing_policy restricts who may share a room, it is applied too.
  rationale: HL7 bed status (Table 0116) tells the bed board what each bed can take. Putting a patient in a dirty or closed bed, or two in one, is a safety failure. The level of care decides staffing and monitoring, so an intensive care patient in a ward bed is a safety failure.
- id: BR-E6
  text: When a patient leaves a bed, by transfer, discharge or death, the bed becomes housekeeping until environmental services marks it unoccupied.
  rationale: A bed must be cleaned before the next patient. The housekeeping status is what stops the next assignment until it is.
- id: BR-E7
  text: A transfer is requested first, which reserves the target bed for this stay. The transfer moves the patient and frees the old bed; cancelling the request releases the reservation. A transfer recorded by mistake can be cancelled, returning the patient to the previous bed if it is still free.
  rationale: IHE defines pending transfer (A15), transfer (A02) and their cancels (A26, A12). Reserving the target stops another patient being placed there in the meantime.
- id: BR-E8
  text: During a leave of absence the stay keeps its bed, reserved, and Encounters puts the encounter on hold. The leave ends when the patient returns.
  rationale: IHE leave of absence (A21) and return (A22). The bed is still the patient's, so nobody else may be placed in it.
- id: BR-E9
  text: A patient is discharged with a discharge order, except when they leave against medical advice or die. The discharge records the disposition and destination, frees the bed, and is reported to Encounters, Appointments and Exchange.
  rationale: A discharge is a clinical decision recorded as an order. A patient who leaves against advice, or dies, is discharged without one, with that disposition.
- id: BR-E10
  text: A discharge recorded by mistake can be cancelled while the patient's bed is still free or reserved; the stay returns to admitted.
  rationale: IHE cancel discharge (A13). If the bed has been given away, a new bed must be assigned first.
- id: BR-E11
  text: A change from observation to inpatient needs an inpatient admission order and takes effect at the order's time. A change from inpatient to observation is allowed only before discharge, with who approved it and why recorded.
  rationale: Medicare counts inpatient status from the admission order (42 CFR 412.3); observation time before it stays outpatient. Changing an inpatient back needs a recorded decision because it changes what the patient pays.
- id: BR-E12
  text: For a Medicare beneficiary admitted as an inpatient, the Important Message from Medicare is due no later than 2 calendar days after admission, and a follow-up copy no more than 2 calendar days before discharge, unless the first was given within 2 days of discharge. Due notices are listed until recorded as given.
  rationale: Hospitals must give Medicare inpatients a standardized notice of their rights, including discharge appeal rights, at these times (42 CFR 405.1205(b) and (c)).
- id: BR-E13
  text: For a Medicare beneficiary in observation, the MOON is due no later than 36 hours after observation starts, or sooner on transfer, discharge or admission. It is listed until recorded as given.
  rationale: Hospitals must give the notice to Medicare patients receiving observation as outpatients for more than 24 hours (42 CFR 489.20(y)).
- id: BR-E14
  text: Admissions, discharges and transfers out of the hospital are announced so the Exchange module can notify the patient's established practitioners and post-acute providers, unless the patient's privacy preferences or the law prevent it. notification_preferences_default decides whether notifications go unless the patient objects, or only after asking.
  rationale: Hospitals with electronic records must send notifications at inpatient admission and at or just before discharge or transfer, consistent with the patient's expressed privacy preferences (42 CFR 482.24(d)).
- id: BR-E15
  text: Admission is announced to Care management, so patients likely to need discharge planning are identified early, and discharge is announced with its destination so necessary information goes with the patient.
  rationale: Hospitals must identify patients needing discharge planning at an early stage and send necessary medical information at discharge to the providers responsible for follow-up (42 CFR 482.43(a) and (b)).
- id: BR-E16
  text: When a patient dies during a stay, the discharge records disposition expired, and the Patients module is told the time and that the death was pronounced in the hospital.
  rationale: The death must be recorded once, by the module that owns it, and the stay must close so the bed and record follow.
- id: BR-E17
  text: At admission, the patient is asked about the facility directory, and Encounters records the answer.
  rationale: HIPAA lets a hospital list a patient in its directory only after telling them and giving them a chance to object (45 CFR 164.510(a)). Admission is the first chance.
- id: BR-E18
  text: A patient in a sensitive department, or with a restricted record, is placed and shown on the bed board only to staff who need it, with name masking where the Patients module requires.
  rationale: The bed board is visible across the hospital. It must not reveal what Part 2 or a privacy alias protects.
- id: BR-E19
  text: Every view and change of a stay is logged with who, when and what.
  rationale: HIPAA requires audit controls (45 CFR 164.312(b)).
- id: BR-E20
  text: Fields the service sets, such as status, admitted_at, discharged_at, observation_started_at, notices, bed_reserved_for and revision, cannot be written by clients, and every change sends the revision it read.
  rationale: Status and times drive notices, billing and census; a client writing them skips the rules. Concurrent bed moves must not overwrite each other.
- id: BR-E21
  text: When the Patients module records a death, the patient's pending admissions are cancelled, and an admitted stay not already discharged as expired is discharged with disposition expired at the recorded time.
  rationale: A death recorded elsewhere, such as by a registrar or from an outside report, must still close the stay, free the bed and stop the notices.
- id: BR-E22
  text: After a patient merge, if the remaining patient has two stays pending or admitted, both are kept and listed for a bed manager to resolve; the one-stay rule (BR-E2) is not applied to the move. When a merge is undone, stays recorded since the merge are listed for review.
  rationale: Duplicate records can each hold an open stay for the same admission. Refusing the merge keeps the duplicate; closing one silently could discharge a real patient.
- id: BR-E23
  text: When the Patients module marks a patient entered in error, their pending admissions are cancelled.
  rationale: The record was created by mistake, so the planned stay will never happen.
- id: BR-E24
  text: A patient, or their representative within the authority the Patients module records, can read their own stays electronically as soon as they are recorded, with no review delay. They can ask for part of a stay record to be corrected; an HIM specialist decides within the Patients module's correction_deadline, and accuracy_contested marks the disputed part meanwhile if they ask.
  rationale: HIPAA gives rights of access and amendment (45 CFR 164.524 and 164.526), a delay in access is likely information blocking (45 CFR 171.103), and GDPR gives access, rectification and restriction (Articles 15, 16 and 18).
- id: BR-E25
  text: 'Where rooming_in is on, a newborn rooms in with their mother: the newborn''s stay shares the mother''s bed location without a bed of its own, and moves with her.'
  rationale: WHO recommends mothers and infants stay together 24 hours a day (Ten Steps to Successful Breastfeeding, Step 7). Requiring a separate bed would separate them on paper and block recording the baby's stay.

workflows:
- name: Admit from the emergency department
  trigger: An emergency physician decides to admit
  actor: Bed manager
  steps:
  - Record a pending admission with the admission order
  - Admit the patient; with no bed ready, record the emergency department as where they wait
  - Assign a bed when one is unoccupied and suitable
  - Ask about the facility directory, and start notice due dates
  - Announce the admission to Encounters, Exchange and Care management
  outcome: The patient is admitted at once, boarding is visible, and the notices and notifications start.
- name: Admit an elective patient
  trigger: A planned admission, such as surgery next week
  actor: Registrar
  steps:
  - Record the pending admission with the expected time; Encounters opens a planned encounter
  - On the day, admit with the order and assign the reserved or available bed
  outcome: Pre-admission work attaches to the stay before it starts.
- name: Transfer a patient
  trigger: A clinician or bed manager requests a move
  actor: Bed manager
  steps:
  - Request the transfer, reserving the target bed
  - Move the patient; the old bed becomes housekeeping
  - Announce the transfer to Encounters
  outcome: No one else is placed in the target bed, and the old bed is cleaned before reuse.
- name: Discharge a patient
  trigger: A discharge order is given
  actor: Registrar
  steps:
  - Record the expected discharge time when known, and the Medicare follow-up notice due date
  - Discharge with disposition and destination
  - Free the bed for housekeeping
  - Announce the discharge to Encounters, Appointments, Exchange and Care management
  outcome: The stay closes, follow-up providers are notified, and the bed is cleaned.
- name: Clean a bed
  trigger: A bed becomes housekeeping
  actor: Environmental services
  steps:
  - Clean the bed
  - Mark it unoccupied
  outcome: The bed is ready for the next patient.

apiConventions:
- topic: Authentication
  text: Every request carries the caller's identity from Identity, and permissions are checked against it.
- topic: Errors
  text: Errors use problem details (application/problem+json) with a code member, such as STAY_BED_UNAVAILABLE.
  reference: RFC 9457
- topic: Concurrency
  text: Every change sends the revision it read; a stale revision fails with STAY_REVISION_CONFLICT. Bed assignment also checks the bed's own state, so two stays never get one bed.
- topic: Retries
  text: Every change accepts an Idempotency-Key header (FR-E11).
  reference: IETF draft-ietf-httpapi-idempotency-key-header
- topic: Pagination
  text: 'Lists are cursor-based: limit and cursor in, next_cursor out.'
- topic: Times
  text: Instants are RFC 3339 timestamps in UTC (CON-E2).
  reference: RFC 3339
- topic: Versioning
  text: Additive changes don't change the version; a breaking change ships as a new major version with Deprecation and Sunset headers.
  reference: RFC 9745, RFC 8594

deliveryGuarantees:
- id: DG-E1
  text: Events are published at least once; consumers drop one with a source and id they have seen.
  rationale: CloudEvents defines source plus id as the duplicate check.
- id: DG-E2
  text: Events for one stay can arrive out of order; each carries the revision, and consumers ignore a lower one.
  rationale: Brokers and retries don't guarantee order.
- id: DG-E3
  text: Events carry ids, codes and times, never names or diagnoses. share_restricted tells Exchange the patient's preference prevents notifications.
  rationale: Minimum necessary (45 CFR 164.502(b)) and GDPR data minimisation (Article 5(1)(c)).

This module moves patients into, around and out of the hospital's beds. Each
step is reported to Encounters, which keeps the clinical record of the stay.

## Admitted doesn't mean in a bed

A patient admitted at 2 a.m. may wait in the emergency department until
morning. They're admitted at 2 a.m., with their real location, and listed as
boarding until a bed is ready (BR-E4), so orders, staffing and the census see
them.

## A bed is clean before it's reused

When a patient leaves, their bed goes to housekeeping until environmental
services marks it ready (BR-E6). Transfers reserve the target bed first, so
nobody else is placed there in between (BR-E7).

## The law ties notices to the stay's clock

Medicare inpatients get their rights notice within 2 days of admission and a
follow-up within 2 days before discharge (BR-E12). Patients in observation get
the MOON within 36 hours (BR-E13). This module knows the dates, so it lists
what's due until someone records it as given.
