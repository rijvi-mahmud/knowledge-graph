---
title: Appointment
description: The scheduling lifecycle (booking, rescheduling, cancellation, reminders and attendance) in industry-neutral terms.
module: appointment
version: 0.12.0
status: stable
purpose: Own the lifecycle of a reserved time slot between participants, independent of what the appointment is for.
scope:
- Book, confirm, reschedule, update, cancel and close out an appointment
- Group appointments with a seat limit, and appointments that belong to a series
- Own the status lifecycle and its legal transitions
- Prevent overlapping appointments for the same participant
- Keep times correct across time zones and daylight saving changes, and show them in each participant's own time zone
- Schedule reminders relative to the start time
- Record attendance per participant
- Emit lifecycle events other modules react to
nonGoals:
- Owning participant identity, which belongs to the Identity module
- Owning payment or billing, which belongs to the Billing module
- Owning what happens during the appointment, such as notes or outcomes
- Computing availability, working hours, buffers or minimum booking notice. Appointment asks Calendar.
- Defining appointment types (default length, booking limits). They belong to an Appointment type module.
- Defining recurrence patterns. A Recurrence module expands a pattern into individual appointments that share a series.
- Booking rooms, chairs or devices. Industry and domain layers add resources where they need them.
- Delivering notifications. Appointment emits reminder_due and Notification delivers it.
- Prescribing storage, transport or code. This specification states what must happen, not how it is built.
actors:
- name: Requester
  description: Party who initiates a booking. May be a participant or someone acting on their behalf.
- name: Participant
  description: Party expected to attend. Every appointment has at least two.
- name: Scheduler
  description: Operator with elevated rights to book, move, cancel or close out appointments on behalf of others.
- name: System
  description: Automated actor that dispatches reminders and, where enabled, records no-shows after a grace period.
assumptions:
- id: AS-1
  text: Calendar is the authority on availability, including working hours, buffers, minimum notice and how far ahead bookings are allowed.
  rationale: Appointment asks Calendar instead of computing availability (ADR-4). If Calendar answers wrongly, Appointment books what it was told is free.
- id: AS-2
  text: Identity ids stay resolvable for the life of an appointment, including after a participant's account is deactivated.
  rationale: Appointments are kept forever (BR-2) and keep referring to their participants. Identity owns what happens to the person behind the id.
- id: AS-3
  text: Notification delivers reminders and formats times for each participant. Appointment only decides when a reminder is due.
  rationale: Channels, templates and languages vary by deployment and don't belong in the booking lifecycle.
- id: AS-4
  text: Server clocks are synchronised to UTC, for example with NTP.
  rationale: Whether a start time is in the future, and when a reminder fires, are decided by server time. A drifting clock accepts bookings in the past and sends reminders late.
- id: AS-5
  text: Identity can hold a person's preferred time zone and locale.
  rationale: People move and travel. Reading the current preference keeps times and reminders right without editing every appointment (BR-9).
- id: AS-6
  text: When a calendar closes, its active appointments are cancelled through Cancel in bulk, each with a reason.
  rationale: Every appointment belongs to one calendar. How and when a calendar closes is Calendar's concern; this module only provides the way to cancel what is on it.
- id: AS-7
  text: This module tells Calendar the status of every appointment through its events. How Calendar shows that time is Calendar's concern.
  rationale: Keeps the boundary clean. For pending appointments, iCalendar's BUSY-TENTATIVE free/busy type is the natural fit.
glossary:
- term: Slot
  definition: A half-open time range [start, end) that an appointment reserves. The end instant is not part of the slot.
- term: Active appointment
  definition: 'An appointment that holds its slot: booked, or pending while pending_holds_slot is on.'
- term: No-show
  definition: An appointment that a participant did not attend, recorded after its start time.
- term: Reminder offset
  definition: Duration before the start time at which a reminder is dispatched.
- term: Revision
  definition: A counter that increases with every change to an appointment. Like SEQUENCE in iCalendar (RFC 5545), it tells others which version they have.
- term: Wall-clock time
  definition: A local date and time, such as 09:00 on 3 March, read in a named time zone.
- term: Reference time zone
  definition: The IANA time zone the appointment's time was agreed in. Usually where it takes place, or the host's zone for a remote appointment.
- term: Participant time zone
  definition: The IANA time zone a participant sees times in. It can differ from the reference time zone.
- term: Group appointment
  definition: One appointment with a host and several guests who share the same slot, up to its capacity.
- term: Capacity
  definition: The most participants a group appointment can hold.
- term: Series
  definition: Appointments created from one recurrence pattern. Each is a separate appointment that shares a series_id.
- term: Late cancellation
  definition: A cancellation made within late_cancellation_window before the start.
- term: Close-out
  definition: 'Recording what happened after an appointment started: completed, or a no-show, with attendance per participant.'
concepts:
- name: Appointment
  description: A reserved time slot between two or more participants, with a status lifecycle.
- name: Participant
  description: An identity taking part in the appointment, with a role such as host or guest, and the time zone they see times in. Industry layers define their own roles.
- name: Reminder
  description: A scheduled notification derived from the appointment. Not independently bookable.
- name: Reschedule
  description: A time change that keeps the appointment's id and history and increases its revision, rather than creating a new appointment.
- name: Attendance
  description: Whether each participant attended or was absent, recorded at close-out.
- name: Series
  description: A group of appointments made from one recurrence pattern. Changing one never changes the others.
dataModel:
- name: id
  type: string
  required: true
  description: Stable identifier, immutable for the life of the appointment.
- name: participants
  type: array<{ identity_id, role, required, response, time_zone?, locale?, attendance? }>
  required: true
  description: The parties involved. role is a label such as host or guest; industry layers define their own roles. time_zone and locale override the person's preferences in Identity for this appointment. attendance is attended or absent, recorded at close-out.
  constraints: 'at least 2 entries and no more than capacity; identity_id values are distinct and resolvable; each time_zone is a valid IANA name; each locale is a valid BCP 47 tag; required defaults to true; response is needs_action, accepted, declined or tentative, accepted for the participant who booked; codes: iCalendar ROLE (RFC 5545): required REQ-PARTICIPANT, optional OPT-PARTICIPANT; iCalendar PARTSTAT: NEEDS-ACTION, ACCEPTED, DECLINED, TENTATIVE'
- name: start_time
  type: local datetime
  required: true
  description: Wall-clock start in time_zone.
  constraints: before end_time
- name: end_time
  type: local datetime
  required: true
  description: Wall-clock end in time_zone. It is exclusive, so the slot ends immediately before this time.
  constraints: after start_time
- name: time_zone
  type: string
  required: true
  description: The reference time zone. start_time and end_time are read in this IANA zone, for example America/New_York.
  constraints: a valid IANA tz database name, not a UTC offset
- name: start_at
  type: instant
  required: true
  description: Absolute start, derived from start_time and time_zone. Used for overlap checks, reminders and ordering.
  constraints: derived, never written by clients; recomputed when tz database rules change
- name: end_at
  type: instant
  required: true
  description: Absolute end, derived from end_time and time_zone.
  constraints: derived, never written by clients
- name: tz_version
  type: string
  required: true
  description: The time zone rules release used to derive start_at and end_at, so the instants can be recomputed after a rules change.
  constraints: derived, never written by clients
- name: status
  type: enum
  required: true
  description: Current lifecycle state.
  constraints: 'pending | booked | cancelled | completed | no_show; changed only through operations; codes: iCalendar STATUS (RFC 5545): pending TENTATIVE, booked CONFIRMED, cancelled CANCELLED'
- name: revision
  type: integer
  required: true
  description: Starts at 0 and increases by 1 on every change. Every change must name the revision it was based on.
- name: capacity
  type: integer
  required: false
  description: Most participants allowed, for a group appointment. Unset means no limit beyond BR-1.
  constraints: at least 2
- name: series_id
  type: string
  required: false
  description: Shared by the appointments of one series.
- name: rebooked_from_id
  type: string
  required: false
  description: The cancelled appointment this one replaces, when it was rebooked (BR-27).
  constraints: set by the service; an appointment that was cancelled
- name: appointment_type_id
  type: string
  required: false
  description: The appointment type this booking was made from, owned by the Appointment type module.
- name: confirm_by
  type: instant
  required: false
  description: 'When a pending appointment expires if nobody confirms it: the booking time plus confirmation_timeout.'
  constraints: derived, never written by clients; set when status = pending
- name: late_cancellation
  type: boolean
  required: false
  description: Whether the appointment was cancelled within late_cancellation_window before the start.
  constraints: set when status = cancelled
- name: location
  type: string
  required: false
  description: Physical address or virtual meeting reference.
- name: reminder_offsets
  type: duration[]
  required: false
  description: When reminders fire, each measured back from start_at.
  constraints: each offset positive; when unset, default_reminder_offsets applies
- name: notes
  type: string
  required: false
- name: history
  type: array<{ revision, change, actor, at, previous_start_at?, previous_end_at? }>
  required: true
  description: 'Every change to the appointment, oldest first: booked, confirmed, rescheduled, updated, participant_added, participant_removed, participant_responded, rebooked, cancelled, completed, no_show, attendance_corrected or anonymised.'
  constraints: append-only; entries are never changed or removed, except that erasure replaces actor identity ids (BR-11)
- name: created_at
  type: instant
  required: true
- name: updated_at
  type: instant
  required: true
- name: cancelled_reason
  type: string
  required: false
  constraints: required when status = cancelled
- name: cancelled_by
  type: string
  required: false
  description: Identity id of the actor who cancelled.
  constraints: set when status = cancelled
- name: cancelled_at
  type: instant
  required: false
  constraints: set when status = cancelled
- name: completed_at
  type: instant
  required: false
  constraints: set when status = completed
- name: no_show_recorded_by
  type: string
  required: false
  description: Identity id of the Scheduler who recorded the no-show, or system.
  constraints: set when status = no_show
- name: anonymised_at
  type: instant
  required: false
  description: When personal fields were erased (BR-11). The appointment, its status and its times remain.

businessRules:
- id: BR-1
  text: An appointment must have at least two distinct participants.
  rationale: A single-party slot is a calendar block, not an appointment. Keeping them distinct stops this module absorbing calendar concerns.
- id: BR-2
  text: Cancelling never deletes the record. It sets status = cancelled and records who cancelled, when and why.
  rationale: Billing, audit and analytics hold references to the appointment id and break on hard delete. Cancellation reasons are also the main input for reducing late cancellations.
- id: BR-3
  text: Rescheduling keeps the appointment id and records the previous times in the appointment's history. It never creates a new appointment.
  rationale: Attendance and no-show statistics must follow one appointment across time changes. Cancel-and-rebook would count every reschedule as a cancellation.
- id: BR-4
  text: A participant cannot have two active appointments whose slots overlap. Slots are half-open, so an appointment ending at 10:00 and one starting at 10:00 do not overlap.
  rationale: Double booking is a data-integrity failure, not a user preference, so it must hold under concurrent requests. Treating the end as inclusive would reject every back-to-back schedule.
- id: BR-5
  text: 'Completed and cancelled are terminal: the appointment never changes again. A no-show can change only to completed, to correct a wrong record (BR-19).'
  rationale: Terminal states let consumers treat records as final. A no-show is the one exception, because a mistaken no-show can trigger fees and must be correctable.
- id: BR-6
  text: Every appointment has one reference time zone. Its times are stored as wall-clock time in that zone, and absolute instants are derived from them and recomputed for future appointments when time zone rules change.
  rationale: People agree to a time in a particular place, such as 09:00 in New York. Governments change daylight saving rules with little notice, and an instant computed before the change would move the appointment.
- id: BR-7
  text: A reminder whose dispatch time has already passed when the appointment is booked or rescheduled is skipped. It never causes the booking to fail.
  rationale: With a 24-hour default, rejecting such bookings would block every same-day appointment. The remaining reminders still fire.
- id: BR-8
  text: A no-show can be recorded only after the appointment's start time. By default a Scheduler records it; automatic recording after a grace period is a per-deployment setting.
  rationale: Leading scheduling products record no-shows manually, because only staff know whether someone is running late, attended remotely or was seen elsewhere. A wrongly recorded no-show is terminal and can trigger fees.
- id: BR-9
  text: 'Every participant sees the appointment, and receives its reminders, in their own time zone and language: the ones set on the appointment, otherwise their preferences in Identity, otherwise the appointment''s time zone and default_locale. Times shown always include the zone or offset.'
  rationale: Participants in different countries share one moment, not one clock reading. The gap between two zones also changes during the year because countries switch daylight saving time on different dates, so a fixed offset per participant would be wrong for weeks at a time.
- id: BR-10
  text: A wall-clock time that doesn't exist in the reference time zone, because clocks went forward, is rejected. A wall-clock time that occurs twice, because clocks went back, must come with a UTC offset to say which one is meant; without one it's rejected.
  rationale: Guessing silently books the wrong hour. A time sent with its UTC offset removes the doubt.
- id: BR-11
  text: When a participant's personal data must be erased, notes, location and cancelled_reason are cleared, identity ids are replaced with an anonymous placeholder, and anonymised_at is set. The appointment, its status and its times are kept. Data needed for a legal obligation or legal claims is kept until that need ends.
  rationale: GDPR Article 17 gives people a right to erasure, but Article 17(3) allows keeping data needed for a legal obligation or legal claims. Clearing personal fields instead of deleting the row keeps billing and audit references intact (BR-2).
- id: BR-12
  text: A reminder is never sent after the appointment has started.
  rationale: After an outage, a scheduler can fire a backlog of overdue reminders at once. A reminder for an appointment that has already started is noise, and for a past one it's wrong.
- id: BR-13
  text: A group appointment never holds more participants than its capacity.
  rationale: Group sessions have a physical or practical limit. Cal.com calls these seats per time slot, and Calendly allows 2 to 9,999 invitees per group event.
- id: BR-14
  text: Every change to an appointment, including cancellation, must name the revision it was based on, and increases the revision by 1. A change based on an older revision is rejected.
  rationale: Without it, someone can cancel an appointment that was just moved without ever seeing the new time.
- id: BR-15
  text: Reminder offsets are exact durations before start_at. Across a clock change, a 24-hour reminder lands an hour earlier or later on the clock.
  rationale: This matches how Google Calendar defines reminders (minutes before the start). For 09:00 on 8 March 2026 in New York, the 24-hour reminder fires at 08:00 the day before.
- id: BR-16
  text: An appointment can be rescheduled, updated, given new participants or cancelled only while it is active and before its end. After the end, only close-out is allowed.
  rationale: Once an appointment is over, what's left is to record what happened. Moving or cancelling it afterwards would rewrite history.
- id: BR-17
  text: When requires_confirmation is on, a booking starts as pending and gets no reminders. While pending_holds_slot is on, it holds its slot. It becomes booked when confirmed, and is cancelled with the reason "not confirmed" if confirm_by passes first.
  rationale: Cal.com holds bookings that need confirmation as pending, and iCalendar has a TENTATIVE status. Holding the slot means a confirmation never fails because someone else took it; turning it off keeps the slot open to others, at the cost that confirming can fail.
- id: BR-18
  text: A cancellation made within late_cancellation_window before the start is marked as a late cancellation.
  rationale: Late cancellations cost a booked slot that can rarely be refilled. Industry layers use the mark, for example to charge a fee or record a code.
- id: BR-19
  text: A Scheduler can correct a no-show to completed. The correction is kept in the history.
  rationale: Cal.com lets hosts change attendance after the fact. A wrong no-show can count against someone and trigger fees.
- id: BR-20
  text: Participants who are not Schedulers cannot cancel or reschedule within self_service_cutoff before the start.
  rationale: A business needs time to refill a slot. Acuity Scheduling lets businesses set this limit and suggests 12 hours by default.
- id: BR-21
  text: Attendance is recorded per participant at close-out. Completing marks participants as attended unless stated otherwise; recording a no-show names at least one absent participant.
  rationale: In a group, some people come and some don't. Cal.com records absence separately for the host and each attendee.
- id: BR-22
  text: Each appointment in a series is independent. Rescheduling, cancelling or closing out one never changes the others.
  rationale: Cal.com creates a separate booking for each occurrence, linked by a recurring id. Independent occurrences keep this lifecycle the same for every appointment.
- id: BR-23
  text: A participant can be removed only if at least two participants remain.
  rationale: BR-1 must keep holding after every change, not only at booking.
- id: BR-24
  text: If an appointment has a participant with the role host, the last host cannot be removed.
  rationale: A host confirms, updates and changes participants (permission matrix). Without one, a group appointment has nobody to run it.
- id: BR-25
  text: Fields the service sets, such as status, start_at, end_at, history, rebooked_from_id and the cancellation and completion fields, cannot be written by clients. Status changes only through its operations.
  rationale: A client that writes status directly skips the state machine, its guards and its events. One that writes start_at bypasses the time zone rules.
- id: BR-26
  text: 'Each participant has a response: needs_action until they answer, then accepted, declined or tentative; the participant who books is accepted. Only the participant, or a Scheduler for them, records it. A participant who declines gets no reminders. When a required participant declines, the appointment is not cancelled automatically; appointment.participant_responded tells the host or a Scheduler, who decides.'
  rationale: iCalendar gives every attendee a participation status (PARTSTAT) and a role saying whether they are required (RFC 5545), and calendar products show who accepted or declined. Without it, an appointment whose other party already said no looks confirmed. Cancelling automatically would remove an appointment the host may want to keep with someone else.
- id: BR-27
  text: A cancelled appointment can be rebooked at a new time. The new appointment copies its participants and details, records rebooked_from_id, and is checked like any booking. The cancelled appointment never changes.
  rationale: Cancelled is terminal (BR-5), so a cancellation made by mistake, or one the participants want to undo, is reversed by rebooking. The link keeps the history that a fresh booking would lose, and the original slot may already be taken, so the new one is checked again.

stateMachine:
  initial: booked
  states:
  - pending
  - booked
  - cancelled
  - completed
  - no_show
  transitions:
  - from: pending
    to: booked
    trigger: confirm
    guard: confirm_by has not passed, revision matches, and the slot is still free when pending_holds_slot is off
  - from: pending
    to: pending
    trigger: reschedule or update
    guard: before the end, revision matches
  - from: pending
    to: cancelled
    trigger: cancel
    guard: cancelled_reason provided, revision matches
  - from: pending
    to: cancelled
    trigger: expire
    guard: confirm_by has passed
  - from: booked
    to: booked
    trigger: reschedule or update
    guard: before the end, new slot is free and in the future, revision matches
  - from: booked
    to: cancelled
    trigger: cancel
    guard: before the end, cancelled_reason provided, revision matches
  - from: booked
    to: completed
    trigger: complete
    guard: start_at has passed
  - from: booked
    to: no_show
    trigger: record_no_show
    guard: start_at has passed, at least one absent participant named
  - from: no_show
    to: completed
    trigger: correct_attendance
    guard: actor is a Scheduler
workflows:
- name: Book an appointment
  trigger: Requester submits a booking request
  actor: Requester
  steps:
  - Validate the time range, duration, time zone, participants and capacity
  - Ask Calendar whether the slot is free for every participant
  - Check for overlapping active appointments and save the appointment in one step, as pending if requires_confirmation is on, otherwise as booked, at revision 0
  - If booked, schedule one reminder per offset whose dispatch time is still in the future
  - Emit appointment.booked
  outcome: The appointment holds its slot, as pending or booked.
- name: Reschedule an appointment
  trigger: Requester or Scheduler proposes a new time
  actor: Scheduler
  steps:
  - Verify the appointment is active, before its end, and that the supplied revision matches
  - Validate the new slot and check availability with Calendar
  - Check for overlaps, update the times, increase revision and record the previous times in history, in one step
  - Withdraw pending reminders and schedule new ones from the new start
  - Emit appointment.rescheduled
  outcome: The same appointment id now holds the new slot, with a higher revision.
- name: Cancel an appointment
  trigger: Requester, Scheduler or an upstream policy cancels
  actor: Requester
  steps:
  - Verify the appointment is booked
  - Record cancelled_reason, cancelled_by and cancelled_at, and set status = cancelled
  - Withdraw pending reminders
  - Emit appointment.cancelled
  outcome: The appointment is retained as cancelled, its slot is released and no reminders fire.
- name: Close out an appointment
  trigger: The start time has passed
  actor: Scheduler
  steps:
  - If the appointment took place, complete it, record completed_at, and record attendance per participant
  - If a participant did not attend, record a no-show naming the absent participants
  - Emit appointment.completed or appointment.no_show
  outcome: The appointment reaches a terminal state that reflects what happened.
- name: Dispatch a reminder
  trigger: A scheduled reminder's dispatch time is reached
  actor: System
  steps:
  - Confirm the appointment is still booked and the reminder belongs to its current revision
  - Emit appointment.reminder_due
  - Notification delivers it to the participants
  outcome: Participants are reminded ahead of the appointment, and never about a superseded time.
- name: Confirm a pending appointment
  trigger: A Scheduler or the host confirms
  actor: Scheduler
  steps:
  - Verify the appointment is pending, confirm_by has not passed and the revision matches
  - Set status to booked and increase revision
  - Schedule its reminders
  - Emit appointment.confirmed
  outcome: The appointment is booked and its reminders are scheduled.
- name: Expire an unconfirmed appointment
  trigger: confirm_by passes while the appointment is pending
  actor: System
  steps:
  - Cancel the appointment with the reason "not confirmed"
  - Release its slot
  - Emit appointment.cancelled
  outcome: The slot is free again.
- name: Update details
  trigger: A participant or Scheduler changes the location, notes, reminders or capacity
  actor: Scheduler
  steps:
  - Verify the appointment is active, before its end, and the revision matches
  - Apply the change and increase revision
  - Re-derive pending reminders
  - Emit appointment.updated
  outcome: The appointment shows the new details at a new revision.
- name: Add or remove a participant
  trigger: A Scheduler or the host changes who attends
  actor: Scheduler
  steps:
  - Verify the appointment is active, before its end, and the revision matches
  - For an addition, check capacity and that the new participant has no overlapping active appointment
  - For a removal, check that at least two participants remain
  - Apply the change and increase revision
  - Emit appointment.participant_added or appointment.participant_removed
  outcome: The participant list changes, and the overlap and capacity rules still hold.
- name: Cancel in bulk
  trigger: 'A Scheduler cancels many appointments at once: one participant in a time window (for example when a provider is ill), or the remaining appointments of a series'
  actor: Scheduler
  steps:
  - 'Find the active appointments that match: the participant within the window, or the series from a given time onwards'
  - Cancel each one separately with the given reason, as in Cancel an appointment
  - Return the result for each appointment, including any that could not be cancelled
  outcome: Each appointment is cancelled on its own, with its own events and reminders withdrawn.
- name: Correct a no-show
  trigger: A Scheduler finds a no-show was recorded by mistake
  actor: Scheduler
  steps:
  - Verify the appointment is a no-show
  - Change it to completed and record attendance
  - Keep the correction in the history
  - Emit appointment.attendance_corrected
  outcome: The appointment is completed and the mistake is traceable.
- name: Signal the end
  trigger: end_at passes while the appointment is still booked
  actor: System
  steps:
  - Emit appointment.ended so a Scheduler knows it needs closing out
  outcome: Appointments are not forgotten in booked after they finish.
- name: Record a no-show automatically
  trigger: automatic_no_show is on and no_show_grace_period has passed after start_at with no completion
  actor: System
  steps:
  - Record a no-show with every participant marked absent, and no_show_recorded_by = system
  - Increase revision and add the change to history
  - Emit appointment.no_show
  outcome: Forgotten appointments are closed out. A Scheduler can still correct the record (BR-19).
- name: Erase personal data
  trigger: A request to erase a person's data, or personal_data_retention passing after an appointment's end
  actor: System
  steps:
  - Find the person's appointments, skipping data that must be kept for a legal obligation or claim
  - Clear notes, location and cancelled_reason, and replace the identity ids in participants and history with an anonymous placeholder
  - Set anonymised_at, increase revision and add the change to history
  - Emit appointment.anonymised for each appointment changed
  outcome: The person is no longer identifiable from their appointments, and the records still add up.
- name: Reschedule in bulk
  actor: Scheduler
  trigger: A Scheduler moves many appointments at once, for example when a provider changes the day they work
  steps:
  - Find the active appointments that match the selection
  - 'Work out each new slot: the new time and weekday, or the shift applied to its wall-clock times'
  - Reschedule each one separately, as in Reschedule an appointment, with its own checks and events
  - Return the result for each appointment, including any that could not be moved
  outcome: Each appointment keeps its id, calendar and history, and none moves into a conflict.

constraints:
- id: CON-1
  text: Time zones are identified by IANA names, and every time exchanged with another system carries its UTC offset, and its time zone when it has one.
  rationale: An offset alone loses the daylight saving rules, and a zone alone is ambiguous for repeated times. Together they lose nothing.
- id: CON-2
  text: The overlap rule holds for every way an appointment can be written, including concurrent requests and bulk imports, not only in one code path.
  rationale: A check that runs before a separate save lets two concurrent requests both succeed, and imports that skip the check create double bookings.
- id: CON-3
  text: Reminders and timed transitions (expiry, automatic no-shows, end signals) still happen after the service restarts.
  rationale: Appointments are booked months ahead, and a restart can't be allowed to lose what's scheduled (ADR-3).
- id: CON-4
  text: Each appointment records the time zone rules release its instants were derived with.
  rationale: When new rules are published, this identifies exactly which future appointments need their instants recomputed.

deliveryGuarantees:
- id: DG-1
  text: Events are published at least once. Consumers must treat an event with a source and id they have already seen as a duplicate.
  rationale: The outbox relay can publish an event twice if it crashes after publishing and before recording it. CloudEvents defines source plus id as the duplicate check.
- id: DG-2
  text: Events for one appointment can arrive out of order. Each payload carries the appointment's revision, and consumers ignore an event whose revision is lower than one they've already applied.
  rationale: Brokers and retries don't guarantee order. The revision gives every change to an appointment a sequence.
- id: DG-4
  text: Adding an optional field to a payload is not a breaking change. Removing or renaming one is, and ships as a new event type, such as appointment.booked.v2.
  rationale: Consumers ignore fields they don't know, but break when a field they use disappears.
- id: DG-5
  text: Every event carries a unique id, its type, the appointment it is about, when the change happened, and the revision.
  rationale: The id lets consumers drop duplicates (DG-1), and the revision lets them order events (DG-2). The CloudEvents specification requires the same id, type and source on every event.

decisions:
- id: ADR-1
  decision: Cancellation is a status change, never a delete.
  rationale: Downstream modules keep references to appointment ids, and hard delete breaks billing and audit trails.
  alternatives: Hard delete with a tombstone table. Rejected because it duplicates state and complicates reads.
  date: '2026-01-15'
- id: ADR-2
  decision: Rescheduling changes the existing appointment and increases its revision instead of creating a successor.
  rationale: Attendance history and no-show statistics must follow one appointment across time changes, and the revision gives clients the same signal as iCalendar's SEQUENCE.
  alternatives: Cancel the original and book a linked replacement, as Cal.com and Calendly do. Rejected because every reschedule then counts as a cancellation and history is split across records. Integrations with those systems map each revision to a new linked booking.
  date: '2026-01-15'
- id: ADR-3
  decision: Reminders are delegated to a durable scheduler rather than in-process timers.
  rationale: Appointments are booked months ahead, and in-process timers don't survive deploys or restarts.
  alternatives: A periodic sweep over the table. Viable, but adds polling latency and load at scale.
  date: '2026-01-20'
- id: ADR-4
  decision: Availability, including working hours, buffers and minimum notice, is asked of Calendar rather than computed inside Appointment.
  rationale: Those rules legitimately differ per deployment and per resource. Keeping them out keeps the booking lifecycle identical everywhere.
  date: '2026-02-02'
- id: ADR-5
  decision: Rescheduled is an event and a revision, not a status.
  rationale: A rescheduled appointment is still booked. A separate status forced a confirm step back to booked, and left completion and no-shows undefined for rescheduled appointments. iCalendar and Google Calendar have no rescheduled status either.
  alternatives: Keep rescheduled as a status. Rejected for the reasons above.
  date: '2026-10-05'
- id: ADR-6
  decision: Each appointment stores wall-clock time in one reference time zone, with instants derived, and each participant has their own display time zone.
  rationale: For future events, an instant computed today can be wrong by the time the event happens if time zone rules change. One reference zone keeps a single source of truth, and converting from the instant gives every participant the correct local time. Google Calendar and RFC 5545 attach a zone to the event, and Cal.com stores the host's and each attendee's zone separately.
  alternatives: Store only UTC instants. Rejected because a rule change silently moves future appointments. Store a fixed UTC offset. Rejected because offsets carry no daylight saving rules. Store each participant's local time. Rejected because the copies disagree after a rule change.
  date: '2026-10-05'
- id: ADR-7
  decision: No-shows are recorded by a Scheduler by default, with automatic recording after a grace period as an opt-in setting.
  rationale: Acuity Scheduling and Cal.com both record no-shows manually. Automatic expiry mislabels late arrivals and remote attendance, and a no-show is terminal and can carry fees.
  alternatives: Always record no-shows automatically when end_at passes. Rejected for the reasons above.
  date: '2026-10-05'
- id: ADR-8
  decision: A series is a set of independent appointments that share a series_id. The recurrence pattern belongs to a Recurrence module.
  rationale: Cal.com creates one booking per occurrence, linked by a recurring id. Independent occurrences keep one lifecycle for every appointment, and moving one never surprises the others.
  alternatives: One appointment with a recurrence rule, as iCalendar does with RRULE. Rejected because changing or cancelling one occurrence then needs exceptions throughout this lifecycle.
  date: '2026-10-05'
- id: ADR-9
  decision: A pending appointment holds its slot by default, and a deployment can turn that off (pending_holds_slot).
  rationale: If it holds nothing, confirmation can fail because someone booked the slot meanwhile. Some businesses prefer to keep slots open until they accept, so Cal.com makes this a switch, and so does this module.
  alternatives: Always hold, or never hold. Rejected because practice differs between businesses.
  date: '2026-10-05'
- id: ADR-10
  decision: Attendance is recorded per participant; the appointment status still says how it ended.
  rationale: Group appointments need to know who came. The status keeps a single answer for reporting.
  date: '2026-10-05'
- id: ADR-11
  decision: Core checks overlaps per participant only. Resources such as rooms or chairs are added by the layers that need them.
  rationale: Which resources matter differs by industry. The EHR layer adds examination rooms in its own rules.
  date: '2026-10-05'
- id: ADR-12
  decision: Appointment types (default length, booking limits) are a separate module that this one references.
  rationale: Cal.com, Calendly and Acuity all model the type separately from the booking. Keeping it out keeps this lifecycle the same however types are defined.
  date: '2026-10-05'

risks:
- id: RISK-1
  risk: The time zone database falls behind a government's rule change.
  impact: Future appointments in that zone show and remind at the wrong hour.
  mitigation: Keep the database current and recompute affected appointments using tz_version (CON-4, EC-9).
- id: RISK-2
  risk: A bulk import or admin script writes appointments without the overlap check.
  impact: Double bookings that the application would have rejected.
  mitigation: Enforce the overlap rule for every way an appointment can be written, not only in one code path (CON-2).
- id: RISK-3
  risk: A scheduler outage builds a backlog of reminders.
  impact: Late or pointless reminders sent all at once when it recovers.
  mitigation: Drop reminders for appointments that have started (BR-12), and alert on reminder delay (PERF-4).
- id: RISK-4
  risk: Erasure requests conflict with retention needed for billing, audit or legal claims.
  impact: Either unlawful retention or broken records.
  mitigation: Erase personal fields but keep the record (BR-11), and set personal_data_retention with legal advice.
- id: RISK-5
  risk: Free-text notes collect sensitive data the deployment didn't plan for.
  impact: Data protection obligations the deployment isn't meeting.
  mitigation: Classify notes as the most sensitive category the deployment handles (Privacy and retention), and erase them with other personal fields.

apiConventions:
- topic: Authentication
  text: Every request carries the caller's identity from the deployment's identity provider. Permissions are checked against that identity (Permission matrix).
- topic: Errors
  text: Errors use problem details with the media type application/problem+json. status is the HTTP status, detail says how to fix the request, and a code member carries the error code, such as APPOINTMENT_SLOT_TAKEN.
  reference: RFC 9457
- topic: Pagination
  text: Lists are cursor-based. A request sends limit (default_page_size, at most max_page_size) and an optional cursor; the response returns next_cursor until there are no more results. Results are ordered by start_at, then id (FR-20).
- topic: Concurrency
  text: Every change sends the revision it read. A stale revision fails with APPOINTMENT_REVISION_CONFLICT (BR-14).
- topic: Retries
  text: Every change accepts an Idempotency-Key header. A retry with the same key returns the original result (FR-8).
  reference: IETF draft-ietf-httpapi-idempotency-key-header
- topic: Rate limits
  text: A caller over its limit gets 429 Too Many Requests with a Retry-After header saying when to try again.
  reference: RFC 6585, RFC 9110
- topic: Times
  text: Instants are RFC 3339 timestamps in UTC, zoned times are RFC 9557 timestamps, and time zones are IANA names (CON-1, FR-11).
  reference: RFC 3339, RFC 9557
- topic: Versioning
  text: Additive changes, such as a new optional field, don't change the version. A breaking change ships as a new major version. The old one sends a Deprecation header, and a Sunset header with the date it will stop working.
  reference: RFC 9745, RFC 8594

An appointment reserves a time slot between two or more participants. Booking,
rescheduling, cancelling, reminding and closing it out work the same way
whether it's a customer meeting a consultant, a candidate meeting an
interviewer, or two colleagues meeting each other.

What changes between industries is what must be true before a slot can be held
and what happens after it closes. Those obligations live in the industry
layers.

## Appointment holds the slot; Calendar decides if it's free

Appointment owns when people meet. It doesn't own who they are, whether they
can pay, what was discussed, or when they're available.

Availability is the boundary that matters most. Working hours, holidays,
buffers between appointments and minimum booking notice all differ between
deployments and between resources. Appointment asks Calendar whether a slot is
free instead of deciding for itself, so the booking lifecycle stays the same
everywhere and those rules live in one place.

## Ten o'clock to eleven doesn't clash with eleven to noon

A slot includes its start and excludes its end, the convention iCalendar and
Google Calendar both use. Back-to-back appointments are allowed; overlapping
ones aren't (BR-4).

The overlap check has to hold when two requests arrive at the same moment. A
check in application code followed by a separate insert lets both requests
succeed. The rule has to hold for every way an appointment can be written,
including bulk imports, not only in one code path (CON-2, AC-4). The
[storage design](/docs/core/appointment/reference-implementation/tables) shows one way
to do that.

## One moment, shown in every participant's own time zone

Participants are often in different countries. An appointment at 09:00 in New
York on 3 March 2026 is 15:00 in Zurich and 23:00 in Tokyo. It's one moment,
and each participant sees it, and is reminded about it, in their own time zone
(BR-9, AC-13).

The gap between two zones isn't fixed. The United States switched to daylight
saving time on 8 March 2026 and Europe on 29 March, so for those three weeks
the same New York appointment is 14:00 in Zurich, not 15:00 (AC-14). Converting
from the absolute instant with the time zone database gets this right. A fixed
offset per participant doesn't.

## The agreed time is stored, not just the instant

Each appointment keeps the time it was agreed in, such as 09:00 plus
`America/New_York`, and derives the absolute instant from it (BR-6). The agreed
zone is usually where the appointment happens, or the host's zone when it's
remote.

Storing only UTC looks simpler, but it fails for future events. When a
government changes its daylight saving rules, an instant computed before the
change points to the wrong local time, and the appointment silently moves by an
hour. Keeping the agreed time means the instant can be recomputed when the time
zone database updates (AC-11).

APIs return both forms: `start_at` in UTC, and `start` in the RFC 9557 format
that carries the offset and the zone, such as
`2026-03-03T09:00:00-05:00[America/New_York]` (FR-11).

Twice a year a local time is unclear. In New York, 02:30 on 8 March 2026 never
happens, because clocks jump from 02:00 to 03:00. 01:30 on 1 November 2026
happens twice, because clocks go back an hour. The first is rejected, and the
second needs a UTC offset to say which 01:30 is meant (BR-10). Guessing would
quietly book the wrong hour.

## A reschedule changes the time, not the appointment

Moving an appointment keeps its id, records the previous time and increases its
revision (BR-3). Its attendance history stays on one record, and the revision
tells clients and calendar integrations that the time changed, the same signal
iCalendar carries in `SEQUENCE`.

Cal.com and Calendly take the other approach: they cancel the original
and book a linked replacement. Every reschedule then also counts as a
cancellation. Integrations with those systems can map each revision to a new
linked booking (ADR-2).

Rescheduling also isn't a status. A moved appointment is still booked, so it
can still be completed, cancelled or recorded as a no-show (ADR-5).

## A reminder that's already due is skipped, not refused

Each appointment can have several reminders. In a randomised trial, people
reminded both 3 days and 1 day ahead missed fewer appointments than people
reminded once.

When an appointment is booked closer to its start than a reminder's offset,
that reminder is skipped (BR-7). Rejecting the booking instead would make the
default 24-hour reminder block every same-day appointment.

## A no-show is recorded by a person, not inferred by a timer

Only someone at the appointment knows whether a participant is running late,
joined remotely or was seen elsewhere. Acuity Scheduling and Cal.com both
record no-shows manually, and so does this module by default (BR-8). A
deployment can opt in to recording them automatically after a grace period.

A no-show can't be recorded before the start time, and once recorded it's
final, like completion and cancellation (BR-5).

## Every change names the version it was based on

Each change, including a cancellation, names the revision it read, and a
change based on an older revision is rejected (BR-14). Without that, someone
can cancel an appointment that was just moved without ever seeing the new
time.

## A booking can wait for confirmation

Some businesses review requests before accepting them. With
`requires_confirmation` on, a booking starts as pending: it holds its slot, so
confirming never fails because someone else took it, but it gets no reminders.
If nobody confirms it in time, it's cancelled and the slot is released (BR-17).

## One slot, many guests

A group appointment has a host and several guests in the same slot, up to its
capacity (BR-13). Attendance is recorded for each person, because in a group
some come and some don't (BR-21). An appointment in a weekly series is still
its own appointment: moving one never moves the others (BR-22).

## Finished appointments never change

Completed and cancelled appointments are terminal. Consumers can cache them
without invalidation. Cancelling sets a status rather than deleting the
record, because billing, audit and analytics all reference the appointment id
(BR-2). A no-show is the one exception: a Scheduler can correct a mistaken
no-show to completed, and the correction stays in the history (BR-19).
