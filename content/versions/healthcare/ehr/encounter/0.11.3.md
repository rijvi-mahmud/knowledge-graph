- Items that start with "US only:" or "EU only:" apply only in that jurisdiction. Skip the ones for a jurisdiction your project doesn't operate in, or add ?jurisdiction=us or ?jurisdiction=eu to this URL to leave them out. Unmarked items apply everywhere.

## Purpose & scope

Keep the record of each interaction between a patient and the hospital: its class, status and period, the practitioners and locations involved, the reasons and diagnoses, and whether its record is complete. Every clinical module attaches its data to an encounter by id.

**In scope**

- Opening encounters from check-ins, emergency arrivals and admissions
- The encounter lifecycle: planned, in progress, on hold, discharged, completed, and cancelled, discontinued or entered in error
- The class of an encounter, and changes to it, such as observation to inpatient
- Participants, such as the attending practitioner, with their periods
- Locations during the encounter, with their periods
- Reasons for the encounter and its diagnoses, with their role and rank
- Encounters grouped under another, such as consultations during a stay
- Record completion and locking, and amendments after locking
- Sensitive encounters, such as at a substance use disorder program
- A department's and a practitioner's open and incomplete encounters
- Pre-admission: planned stays before the patient arrives
- The start of observation, for the US notice deadline
- Links to episodes of care
- Patients' own access to their encounters, and their correction requests
- Restricting disclosure to a health plan for care paid in full

**Out of scope**

- Booking. The Appointments module schedules visits; an encounter records what actually happened.
- Admission orders, beds, transfers and discharge orders. The Admission, discharge and transfer module owns them and reports them here.
- Triage and emergency department flow. The Emergency department module owns them and reports arrivals and triage here.
- Clinical notes, assessments, the history and physical, and discharge summaries. Clinical documentation owns them, attached to the encounter by id.
- Conditions and the problem list. The Conditions module owns them; an encounter refers to them, or carries a coded diagnosis.
- Coding for claims, diagnosis-related groups and charges. Billing owns them, using the diagnoses and class history this module gives.
- Consent to disclose sensitive records. The Consent module decides; this module flags the encounter.
- Episodes of care, such as a course of chemotherapy or a pregnancy. An Episodes module owns them; an encounter only links to them.

### Assumptions and dependencies

**AS-E1** Appointments, the Emergency department module and Admission, discharge and transfer report check-ins, arrivals, admissions, transfers and discharges as they happen.
  - Why: An encounter records what happened. The modules where it happens tell this one.
**AS-E2** Clinical documentation reports when an encounter's documentation is complete.
  - Why: Only the module holding the notes knows whether the record is complete (BR-E7).
**AS-E3** The jurisdiction, record_completion_deadline and diagnosis_code_system settings are set with legal advice before go-live.
  - Why: Completion deadlines and code systems differ by US state, by payer and by EU member state.

## Functions

### Business rules

**BR-E1** Every encounter has one patient in use, one class and one responsible department.
  - Why: Every clinical entry is found through its encounter. An encounter without a patient or department is care nobody can find or bill.
**BR-E2** An ambulatory or virtual encounter is opened when Appointments reports a check-in or a walk-in, at the moment encounter_opens_at sets: at check-in, as planned with subject status arrived, or when a practitioner starts care, in progress. It records the appointment and reports its own id back.
  - Why: FHIR is not consistent on this: the Appointment page says the encounter "is typically created when the service starts, not when the patient arrives", and the Encounter page lets it start as arrived. Practice varies, so it is a setting; either way waiting time and care time stay separate.
**BR-E3** An emergency encounter is opened, in progress with subject status arrived, when the Emergency department module registers an arrival. Its subject status follows triage, care and departure as that module reports them.
  - Why: In an emergency department the patient's position changes fast and matters for safety and for billing; FHIR tracks it as subject status.
**BR-E4** An inpatient or observation encounter is opened, in progress, when Admission, discharge and transfer reports an admission. Transfers add locations and close the previous one; leave puts the encounter on hold with subject status on leave; discharge sets discharged with the disposition and destination.
  - Why: The admission, transfers and discharge happen in that module; the encounter records them so the stay is one record from admission to discharge (FHIR admission component).
**BR-E5** A change of class, such as observation to inpatient, keeps the same encounter and adds the new class to class_history with the time it took effect.
  - Why: Medicare counts observation as outpatient care until an inpatient order, so the time in each class matters to billing; FHIR keeps class history (EncounterHistory).
**BR-E6** An encounter part of another has the same patient, except that a newborn's encounter can be part of its mother's encounter. A part's period is within its parent's, and the parent cannot be completed while a part is still open.
  - Why: FHIR groups encounters with partOf, such as a consultation during a stay, and says a child's encounter should reference the mother's encounter with partOf. A parent closed over an open part leaves care outside any complete record.
**BR-E7** A discharged encounter becomes completed only when Clinical documentation reports its documentation complete and a final diagnosis is recorded. It must happen within record_completion_deadline of actual_end; encounters past that are listed as overdue. If Clinical documentation reports documentation reopened before then, documentation_complete is cleared.
  - Why: US hospitals must record the final diagnosis and complete the medical record within 30 days after discharge (42 CFR 482.24(c)(4)(viii)). FHIR uses discharged for care that is over while the record is still being completed.
**BR-E8** A completed encounter is locked. Any later change is an amendment that records who, when, why and the previous value, and emits encounter.amended.
  - Why: Hospitals must keep record entries complete and protect their integrity (42 CFR 482.24(b)). Silent changes after completion would change a record others have relied on, including claims.
**BR-E9** A planned encounter that never starts is cancelled. An encounter that started but ended early, such as the patient leaving against advice or without being seen, is discontinued with a departure reason.
  - Why: FHIR separates encounters that never happened from those cut short. A patient who left is part of the record and of quality reporting, so nothing is deleted.
**BR-E10** An encounter can be marked entered in error only while no clinical data has been recorded against it (has_clinical_data unset). Otherwise its data is moved to the right encounter first.
  - Why: Discarding an encounter that holds notes or results would hide that care. Each clinical module moves its own entries.
**BR-E11** An inpatient or observation encounter has exactly one attending practitioner at any time. Every participant holds a role in the department on the date (Organization).
  - Why: Someone must be responsible for an admitted patient at every moment. A practitioner without a role there cannot be accountable for care in it.
**BR-E12** An encounter has at most one active location at a time, and each location's period is within the encounter's. When the patient goes somewhere temporarily, such as the scanner or dialysis, their bed becomes reserved and the temporary location active; on return the temporary location completes and the bed is active again.
  - Why: The patient is in one place, but their bed is still theirs. FHIR marks a held location reserved, and IHE tracks temporary transfers to radiotherapy, scanners and dialysis as their own movements.
**BR-E13** Each diagnosis has a role and a rank, and a code valid in diagnosis_code_system on the right date: the discharge date for inpatient encounters, the encounter date otherwise. A completed encounter has at least one discharge or billing diagnosis.
  - Why: CMS applies each fiscal year's ICD-10-CM codes to discharges and encounters from October 1 (the FY 2027 files to those from October 1, 2026). A code valid at admission may be retired by discharge.
**BR-E14** An encounter carries the sensitivity codes Organization gives for its department, such as SUD for a substance use disorder program. Data attached to it carries the same codes, and disclosure follows the Consent module.
  - Why: Records from US federally assisted substance use disorder programs have extra protection under 42 CFR Part 2, revised with a compliance date of February 16, 2026. Marking the encounter is what lets every module apply it.
**BR-E15** A virtual encounter records its connection details and where the patient is.
  - Why: In the United States a practitioner must be licensed where the patient is, as the Clinical appointments rules state.
**BR-E16** length is the time from actual_start to actual_end less time on leave.
  - Why: FHIR defines length as the time the encounter lasted, less time absent. Leave counts toward neither care nor billing time.
**BR-E17** When the Patients module merges two patients, their encounters move to the remaining record; when a merge is undone, encounters recorded since the merge are listed for review.
  - Why: An encounter on a retired record disappears from the patient's history. Only a person can tell whose care a post-merge encounter was.
**BR-E18** Every view and change of an encounter is logged with who, when and what. Opening an encounter of a restricted patient needs a reason, as the Patients module requires.
  - Why: HIPAA requires audit controls (45 CFR 164.312(b)). An encounter reveals why someone was seen.
**BR-E19** Fields the service sets, such as status, class_history, length, documentation_complete, record_due_at, locked_at, amendments, has_clinical_data and revision, cannot be written by clients.
  - Why: A client writing status skips the lifecycle and its guards; one writing locked_at changes what counts as the legal record.
**BR-E20** Every change sends the revision it read, and a stale revision is rejected.
  - Why: Registrars, clinicians and HIM staff change the same encounter; without this, one silently undoes another.
**BR-E21** A patient has at most one inpatient or observation encounter that is in progress or on hold. A patient merge can break this; BR-E29 handles it.
  - Why: A patient is in one hospital stay at a time. Two open stays send orders, results and charges to the wrong one.
**BR-E22** No encounter starts after the patient's recorded death.
  - Why: An encounter after death almost always means the wrong patient. A death recorded in error is retracted in the Patients module first.
**BR-E23** When an encounter enters observation, encounter.observation_started is emitted with observation_notice_due_at: the start plus observation_notice_deadline. Deciding who must receive the notice, and giving it, belongs to Admission, discharge and transfer.
  - Why: US hospitals must give Medicare patients who receive observation services as outpatients for more than 24 hours a standardized written notice no later than 36 hours after observation starts, or sooner on transfer, discharge or admission (42 CFR 489.20(y)). The encounter knows when observation began.
**BR-E24** A pre-admission from Admission, discharge and transfer opens a planned inpatient encounter with planned_start and the pre-admission id. The admission starts that same encounter; a cancelled pre-admission cancels it with the reason admission_cancelled.
  - Why: FHIR uses planned encounters for pre-admission, and HL7 v2 sends pre-admit (A05) and cancel pre-admit (A38). Testing and paperwork before an elective stay need an encounter to attach to.
**BR-E25** A change of responsible department keeps the encounter and adds the new department to department_history with the time it took over.
  - Why: Who was responsible at each point matters for billing, quality reporting and accountability, the same as class history.
**BR-E26** Encounters of a patient under legal hold are never erased or anonymised, even after the Patients module's retention ends.
  - Why: The Patients module stops erasure during litigation, and the encounters are the record in question.
**BR-E27** A re-admission is marked on the admission when Admission, discharge and transfer reports it.
  - Why: Readmissions are tracked for quality and payment. FHIR records it as admission.reAdmission.
**BR-E28** When the Patients module records a death, the patient's planned encounters are cancelled with the reason patient_deceased. Encounters in progress are discharged by Admission, discharge and transfer or a clinician.
  - Why: A planned stay or visit for someone who has died would stay on lists and schedules. Encounters in progress need a person to record what happened.
**BR-E29** After a patient merge, if the remaining patient has two inpatient or observation encounters in progress or on hold, both are kept and encounter.patient_review_needed is emitted for each, so an HIM specialist resolves them. Merging them (BR-E48) resolves it.
  - Why: Duplicate records can each hold an open stay for the same admission. Refusing the merge keeps the duplicate; keeping both silently breaks the one-stay rule (BR-E21).
**BR-E30** When Appointments undoes a check-in, the planned encounter opened for it is cancelled with the reason check_in_undone.
  - Why: The check-in was a mistake, so the visit it opened never happened.
**BR-E31** When the Patients module marks a patient entered in error, the patient's planned encounters are cancelled with the reason patient_entered_in_error.
  - Why: The record was created by mistake, so a planned visit or stay for it will never happen. Leaving it planned keeps it on lists and schedules.
**BR-E32** When the Patients module retracts a death, the encounters cancelled for it (BR-E28) are listed, and encounter.patient_review_needed is emitted for each so they can be planned again.
  - Why: A death recorded for the wrong patient must not silently cost them a planned admission. A person decides whether each one still stands.
**BR-E33** A check-in for a patient who already has an open ambulatory encounter in the same visit_grouping scope adds the appointment to that encounter instead of opening another.
  - Why: FHIR lets one encounter fulfil several appointments. A morning of blood draw, X-ray and consultation is one visit to document and bill, not three.
**BR-E34** Every encounter gets a visit number from visit_number_system when it is opened. It is never reused, and stays with the encounter through merges and amendments.
  - Why: Laboratory, radiology and billing systems match their data to a visit by its number (HL7 v2 PV1-19). A reused number would attach results to the wrong visit.
**BR-E35** An encounter records the orders it carries out, and Orders confirms each one is for the same patient.
  - Why: FHIR links an encounter to the request that initiated it (basedOn). Without the link nobody can tell whether an order was carried out.
**BR-E36** Pending events from Admission, discharge and transfer are recorded as plans: a pending transfer adds a planned location, a pending discharge sets planned_end, and each is removed when cancelled or replaced by the real event.
  - Why: IHE Patient Administration Management defines pending admit, transfer and discharge (A14, A15, A16, cancelled by A27, A26, A25). Bed management and discharge planning work from who is expected to move.
**BR-E37** A transfer recorded by mistake can be cancelled, which completes nothing and makes the previous location active again. An earlier movement is corrected by its entry id; on a locked encounter the correction is an amendment.
  - Why: IHE defines cancel transfer (A12) and identifies each movement so a past one can be corrected (Historic Movement option). Location-based billing and the record of where the patient was depend on it.
**BR-E38** A patient in a stay is listed in the facility directory (name and location, for people who ask by name) only after being told and not objecting, which sets directory_status listed. In an emergency or incapacity they can be listed if it fits their known wishes and best interest, and are asked as soon as they can be. An objection removes the listing at once, and restricted or very restricted patients are never listed.
  - Why: HIPAA lets a hospital keep a directory of name and location for people who ask by name, but only after telling the patient and giving them the chance to object, with an exception for incapacity or emergency until they can be asked (45 CFR 164.510(a)). Telling a caller someone is in hospital, against their wishes, can put them at risk.
**BR-E39** A patient, or their representative within the authority the Patients module records, can read their own encounters electronically as soon as they are recorded. No policy holds them back for review. A patient can ask for their own access to be delayed. Part of an encounter can be withheld only where a licensed professional decides access is reasonably likely to endanger life or physical safety; the decision is recorded and the patient can have it reviewed.
  - Why: HIPAA gives a right of access and allows denial on narrow reviewable grounds (45 CFR 164.524(a)(3)); a delay is likely information blocking (45 CFR 171.103); GDPR gives a right of access (Article 15), and the EHDS gives EU patients immediate access (Article 3).
**BR-E40** A patient, or their representative, can ask for an encounter to be corrected. An HIM specialist decides within the Patients module's correction_deadline, extendable once by correction_extension with a written reason. An accepted correction is made as an amendment linked to the request. A denied one gives the reason in writing, and the patient can add a statement of disagreement, which goes with later disclosures.
  - Why: HIPAA requires a decision within 60 days, with one 30-day extension, a written denial and a right to disagree (45 CFR 164.526). GDPR gives rights to rectification and to restriction while accuracy is checked (Articles 16 and 18).
**BR-E41** When the patient, or someone other than the health plan, has paid in full for the encounter's services and asks that they not be disclosed to the health plan, health_plan_restriction is set. Billing must not disclose the encounter to that plan for payment or operations unless the law requires it. The restriction is never refused.
  - Why: HIPAA requires a covered entity to agree to this restriction when the care was paid in full out of pocket and the disclosure is for payment or operations and not otherwise required by law (45 CFR 164.522(a)(1)(vi)).
**BR-E42** When a patient who requests a correction asks for the data to be restricted meanwhile, accuracy_contested is set. Everyone reading the encounter sees the contested part marked, and it is cleared when the request is decided.
  - Why: GDPR gives a right to restriction while the accuracy of personal data is checked (Article 18(1)(a)). Marking it, rather than hiding it, keeps the data available for care while warning everyone it is disputed.
**BR-E43** When Admission, discharge and transfer changes the attending practitioner, the previous attending participation ends and the new one starts at the effective time; a cancelled change ends the new participation and restores the previous one. A cancelled leave resumes the encounter and removes the time on hold; a cancelled return puts it back on hold. A bed swap is recorded as a transfer for each encounter.
  - Why: IHE PAM sends change attending doctor (A54, cancelled by A55), cancel leave and cancel return (A52, A53) and swap patients (A17). BR-E11 needs exactly one attending at a time, and length (BR-E16) excludes leave, so both must follow the cancels.
**BR-E44** Each correction request is kept and linked to the encounter it concerns, with the request, any denial, any statement of disagreement and any rebuttal. It is kept for as long as the encounter, and in the United States for at least 6 years from its decision.
  - Why: HIPAA requires the request, denial, disagreement and rebuttal to be appended or linked to the record (45 CFR 164.526(d)(4)) and kept for 6 years from creation or when last in effect (45 CFR 164.530(j)(2)).
**BR-E45** When Admission, discharge and transfer discharges a patient who is on leave, the encounter is discharged from on hold: the leave ends at the discharge time and the disposition and destination are recorded.
  - Why: A patient can die or decide not to return while on leave. Requiring the encounter to resume first would record care that never happened, and would leave length counting the leave (BR-E16).
**BR-E46** When Admission, discharge and transfer cancels an admission recorded in error, the encounter it started is marked entered in error with the reason admission_cancelled. When it clears an expected discharge, planned_end is cleared.
  - Why: FHIR uses cancelled only for encounters that never started, so a started encounter opened by mistake is entered in error. ADT cancels an admission only when no clinical data is recorded, which Encounters also requires. IHE PAM cancels a pending discharge with A25.
**BR-E47** An encounter recorded on the wrong patient is moved to the right patient by an HIM specialist, with a reason. It keeps its id and visit number; on a locked encounter the move is also recorded as an amendment. The move is refused if the right patient is not in use or would then have two inpatient or observation encounters open (BR-E21). An encounter that belongs to a stay moves only with its stay, through Admission, discharge and transfer.
  - Why: A visit with notes on the wrong person is a record overlay, which ONC SAFER Guide 6 counts among the most dangerous identification errors. Entered in error is refused once clinical data exists, so a move is the only fix. HL7 moves a visit between patients (A45). Clinical modules keep their entries on the encounter id, so they follow it.
**BR-E48** Two encounters of the same patient for one visit are merged by an HIM specialist, with a reason. The duplicate's appointments, locations, participants and diagnoses join the remaining encounter; the duplicate becomes entered in error with status_reason merged and merged_into set, and its visit number stays on the remaining encounter as an old identifier. encounter.merged tells clinical modules to move their entries. Encounters that belong to stays merge only when their stays merge.
  - Why: Double check-ins and patient merges create two encounters for one visit (BR-E29). HL7 merges visits (A42). Keeping the old visit number lets laboratory and billing messages sent with it still find the visit (BR-E34).

### State machine

Initial state: `planned`. States: `planned`, `in_progress`, `on_hold`, `discharged`, `completed`, `cancelled`, `discontinued`, `entered_in_error`.
Any transition not listed below is invalid.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| on_hold | in_progress | leave of absence ends | - |
| on_hold | discharged | discharge while on leave (BR-E45) | - |

### Workflows

#### Open at check-in
Actor: System
Trigger: Appointments reports appointment.checked_in

1. Create the encounter as planned, subject status arrived, with the appointment id, class and department
2. Report the encounter id to Appointments
3. Emit encounter.created

Outcome: The visit has a record before care starts, and the appointment points to it.

#### Start care
Actor: Clinician
Trigger: A practitioner begins seeing the patient

1. Set actual_start, status in progress and subject status receiving care
2. Add the practitioner as primary performer or attending
3. Report care to the Patients module
4. Emit encounter.started

Outcome: Waiting and care time are recorded separately.

#### Follow an emergency visit
Actor: System
Trigger: The Emergency department module reports arrival, triage and departure

1. Open the encounter in progress with subject status arrived
2. Set subject status triaged, then receiving care, as reported
3. On departure, discharge it, or discontinue it with the departure reason if care was not complete
4. If the patient is admitted, ADT opens an inpatient encounter; the emergency encounter is discharged

Outcome: Every emergency visit is one encounter, including patients who leave.

#### Pre-admit a stay
Actor: System
Trigger: Admission, discharge and transfer reports a pre-admission

1. Open a planned inpatient encounter with planned_start and the pre-admission id
2. On admission, start this encounter instead of opening another
3. On a cancelled pre-admission, cancel it

Outcome: Pre-admission testing and paperwork attach to the stay before it begins.

#### Follow a stay
Actor: System
Trigger: Admission, discharge and transfer reports an admission, transfer, leave or discharge

1. On admission, open the encounter in progress with the admission details and first location
2. On transfer, complete the previous location and add the new one
3. On leave, put the encounter on hold; on return, resume it
4. On a class change, add it to class_history
5. On discharge, set discharged with disposition and destination, and record_due_at

Outcome: The stay is one record from admission to discharge.

#### Complete the record
Actor: Health information management (HIM) specialist
Trigger: Clinical documentation reports the documentation complete

1. Check a final diagnosis is recorded and no part is open
2. Set completed and locked_at
3. Emit encounter.completed with the diagnoses and class history for Billing

Outcome: The record is complete and locked within the deadline.

#### Amend a locked encounter
Actor: Health information management (HIM) specialist
Trigger: A locked encounter needs a correction

1. Record the new value, the previous value, who, when and why
2. Emit encounter.amended

Outcome: The record is corrected and its history kept.

| status_reason | required to cancel, mark entered in error, move or merge; a code from cancel_reasons, or a service-set code | ENCOUNTER_REASON_REQUIRED |

### Edge cases

**EC-E1** A patient checks in at 09:00 and is seen at 10:10.
  - Required behaviour: The encounter is planned with subject status arrived from 09:00, and actual_start is 10:10.
  - Covered by: BR-E2, AC-E1
**EC-E2** A patient leaves the emergency department after triage without being seen.
  - Required behaviour: The encounter is discontinued with departure reason left without being seen. It is not deleted.
  - Covered by: BR-E9, AC-E7
**EC-E3** A patient under observation is admitted as an inpatient the next morning.
  - Required behaviour: The same encounter adds inpatient to class_history at the order time, and Billing receives encounter.class_changed.
  - Covered by: BR-E5, AC-E5
**EC-E4** A patient goes home for a weekend during a long stay.
  - Required behaviour: The encounter is on hold with subject status on leave, and the weekend is excluded from length.
  - Covered by: BR-E4, BR-E16, AC-E12
**EC-E5** A patient is discharged on 30 September with a diagnosis code that is retired on 1 October.
  - Required behaviour: The code is valid, because the discharge date decides (BR-E13).
  - Covered by: BR-E13, AC-E10
**EC-E6** A discharged encounter has no final diagnosis 25 days after discharge.
  - Required behaviour: It cannot be completed, and it is listed as due in 5 days; after 30 days it is overdue.
  - Covered by: BR-E7, AC-E8
**EC-E7** A coder finds a wrong discharge diagnosis after the encounter is locked.
  - Required behaviour: The HIM specialist amends it with a reason; the previous code is kept and encounter.amended is emitted.
  - Covered by: BR-E8, AC-E9
**EC-E8** A consultation encounter part of a stay is still open when the stay is completed.
  - Required behaviour: Completing the stay fails with ENCOUNTER_OPEN_PARTS until the consultation is closed.
  - Covered by: BR-E6, AC-E6
**EC-E9** An encounter is opened for the wrong patient and a nurse has already recorded vital signs.
  - Required behaviour: Marking it entered in error fails with ENCOUNTER_HAS_CLINICAL_DATA; the vital signs are moved first.
  - Covered by: BR-E10, AC-E11
**EC-E10** Two attending practitioners are added for the same hours of a stay.
  - Required behaviour: Rejected with ENCOUNTER_INVALID_PARTICIPANT; the first attending's period is ended first.
  - Covered by: BR-E11, AC-E13
**EC-E11** A patient is seen at the hospital's substance use disorder program.
  - Required behaviour: The encounter carries SUD sensitivity, and its notes and results inherit it.
  - Covered by: BR-E14, AC-E14
**EC-E12** The patient's record is merged into another while a stay is open.
  - Required behaviour: The stay moves to the remaining patient and keeps its id.
  - Covered by: BR-E17, AC-E15
**EC-E13** Admission, discharge and transfer cancels a discharge entered by mistake.
  - Required behaviour: The encounter returns from discharged to in progress, and record_due_at is cleared.
  - Covered by: BR-E4, AC-E16
**EC-E14** The Organization module is down when a transfer is reported.
  - Required behaviour: The request fails with ENCOUNTER_DEPENDENCY_UNAVAILABLE and ADT retries; no location is recorded unchecked.
  - Covered by: FR-E13, AC-E25
**EC-E15** A baby is born during the mother's stay.
  - Required behaviour: The baby's encounter is part of the mother's encounter, though the patients differ.
  - Covered by: BR-E6, AC-E31
**EC-E16** ADT reports an admission for a patient whose earlier stay is still in progress.
  - Required behaviour: Rejected with ENCOUNTER_CONCURRENT_STAY; the earlier stay is discharged first.
  - Covered by: BR-E21, AC-E32
**EC-E17** A patient dies during a stay.
  - Required behaviour: ADT discharges the encounter with disposition expired; the Patients module records the death. A later encounter for that patient fails with ENCOUNTER_PATIENT_DECEASED.
  - Covered by: BR-E22, AC-E33
**EC-E18** US only: A Medicare patient is placed in observation at 20:00.
  - Required behaviour: encounter.observation_started carries a notice due by 08:00 two days later; ADT gives the notice.
  - Covered by: BR-E23, AC-E34
**EC-E19** An elective hip replacement is pre-admitted a week ahead.
  - Required behaviour: A planned encounter holds the pre-admission tests, and admission starts it.
  - Covered by: BR-E24, AC-E35
**EC-E20** A patient moves from the medicine service to surgery.
  - Required behaviour: department_history records both, and encounter.department_changed is emitted.
  - Covered by: BR-E25, AC-E36
**EC-E21** The patient's records are under legal hold when retention ends.
  - Required behaviour: The encounters are kept.
  - Covered by: BR-E26, AC-E37
**EC-E22** A patient with a pre-admission for next week dies today.
  - Required behaviour: The planned encounter is cancelled with reason patient_deceased.
  - Covered by: BR-E28, AC-E40
**EC-E23** Two duplicate records each have a stay in progress, and they are merged.
  - Required behaviour: Both stays move and encounter.patient_review_needed is emitted for each; BR-E21 is not applied to the move.
  - Covered by: BR-E29, AC-E41
**EC-E24** The front desk undoes a wrong check-in.
  - Required behaviour: The planned encounter is cancelled and encounter.cancelled is emitted.
  - Covered by: BR-E30, AC-E42
**EC-E25** A record created by mistake has a pre-admission next week.
  - Required behaviour: When Patients marks it entered in error, the planned encounter is cancelled with reason patient_entered_in_error.
  - Covered by: BR-E31, AC-E43
**EC-E26** A death recorded for the wrong patient cancelled their pre-admission, and the death is retracted.
  - Required behaviour: The cancelled encounter is listed and encounter.patient_review_needed is emitted so it can be planned again.
  - Covered by: BR-E32, AC-E44
**EC-E27** A patient has a 09:00 blood draw and a 10:30 consultation in the same department.
  - Required behaviour: With visit_grouping per_department_day, the consultation check-in joins the morning's encounter and encounter.appointment_added is emitted.
  - Covered by: BR-E33, AC-E45
**EC-E28** An MRI order is carried out in an outpatient encounter.
  - Required behaviour: The encounter records the order, and Orders learns it was done from encounter.completed.
  - Covered by: BR-E35, AC-E47
**EC-E29** A patient is taken to the scanner for an hour.
  - Required behaviour: The ward bed becomes reserved and the scanner active; on return the bed is active again.
  - Covered by: BR-E12, AC-E50
**EC-E30** A transfer to ward 5 is entered for the wrong patient.
  - Required behaviour: ADT cancels it: ward 5 is removed and the previous ward is active again.
  - Covered by: BR-E37, AC-E49
**EC-E31** Discharge is expected tomorrow at 11:00.
  - Required behaviour: planned_end is 11:00 tomorrow; a cancelled pending discharge clears it.
  - Covered by: BR-E36, AC-E48
**EC-E32** An unconscious patient is admitted after an accident and their family calls.
  - Required behaviour: They can be listed if it fits their known wishes and best interest, and are asked as soon as they wake.
  - Covered by: BR-E38, AC-E52
**EC-E33** A patient reads their encounter notes in the portal the evening after a clinic visit.
  - Required behaviour: They see them at once; nothing waits for clinician review.
  - Covered by: BR-E39, AC-E53
**EC-E34** A patient pays cash for a sensitive test so their employer-sponsored plan won't learn of it.
  - Required behaviour: The restriction is set and Billing never sends that encounter to the plan.
  - Covered by: BR-E41, AC-E55
**EC-E35** A patient disputes a mental health diagnosis on a past visit and asks that it be restricted while it is checked.
  - Required behaviour: The diagnosis stays available for care but is marked as disputed everywhere until the HIM specialist decides.
  - Covered by: BR-E42, AC-E56
**EC-E36** A planned admission is cancelled because the surgery was postponed.
  - Required behaviour: status_reason records admission_cancelled from ADT; the reason for the encounter, the planned surgery, is kept unchanged.
  - Covered by: BR-E24, AC-E57
**EC-E37** ADT changes the attending at 07:00, then cancels the change because it was the wrong doctor.
  - Required behaviour: The wrong doctor's participation ends and the previous attending is restored, so there is never no attending or two.
  - Covered by: BR-E43, AC-E58
**EC-E38** A patient on weekend leave dies at home.
  - Required behaviour: ADT discharges the stay as expired and the encounter goes straight from on hold to discharged at that time.
  - Covered by: BR-E45, AC-E60
**EC-E39** A laboratory result arrives with the visit number of an encounter that was merged yesterday.
  - Required behaviour: The old visit number is found on the remaining encounter, so the result files to the right visit.
  - Covered by: BR-E48, AC-E63
**EC-E40** A clinic visit with a signed note was registered on the patient's twin.
  - Required behaviour: The encounter is moved to the right twin; the note follows the encounter, and the move is an amendment because the encounter was locked.
  - Covered by: BR-E47, AC-E62

## External interfaces

### API

#### Open an encounter: `POST /encounters`
Open an encounter from a check-in, walk-in, emergency arrival or admission. Accepts a retry key.
- Success: 201
- Actor: System
- Input: patient, class, department, and optional appointments, orders, pre-admission id, episodes, part_of, planned start, type, priority, reasons, admission details, location, virtual service and patient location
- Result: the encounter
- Errors: ENCOUNTER_INVALID_PATIENT, ENCOUNTER_INVALID_CODE, ENCOUNTER_INVALID_DEPARTMENT, ENCOUNTER_INVALID_REFERENCE, ENCOUNTER_INVALID_PARENT, ENCOUNTER_INVALID_PERIOD, ENCOUNTER_INVALID_LOCATION, ENCOUNTER_INVALID_VIRTUAL, ENCOUNTER_READ_ONLY_FIELD, ENCOUNTER_IDEMPOTENCY_KEY_REUSED, ENCOUNTER_CONCURRENT_STAY, ENCOUNTER_PATIENT_DECEASED, ENCOUNTER_DEPENDENCY_UNAVAILABLE

#### Get an encounter: `GET /encounters/{id}`
Read an encounter. A restricted patient's encounter needs a reason.
- Success: 200
- Actor: Clinician
- Input: encounter id; a reason where required
- Result: the encounter, as JSON or a FHIR Encounter
- Errors: ENCOUNTER_NOT_FOUND, ENCOUNTER_NOT_AUTHORISED_FOR_PATIENT, ENCOUNTER_REASON_REQUIRED, ENCOUNTER_DEPENDENCY_UNAVAILABLE

#### List encounters: `GET /encounters`
Encounters by patient, department, practitioner, status and date.
- Success: 200
- Actor: Clinician
- Input: filters; limit and cursor
- Result: encounters in time order
- Errors: ENCOUNTER_INVALID_QUERY, ENCOUNTER_DEPENDENCY_UNAVAILABLE

#### Update an encounter: `PATCH /encounters/{id}`
Change type, priority, reasons, episodes, department or virtual details of an open encounter. A department change is added to department_history.
- Success: 200
- Actor: Registrar
- Input: encounter id, revision, the fields to change
- Result: the encounter
- Errors: ENCOUNTER_NOT_FOUND, ENCOUNTER_LOCKED, ENCOUNTER_REVISION_CONFLICT, ENCOUNTER_INVALID_CODE, ENCOUNTER_INVALID_DEPARTMENT, ENCOUNTER_INVALID_VIRTUAL, ENCOUNTER_READ_ONLY_FIELD, ENCOUNTER_DEPENDENCY_UNAVAILABLE

#### Start an encounter: `POST /encounters/{id}/start`
Record that care began.
- Success: 200
- Actor: Clinician
- Input: encounter id, revision, start time
- Result: the encounter
- Errors: ENCOUNTER_NOT_FOUND, ENCOUNTER_INVALID_TRANSITION, ENCOUNTER_INVALID_PERIOD, ENCOUNTER_REVISION_CONFLICT, ENCOUNTER_CONCURRENT_STAY, ENCOUNTER_PATIENT_DECEASED, ENCOUNTER_DEPENDENCY_UNAVAILABLE

#### Record subject status: `POST /encounters/{id}/subject-status`

- Actor: System
- Input: encounter id, revision, subject status
- Result: the encounter
- Errors: ENCOUNTER_NOT_FOUND, ENCOUNTER_INVALID_CODE, ENCOUNTER_LOCKED, ENCOUNTER_REVISION_CONFLICT, ENCOUNTER_DEPENDENCY_UNAVAILABLE

#### Change participants: `POST /encounters/{id}/participants`
Add a participant, or end one's period.
- Success: 200
- Actor: Clinician
- Input: encounter id, revision, practitioner, role, start or end

- Errors: ENCOUNTER_NOT_FOUND, ENCOUNTER_INVALID_PARTICIPANT, ENCOUNTER_LOCKED, ENCOUNTER_REVISION_CONFLICT, ENCOUNTER_DEPENDENCY_UNAVAILABLE

Add, rank or remove diagnoses.
- Success: 200
- Actor: Clinician
- Input: encounter id, revision, diagnoses with role and rank
- Result: the encounter

- Success: 200

Complete and lock an encounter whose record is complete.

### API conventions

| Topic | Convention | Reference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Authentication | Every request carries the caller's identity from Identity. Permissions are checked against it (Permission matrix). | - |
| Errors | Errors use problem details (application/problem+json) with a code member, such as ENCOUNTER_LOCKED. | RFC 9457 |
| Pagination | Lists are cursor-based: limit and an optional cursor in, next_cursor out. | - |
| Concurrency | Every change sends the revision it read. A stale revision fails with ENCOUNTER_REVISION_CONFLICT (BR-E20). | - |
| Retries | Every change accepts an Idempotency-Key header; a retry returns the original result (FR-E12). | IETF draft-ietf-httpapi-idempotency-key-header |
| Times | Instants are RFC 3339 timestamps in UTC (CON-E3). | RFC 3339 |
| FHIR | GET /encounters/{id} returns an HL7 FHIR R5 Encounter when the request asks for application/fhir+json (FR-E11). | - |
| Versioning | Additive changes don't change the version. A breaking change ships as a new major version with Deprecation and Sunset headers. | RFC 9745, RFC 8594 |

### Event delivery

**DG-E1** Events are published at least once; consumers drop one with a source and id they have seen.
  - Why: A relay can publish twice after a crash. CloudEvents defines source plus id as the duplicate check.
**DG-E2** Events for one encounter can arrive out of order; each carries the revision, and consumers ignore a lower one.
  - Why: Brokers and retries don't guarantee order.
**DG-E3** Events carry ids, codes and changed field names, never diagnoses text, notes or the patient's details. Consumers read the encounter.
  - Why: Events fan out widely. This follows the minimum necessary standard (45 CFR 164.502(b)) and keeps sensitive encounters out of logs.
**DG-E4** Every event carries a unique id, its type, the encounter it is about, when the change happened, and the revision.
  - Why: Consumers order and deduplicate by these, and need the time of the change rather than the time they received it. CloudEvents defines id, type and time for every event.

any: every appointment. own: appointments the actor takes part in. none: not allowed.

- Get or list encounters: own: encounters of patients in their care. Restricted patients need a reason (BR-E18). Patients and their representatives read their own encounters, except parts withheld under BR-E39.

- Record subject status, a location, a class change, hold, discharge or discontinue: Usually reported by Appointments, the Emergency department module or ADT.

- Report clinical data: Only modules that record clinical data.

- Restrict disclosure to the health plan: Recorded at the patient's request.

- Withhold part of an encounter: Only a licensed professional caring for the patient.

**CMP-E25** GDPR (EU) 2016/679, Article 6(1): EU only: Processing health data needs a lawful basis under Article 6 as well as an exception under Article 9.

**CMP-E26** GDPR (EU) 2016/679, Articles 44 to 49: EU only: Transfers of personal data outside the EU need an adequacy decision or appropriate safeguards.
  - How it's met: Where data is stored and who processes it is a deployment choice.
  - Status: deployment

### Quality attributes

| Category | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| compatibility | Encounters map onto HL7 FHIR R5 Encounter: status to status, subject_status to subjectStatus, class to class, admission to admission, and class_history to EncounterHistory. |
| compatibility | Events map to HL7 v2 ADT messages for systems that use them: an ambulatory or emergency encounter.created to A04, a pre-admission to A05 and its cancellation to A38, admission to A01, a location change to A02, discharge to A03, class changes to A06 and A07, leave to A21 and A22, a cancelled discharge to A13. Pending admit, transfer and discharge map to A14, A15 and A16, and their cancels to A27, A26 and A25. |
| security | Only people involved in the patient's care, or with a role that needs it, can read an encounter, and every read is logged. |
| reliability | An encounter change is acknowledged only after it is committed together with its events. |
| availability | Opening an encounter never waits on Billing, Consent or Clinical documentation. |
| portability | No requirement depends on a particular database, language or framework. |

### Design constraints

**CON-E1** Encounter data is encrypted in transit and at rest.
  - Why: GDPR Article 32 lists encryption as a security measure, and HIPAA treats it as an addressable safeguard (45 CFR 164.312).
**CON-E2** Coded fields use the code systems named in the data model, and local codes are mapped to them before data leaves this module.
  - Why: Billing, reporting and exchange all assume these code systems.
**CON-E3** Times are RFC 3339 instants in UTC.
  - Why: Stays cross daylight saving changes; instants keep length correct.

### Implementation notes

Non-normative: one way to build what the requirements describe. Nothing in the requirements depends on it.

#### TN-E1: Pick the code release by date

Load each fiscal year's ICD-10-CM release with its effective dates, and validate a diagnosis against the release in effect on the discharge date for inpatient encounters, or the encounter date otherwise.

#### TN-E2: Make event consumers idempotent

Key encounter creation on the source event (appointment id, emergency arrival id or admission id) as well as the Idempotency-Key, so a replayed event from Appointments or ADT returns the existing encounter.

## Supporting information

### Concepts

| Concept | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Appointment, then encounter | The appointment is the plan. When the patient checks in, the appointment is fulfilled and linked to the encounter opened for it (FHIR). |
| Status is not the patient's position | status tracks the encounter; subject_status tracks the patient, such as arrived or triaged. A patient can arrive an hour before the encounter starts. |

### Glossary

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Encounter | An interaction between a patient and healthcare providers to provide care or assess health (HL7 FHIR). An appointment records what is planned; an encounter records what happened. |
| Encounter class | The setting: ambulatory (AMB), virtual (VR), emergency (EMER), observation (OBSENC) or inpatient (IMP). |
| Subject status | Where the patient is in the encounter, such as arrived, triaged or receiving care, separate from the encounter's own status. |
| Attending | The practitioner responsible for the patient's care during the encounter (HL7 participation ATND). |
| Record completion | The point where the encounter's documentation and final diagnosis are complete, after which it is locked. |
| Amendment | A change to a locked encounter, recorded with who made it, when and why, keeping what it replaced. |
| Part 2 program | A federally assisted substance use disorder program in the United States, whose records have extra protection (42 CFR Part 2). |

### Decisions

**ADR-E1 (2026-10-07)** Encounters are an EHR-only module with no core or healthcare page.
  - Why: An encounter is a clinical-record concept, and the EHR is the only domain specified. If another domain needs encounters, its common rules move up to a healthcare page.
**ADR-E2 (2026-10-07)** A class change keeps the same encounter, with class history.
  - Why: FHIR R5 keeps class history in EncounterHistory, and one record keeps notes and results together across the change.
  - Considered: A new encounter per class. Rejected because it splits one stay's care across records.
**ADR-E3 (2026-10-07)** One encounter per visit or stay, with part_of for consultations and sub-stays where a hospital wants them.
  - Why: FHIR notes aggregation varies between organisations and countries. A single stay record with optional parts fits both practices.
**ADR-E4 (2026-10-07)** Admission and discharge happen in ADT; this module records them.
  - Why: Bed management and orders are a different job. The encounter is the clinical record of the stay.
**ADR-E5 (2026-10-08)** When an ambulatory encounter opens is a setting (encounter_opens_at).
  - Why: FHIR's Appointment and Encounter pages describe different moments, and hospitals differ. A setting keeps both correct.

### Risks

**RISK-E1** Records stay incomplete past the deadline.
  - Impact: Survey findings under the Medicare conditions of participation, and claims that cannot be coded.
  - Mitigation: record_due_at, the records-due list and encounter.record_overdue (BR-E7, PERF-E1).
**RISK-E2** A retried check-in opens a second encounter.
  - Impact: Care split across two records.
  - Mitigation: Idempotency keys (FR-E12, PERF-E4).
**RISK-E3** Sensitive encounter data is disclosed without the required consent.
  - Impact: A Part 2 violation and harm to the patient.
  - Mitigation: Sensitivity codes propagate to every attached entry (BR-E14), and Consent decides disclosure.
**RISK-E4** A locked record is changed silently.
  - Impact: The legal record and claims no longer match.
  - Mitigation: Locking and amendments with history (BR-E8).

Every note, order and result in the record belongs to an encounter. This
module keeps that record of each visit and stay: when it happened, who was
responsible, where the patient was, why they came and what was found.

## The appointment is the plan, the encounter is what happened [#the-appointment-is-the-plan-the-encounter-is-what-happened]

Checking in opens the encounter, but arrival is not care. The encounter waits,
planned, until a practitioner starts ([BR-E2](/docs/healthcare/ehr/encounter/functions/business-rules#br-e2)), so the record shows both how long
the patient waited and how long they were seen.

## A stay is one record, whatever it becomes [#a-stay-is-one-record-whatever-it-becomes]

A patient under observation who is admitted keeps the same encounter, with the
time of each class recorded ([BR-E5](/docs/healthcare/ehr/encounter/functions/business-rules#br-e5)). Transfers, leave and discharge come from
Admission, discharge and transfer and are recorded as they happen ([BR-E4](/docs/healthcare/ehr/encounter/functions/business-rules#br-e4)).

## Discharged is not done [#discharged-is-not-done]

Care can be over while the record isn't. An encounter stays discharged until
its documentation and final diagnosis are complete, within the deadline the
law sets: 30 days for US hospitals ([BR-E7](/docs/healthcare/ehr/encounter/functions/business-rules#br-e7)). Then it locks, and later changes
are amendments that keep what they replaced ([BR-E8](/docs/healthcare/ehr/encounter/functions/business-rules#br-e8)).

## Codes are checked against the right day [#codes-are-checked-against-the-right-day]

ICD-10-CM changes every 1 October. A stay's diagnoses are checked against the
release in effect on the discharge date, so a stay that crosses the change
isn't rejected for a code that was valid when it ended ([BR-E13](/docs/healthcare/ehr/encounter/functions/business-rules#br-e13)).
