title: Appointments
description: 'Hospital appointments for outpatients and patients in a hospital stay: departments, rooms and equipment, referrals, walk-ins, overbooking, linked appointments, and the link to the patient''s encounter.'
module: appointment
version: 0.9.0
status: draft
industry: healthcare
domain: ehr
extends: healthcare/appointment
variants:
- id: outpatient
  title: Outpatient appointments
  description: 'Appointments for patients who come in for care, or join remotely, and are not in a hospital stay: clinic visits, follow-ups, day procedures, imaging and virtual visits.'
  summary: An outpatient receives care without being formally admitted (Medicare; HL7 encounter class AMB). A virtual appointment is outpatient care held remotely (HL7 class VR). Either one opens its own encounter at check-in, and an outpatient can arrive as a walk-in.
- id: inpatient
  title: Inpatient appointments
  description: 'Appointments for patients during a hospital stay, admitted or under observation: imaging, therapy sessions and other services.'
  summary: An inpatient is formally admitted with a doctor's order (HL7 class IMP). A patient under observation is still an outpatient for Medicare, even in a hospital bed, but is in a stay of their own (HL7 class OBSENC). Either way, their appointments belong to the stay, need an active stay to be booked, and are reviewed when the stay ends. Booking the admission itself belongs to the Admission, discharge and transfer module.
purpose: 'Add what a hospital EHR needs for scheduling: outpatient and virtual visits, services booked during a hospital stay, departments, rooms and equipment, referral rules, walk-ins, overbooking, appointments that must happen in order, and the link between each appointment and the patient''s encounter.'
scope:
- Outpatient and virtual appointments, and appointments for patients in a hospital stay (inpatient or observation)
- Departments (clinics), rooms and equipment as part of a booking
- Practitioners booked only into departments they work in
- Referral-required departments
- Walk-in outpatients
- Overbooking practitioners where a department allows it
- Appointments that must happen in order, such as a blood test before a consultation
- Showing the prior authorization status Authorization reports
- Linking each appointment to the right encounter
- 'Reviewing appointments whose assumptions changed: the stay ended, an earlier linked appointment was cancelled, or authorization was denied'
- A department's daily worklist
nonGoals:
- Admissions, beds, transfers and discharge, including planned admissions and pre-admission (HL7 class PRENC). They belong to the Admission, discharge and transfer module.
- The encounter itself. The Encounter module owns it; this layer only links to it.
- Operating theatre lists, surgical case scheduling and theatre time. They belong to the Theatre module.
- Emergency department arrivals, triage and treatment (HL7 class EMER). They belong to the Emergency department module. An emergency patient is never registered as a walk-in here.
- 'Waiting lists: who is waiting, in what order, and who is offered a freed slot. They belong to the Waitlist module, which books through this one.'
- Requesting prior authorization from payers and tracking the decision. It belongs to the Authorization module; this layer only shows the status it reports.
- Which practitioners, rooms and equipment belong to a department. The Organization module owns them.
actors:
- name: Clinic manager
  description: Sets a department's scheduling rules, such as overbooking and referral requirements.
assumptions:
- id: AS-E1
  text: The Encounter module opens an encounter when it receives appointment.checked_in, and reports its id back.
  rationale: FHIR links an encounter to the appointment that scheduled it (Encounter.appointment). The encounter is the clinical record of the visit.
- id: AS-E2
  text: A Referral service can confirm that a referral is valid for a department.
  rationale: Some departments only accept referred patients (BR-E3).
- id: AS-E3
  text: Departments, rooms and equipment are owned by the Organization module. This module only refers to them by id.
  rationale: Opening a clinic or renaming a room is not a scheduling decision.
- id: AS-E4
  text: The Admission, discharge and transfer module knows whether a patient is in an active stay, inpatient or observation, and announces when the stay ends.
  rationale: Admitting and discharging patients is its job; this module only needs the answer and the event.
- id: AS-E5
  text: The Organization module knows which practitioners hold a role in a department on a given date, and which rooms and equipment belong to it.
  rationale: FHIR models a practitioner's roles, locations and services at an organization for a period of time as PractitionerRole. Staffing a department is not a scheduling decision.
- id: AS-E6
  text: Where payers require prior authorization, an Authorization module knows whether a service needs it for this patient and reports its status.
  rationale: Which services need authorization depends on the payer and the patient's coverage, and changes often. Only the module that talks to payers can know it.

businessRules:
- id: BR-4
  text: A patient cannot have two active appointments whose slots overlap. A practitioner can, only when their department has allow_overbooking on, and with no more than max_overbooked_per_slot appointments overlapping one slot. Slots are half-open.
  rationale: No-show rates differ by clinic and specialty, and a 2023 systematic review found the effect of overbooking on no-shows uncertain. So each department decides, and it is off by default. A patient, though, can only be in one place.
- id: BR-E1
  text: A room, or an item of equipment, cannot hold two active appointments whose slots overlap.
  rationale: Two patients cannot be examined in one room, or scanned with one portable machine, at once. The core Appointment specification leaves resources to the layers that need them (ADR-11), and FHIR books both as appointment participants.
- id: BR-E2
  text: For an outpatient or virtual appointment, check-in opens an encounter and the appointment records its encounter_id. A checked-in appointment of either kind completes only once its encounter exists.
  rationale: In FHIR, the appointment is fulfilled and linked to the newly created encounter at check-in. The encounter is where care is documented.
  variant: outpatient
- id: BR-E3
  text: A department with referral_required on accepts a booking only with a valid referral.
  rationale: Many health plans pay for specialist care only with a referral from the primary care doctor (HealthCare.gov), and 31 of 38 OECD countries have GP gatekeeping, 24 of them mandatory. Checking before the slot is taken avoids a visit the clinic will not be paid for, or must turn away on the day.
- id: BR-E4
  text: An outpatient walk-in gets an appointment that starts now, marked walk_in, and is checked in straight away. Emergency arrivals are not walk-ins; they go to the Emergency department module.
  rationale: Every visit needs an appointment to link its encounter and keep statistics complete.
  variant: outpatient
- id: BR-E5
  text: 'An appointment for a patient in a hospital stay is observation or inpatient, matching the stay, and belongs to it: it records the stay''s encounter_id at booking, and check-in opens no new encounter.'
  rationale: HL7 separates inpatient (IMP) and observation (OBSENC) encounters from ambulatory ones (AMB). Care given during a stay, such as an MRI, is documented on that stay. Medicare counts observation as outpatient care, so billing must not infer admission from where the appointment belongs.
  variant: inpatient
- id: BR-E6
  text: An observation or inpatient appointment can be booked only while the patient has an active stay of that kind.
  rationale: Without a stay, there is no encounter to belong to, and the patient may not be in the building.
  variant: inpatient
- id: BR-E7
  text: When a stay ends, each of the patient's observation and inpatient appointments still in the future is flagged needs_review with review_reason stay_ended. A Scheduler then cancels it, or changes it to outpatient.
  rationale: These appointments assumed the patient was on a ward. Some are still needed after the stay, such as follow-up imaging, so neither cancelling nor keeping them silently is safe.
  variant: inpatient
- id: BR-E8
  text: Every practitioner on an appointment holds a role in its department on the appointment date.
  rationale: A practitioner's privileges and schedule are per department and per period (FHIR PractitionerRole). Booking them into a clinic they do not work in fills a slot nobody will staff.
- id: BR-E9
  text: An appointment's room and equipment belong to its department.
  rationale: A department schedules only what it controls. Booking another department's room takes it without that department knowing.
- id: BR-E10
  text: An appointment with previous_appointment_id is for the same patient as that appointment, and starts no earlier than it ends. A booking or reschedule that would break the order is rejected, whichever appointment moves.
  rationale: 'Some care must happen in order: the blood test before the consultation that reviews it, or imaging before the surgeon sees the patient. FHIR links such appointments with previousAppointment. Checking on every move stops a reschedule from silently reversing them.'
- id: BR-E11
  text: When an appointment is cancelled or marked no_show, every active appointment whose previous_appointment_id names it is flagged needs_review with review_reason previous_cancelled. Nothing is cancelled automatically.
  rationale: The later appointment may now be pointless, as with a consultation with no results to review, or still be needed. A person decides.
- id: BR-E12
  text: Booking never waits for prior authorization. The appointment records the authorization_status and authorization_id the Authorization module reports, and the department worklist shows them. When the status becomes denied, the appointment is flagged needs_review with review_reason authorization_denied.
  rationale: 'Payer decisions take time: from January 1, 2026, CMS requires impacted payers to decide within 72 hours for expedited and 7 calendar days for standard requests (CMS-0057-F). Holding the slot while that runs would lose it, and a denial does not always mean the care should not happen, so a person decides.'
- id: BR-E13
  text: needs_review clears when the appointment is rescheduled, cancelled or changed to outpatient, or when a Scheduler marks it reviewed.
  rationale: A flag nobody can clear stops meaning anything. Each of these is a decision about the appointment.
- id: BR-E14
  text: A virtual appointment has care_setting virtual, and an appointment with care_setting virtual has virtual_service.
  rationale: HL7 classes remote care as VR, not AMB. Keeping the setting and the connection details in step keeps encounters and statistics right.
- id: BR-E15
  text: When the encounter for a checked-in appointment starts, the appointment completes. When that encounter is discontinued or cancelled before care, the appointment is cancelled with the reason left_without_being_seen or encounter_cancelled.
  rationale: FHIR marks an appointment fulfilled and linked to its encounter; once the encounter decides what happened, the appointment follows. Without this, checked-in appointments never close and worklists fill with old visits.
- id: BR-E16
  text: Undoing a check-in (the Clinical appointments rule) tells Encounters to cancel the planned encounter opened for it. It is refused once that encounter has started.
  rationale: The wrong visit's encounter must not stay on record, and once care has started the check-in was real.
- id: BR-E17
  text: An appointment in a department Organization marks as sensitive, such as a substance use disorder program, carries that department's sensitivity codes. It is shown only to the department's staff and the patient, and its messages name neither the department nor the service.
  rationale: Records that identify someone as a patient of a US federally assisted substance use disorder program are protected by 42 CFR Part 2, compliance date February 16, 2026. An appointment at that program identifies the patient as much as the encounter does.
- id: BR-E18
  text: After a patient merge, appointments that now overlap are flagged needs_review with review_reason patient_merged.
  rationale: Duplicate records often hold the same visit booked twice. A Scheduler decides which booking is real.
- id: BR-E19
  text: In the EHR, charges for care come from encounters. Appointments give Billing only no-shows and the late cancellation flag, for missed-appointment fees where the jurisdiction allows them.
  rationale: The encounter is what was done and is coded; the appointment is the plan. Charging from both would bill one visit twice.
- id: BR-E20
  text: When the encounter opened at check-in is marked entered in error, a checked-in appointment that has not ended returns to booked, as an undone check-in, so it can be checked in correctly. One that has ended is flagged needs_review with review_reason encounter_entered_in_error.
  rationale: The encounter was opened by mistake, often for the wrong patient. Leaving the appointment checked in would keep it out of every worklist, and only a person can tell what happened at a visit that has already ended.
- id: BR-E21
  text: An appointment booked to carry out an order records it, and Orders learns when the appointment is booked, rescheduled, cancelled or completed.
  rationale: IHE Scheduled Workflow books imaging and procedures against the order that asked for them, and FHIR records it as Appointment.basedOn. Without the link an order can sit unscheduled with nobody noticing.
- id: BR-E22
  text: Booking never waits for financial clearance. The appointment records the status Coverage reports, and the department worklist shows it, so staff can verify insurance and explain costs before the day.
  rationale: HFMA patient access practice verifies insurance and estimates the patient's cost before scheduled services. Holding the slot for it would lose it; showing it lets staff act in time.
- id: BR-E23
  text: An appointment is pre-registered when the patient has confirmed their details with the Patients module and Coverage reports financial clearance, both after booking. Check-in for a pre-registered appointment only verifies identity.
  rationale: HFMA calls registration before the service the best way to speed up access. Asking again at the desk what the patient already gave wastes their time and the front desk's.
- id: BR-E24
  text: 'The cancellation reasons this layer sets map to HL7 appointment cancellation reasons for exchange: left_without_being_seen to pat, because the patient left, and encounter_cancelled to other.'
  rationale: BR-H22 requires every service-set reason to map to one HL7 code. HL7 has no code for leaving before being seen, and an encounter cancelled before care has no single cause, so these are the closest codes that don't misstate who cancelled.
- id: BR-E25
  text: When a patient in a stay starts a leave of absence, each of their observation and inpatient appointments that starts before the expected return is flagged needs_review with review_reason patient_on_leave. If the leave is cancelled, the flag is removed from those appointments; a Scheduler decides the rest.
  rationale: A patient on leave isn't on the ward. Without a review, staff go to an empty bed or record a no-show for a patient the hospital let go home for the weekend. IHE PAM sends leave (A21) and its cancel (A52).
  variant: inpatient

workflows:
- name: Register a walk-in
  trigger: A patient arrives without an appointment
  actor: Front desk
  steps:
  - Verify the patient with two identifiers
  - Book an appointment starting now in the department, marked walk_in
  - Check the patient in, which opens an encounter
  - Emit appointment.booked and appointment.checked_in
  outcome: The patient is in the queue like any other, with an encounter to document the visit.
  variant: outpatient
- name: Link the encounter
  trigger: The Encounter module reports the encounter it opened
  actor: System
  steps:
  - Record encounter_id and increase revision
  - Emit appointment.encounter_linked
  outcome: The appointment and its encounter point to each other.
- name: Book during a stay
  trigger: A ward or department books a service for a patient in a stay, such as an MRI
  actor: Scheduler
  steps:
  - Ask Admission, discharge and transfer for the patient's active stay and its kind
  - Book the appointment as observation or inpatient, with the stay's encounter_id
  - Apply the usual overlap, room, equipment and overbooking rules
  - Emit appointment.booked
  outcome: The appointment is part of the patient's stay.
  variant: inpatient
- name: Review when a stay ends
  trigger: Admission, discharge and transfer announces that a stay ended
  actor: System
  steps:
  - Find the patient's observation and inpatient appointments still in the future
  - Flag each one needs_review with review_reason stay_ended, and increase its revision
  - Emit appointment.review_needed for each
  outcome: A Scheduler decides each one, and none is lost or kept by accident.
  variant: inpatient
- name: Review a linked appointment
  trigger: An appointment is cancelled or marked no_show
  actor: System
  steps:
  - Find the active appointments whose previous_appointment_id names it
  - Flag each one needs_review with review_reason previous_cancelled, and increase its revision
  - Emit appointment.review_needed for each
  outcome: The next step in the sequence is looked at by a person, not left waiting on care that will not happen.
- name: Record prior authorization
  trigger: Authorization reports a status for an appointment
  actor: System
  steps:
  - Record authorization_status and authorization_id, and increase revision
  - If the status is denied, flag needs_review with review_reason authorization_denied and emit appointment.review_needed
  - Emit appointment.authorization_updated
  outcome: The worklist shows which appointments still need authorization before the day.

Appointments build on the
[clinical appointment rules](/docs/healthcare/appointment), which build on the
[core Appointment specification](/docs/core/appointment). This layer adds how a
hospital schedules care, for outpatients and for patients in a hospital stay.

## Where the patient is decides which encounter the appointment belongs to

An outpatient comes in for the appointment and leaves, and a virtual patient
joins from elsewhere. Each check-in opens a new encounter (BR-E2). A patient in
a hospital stay already has one encounter for the whole stay, so an MRI booked
for them belongs to that stay and opens nothing new (BR-E5).

## Observation is a stay, but not an admission

A patient under observation can spend the night in a hospital bed and still be
an outpatient for Medicare. Their appointments belong to the observation stay,
like an inpatient's, but keep their own care setting, so nothing downstream
treats them as admitted (ADR-E7).

## A stay ending doesn't silently cancel what was booked

When a patient goes home with an appointment from their stay still ahead, such
as physiotherapy next week, the appointment is flagged for review (BR-E7). A
Scheduler then changes it to outpatient or cancels it. Booking the admission
itself belongs to the Admission, discharge and transfer module.

## A department books only what it controls

A practitioner is booked only into a department they work in on that date
(BR-E8), and the room and equipment must belong to the department (BR-E9).
Two patients can't share a room or a portable scanner at the same time
(BR-E1).

## A clinic can overbook a doctor, never a patient

No-show rates differ by clinic, and the evidence on overbooking is uncertain.
So each department decides whether to overbook and sets its own limit, with no
default (BR-4). A patient can still only be in one place.

## Care that must happen in order stays in order

A blood test before the consultation that reviews it is a chain, not two
separate bookings. Neither can be moved out of order (BR-E10), and if the first
is cancelled or missed, the second is flagged rather than kept or cancelled
blindly (BR-E11).

## Prior authorization never holds a slot hostage

Payers can take days to decide, so booking never waits. The worklist shows the
status Authorization reports, and a denial flags the appointment for a person
to decide (BR-E12).
