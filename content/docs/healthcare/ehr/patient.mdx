- Registering unidentified patients under an alias, and identifying them later
- Registering newborns with distinct names, linked to their mother
- The data printed on identification bands
- Quick registration with minimal data, completed later
- Registering a patient under a privacy alias

- Emergency department arrival, triage and treatment. They belong to the Emergency department module, which registers patients through this one.
- Admissions and bed assignment. They belong to the Admission, discharge and transfer module.
- Printing bands and labels. A printing service prints what this module returns.
- Birth registration with civil authorities. This module records the newborn as a patient only.

assumptions:
- id: AS-E1
  text: The hospital has one MRN system for each scope set in mrn_scope, and this module is the only one that assigns numbers in it.
  rationale: Two systems assigning MRNs from the same range would give two patients one number.
glossary:
- term: Medical record number (MRN)
  definition: The number a hospital assigns to identify a patient's record. One of the identifiers The Joint Commission lists for patient identification.
- term: Unidentified patient
  definition: A patient whose identity is unknown on arrival, such as an unconscious trauma patient, registered under an alias.
- term: Alias
  definition: A temporary name given to an unidentified patient, from the hospital's alias scheme, such as a phonetic word and a number.
- term: Identification band
  definition: The wristband a patient wears in hospital, showing identifiers and a barcode.
- term: Quick registration
  definition: Registering with the minimum data so care can start, and completing the details later.

- name: unidentified
  type: boolean

  description: Set while the patient's identity is unknown and they are registered under an alias.

  type: string

businessRules:
- id: BR-H1
  text: Before a new patient is registered, existing records are searched with at least the name and birth date. If the search finds a probable match, the registrar either picks that record or registers a new one with a reason. Unidentified patients and babies born in the hospital are registered without a search.
  rationale: HL7 FHIR notes that around 2% of registrations are in error, mostly duplicate records. An unidentified patient has nothing to search with, and a baby born here cannot have a record yet. Both are checked later (BR-E3).
- id: BR-H4
  text: A record has at least one usual or official name, a birth date and an administrative gender. The birth date can be partial or estimated, and the gender can be unknown, when that is all anyone knows. An unidentified patient has a temp alias as their only name and an estimated birth year.
  rationale: Name and birth date are what every search and identity check rely on. An unidentified patient still needs both to be told apart, so the alias and an estimate stand in for them.
- id: BR-E1
  text: Every patient gets an MRN from mrn_system at registration. An MRN is never reused, including after a merge, where it stays on the target marked old.
  rationale: The Joint Commission lists an assigned identification number, such as the MRN, as a patient identifier (NPG.01.01.01). Reusing one would make old documents and labels point at the wrong person.
- id: BR-E2
  text: An unidentified patient is registered with an alias from alias_scheme and marked unidentified. An alias is never given to two records.
  rationale: Placeholder names such as John Doe cause duplicates and mix-ups. A UK trauma centre found a phonetic alias system reduced duplication, but staff confused similar-sounding aliases, so aliases must be unique.
- id: BR-E3
  text: When an unidentified patient is identified, the hospital searches for an existing record. If one is found, the alias record is merged into it. If not, the real details are added, the alias is kept as an old name, and unidentified is cleared. Either way patient.identified is emitted.
  rationale: Care given under the alias must stay with the person. The same study found record merging after identification was done inconsistently, so it is one defined step.
- id: BR-E4
  text: A baby born in the hospital is registered with a distinct name from newborn_naming, such as "Smith, Judy Girl", with a letter for each baby in a multiple birth, its birth order, born_at, and mother_patient_id. When the baby's name is given, it becomes official and the newborn name is kept as old.
  rationale: The Joint Commission requires distinct identification methods for newborns, giving "Smith, Judy Girl A" and "Smith, Judy Girl B" as examples (NPG.01.01.01 EP 3). Babies cannot say who they are and often share names such as Baby Boy Smith.
- id: BR-E5
  text: An identification band shows at least two person-specific identifiers, such as name, birth date and MRN, and a barcode that encodes them. It never shows a room or bed.
  rationale: The Joint Commission requires at least two identifiers and never the room number, and lists barcoding that includes two or more identifiers (NPG.01.01.01 EP 1). The band is what staff check before each test and treatment.
- id: BR-E6
  text: A patient who needs care at once can be registered with only what BR-H4 requires, as registration_status incomplete. Incomplete registrations are listed for staff to complete. Insurance and payment questions are never asked before the patient is seen.
  rationale: In the United States, a hospital may not delay a medical screening examination or treatment to ask about payment or insurance, and registration must not discourage patients from staying (42 CFR 489.24(d)(4)).
- id: BR-E7
  text: A patient can be registered under a privacy alias, recorded as a name with use anonymous, with confidentiality restricted or very restricted. Lists, boards and bands show the alias; the official name is shown only when the record is opened with a reason.
  rationale: FHIR defines the anonymous name use to protect a person's identity for privacy reasons. Public figures and people at risk of harm are found through ward boards and lists, not only through records.
validations:
- field: mother_patient_id
  rule: an existing patient in use
  error: PATIENT_INVALID_MOTHER
- field: born_at

workflows:
- name: Register an unidentified patient
  trigger: A patient arrives who cannot say who they are and has no identification
  actor: Emergency department staff
  steps:
  - Take the next unused alias from alias_scheme
  - Record the alias, an estimated birth year and the administrative gender if known, as unidentified
  - Assign an MRN and print the band
  - Emit patient.registered
  outcome: Care starts at once, under a name nobody else has.
- name: Identify a patient
  trigger: Staff learn who an unidentified patient is
  actor: Registrar
  steps:
  - Search with the real name and birth date
  - If a record exists, merge the alias record into it
  - Otherwise add the real details, keep the alias as an old name, and clear unidentified
  - Emit patient.identified, and reprint the band
  outcome: The patient's care since arrival is on their one record.
- name: Register a newborn
  trigger: A baby is born in the hospital
  actor: Maternity staff
  steps:
  - Build the name from newborn_naming, with a letter for each baby in a multiple birth
  - Record born_at, birth order, administrative gender and mother_patient_id
  - Assign an MRN and print the band
  - Emit patient.registered
  outcome: Each baby has a distinct name and band from birth.
- name: Complete a quick registration
  trigger: A patient registered as incomplete can now give their details
  actor: Registrar
  steps:
  - Search for an existing record with the details given
  - If one exists, merge the quick record into it
  - Otherwise add the missing details and set registration_status complete
  outcome: No quick registration is left as a duplicate.

Patients build on the [patient record rules](/docs/healthcare/patient)
every healthcare application shares. This layer adds what happens at a
hospital's front door, in its emergency department and on its maternity ward.

## A patient nobody can name still gets a unique one

An unconscious patient gets an alias nobody else has had (BR-E2), so care can
start at once without mixing them up with the patient in the next bay. Once
someone knows who they are, the alias record joins their real one (BR-E3).

## Babies are told apart from birth

Newborns can't say who they are and often share a surname. Each one gets a
distinct name built from the mother's name, with a letter for twins and
triplets, and a link to the mother (BR-E4).

## Registration never stands between a patient and care

A patient who needs care now is registered with the minimum, and the rest is
completed later (BR-E6). Nothing about payment is asked first.

## The band is what staff check

Every test and treatment starts by checking the band against two identifiers,
so the band carries two of them and a barcode, and never the bed (BR-E5).
