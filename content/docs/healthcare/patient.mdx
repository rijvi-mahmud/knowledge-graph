---
title: Patient records
description: 'Who the patient is, kept as one trustworthy record: registration, demographics, identifiers, duplicates and merges, death, personal representatives and the patient''s privacy rights.'
module: patient
version: 0.1.0
status: draft
industry: healthcare
purpose: 'Keep one accurate record of who each patient is, so every clinical and administrative module attaches its data to the right person: register patients without creating duplicates, keep demographics and identifiers with their history, find and resolve duplicate records, record death, record who may act for the patient, and honour the patient''s rights over their data. Works in the United States and the European Union.'
scope:
- Searching for a patient, and registering one only when no existing record matches
- Names, birth date, administrative gender, addresses, phone numbers and email, with their history
- Identifiers, such as medical record numbers and national health identifiers
- Optional demographics a deployment chooses to collect, such as race and ethnicity or gender identity
- Contacts, including emergency contacts
- Personal representatives and proxies, and whether one may act for the patient
- Confidential communication preferences
- Finding duplicate records, merging them, and undoing a wrong merge
- Recording and retracting death
- Restricted records, such as for staff or public figures
- Requests to correct, restrict or erase patient data
- A log of who viewed or changed each record
nonGoals:
- Clinical facts about the patient, such as allergies, problems, sex for clinical use or care plans. Clinical modules own them.
- Insurance and coverage. They belong to the Coverage module, which refers to the patient by id.
- Consent to treatment, to sharing and to research, and EU opt-outs and access restrictions on health data. They belong to the Consent module.
- Logins, passwords and patient portal accounts. They belong to Identity, which links an account to a patient id.
- Printing documents and sending messages. Other modules ask this one where to send them.
- Biometric identification, such as palm or face scans.
- Exchanging records with other organisations or national services. An Exchange module does that, using the patient id and identifiers.
actors:
- name: Registrar
  description: Staff who register patients and keep their details up to date, such as front desk or admissions staff.
- name: Health information management (HIM) specialist
  description: Resolves duplicate records, merges and unmerges them, and decides correction requests.
- name: Clinician
  description: Reads patient details to provide care.
- name: Patient
  description: The person the record is about.
- name: Personal representative
  description: 'A person allowed to act for the patient: a parent, guardian or other legal representative, or a proxy the patient chose.'
- name: Privacy officer
  description: Reviews who accessed patient records, and handles requests about patient data.
- name: System
  description: Another module, or this one acting on its own, such as when it proposes duplicates.
assumptions:
- id: AS-H1
  text: Identity authenticates every caller and says which permissions they hold.
  rationale: Who someone is as a user is a different question from who a patient is. Keeping them apart lets a patient exist with no account.
- id: AS-H2
  text: 'The deployment has a lawful basis to process health data for care: treatment under HIPAA in the United States, and GDPR Article 9(2)(h) in the European Union.'
  rationale: Without it, no patient record could be held at all. The legal basis is the deployment's responsibility, not this module's.
- id: AS-H3
  text: The jurisdiction, record_retention, identifier_systems and collected_fields settings are set with legal advice before go-live.
  rationale: Retention, national identifiers and which demographics may be collected differ by US state and EU member state.
- id: AS-H4
  text: Modules that record care tell this one when they do, so it knows when a patient was last cared for.
  rationale: Retention runs from the last care (BR-H18), and a record with care on it must be merged, not discarded (BR-H11).
glossary:
- term: Patient record
  definition: The one record of who a patient is. Clinical data lives in other modules and points to it by id.
- term: Duplicate record
  definition: A second record for a person who already has one. Around 2% of registrations are in error, mostly duplicates (HL7 FHIR).
- term: Merge
  definition: Retiring a duplicate (the source) in favour of the record to keep (the target). The source stays, marked merged, and points to the target.
- term: Unmerge
  definition: Undoing a merge that joined two different people.
- term: Identifier
  definition: A number assigned to the patient by an organisation or government, such as a medical record number or a national health identifier, with the system that issued it.
- term: Administrative gender
  definition: The gender used for administration and record keeping (FHIR Patient.gender). It has no clinical meaning.
- term: Personal representative
  definition: Someone the law treats as the patient for their health information, such as a parent or guardian (45 CFR 164.502(g)), or a proxy the patient authorised (EHDS Article 4(2)).
- term: Restricted record
  definition: A record that needs extra protection, such as a staff member's or a public figure's (HL7 confidentiality R or V).
- term: Statement of disagreement
  definition: A patient's written statement disagreeing with a denied correction. In the United States it is kept with the record (45 CFR 164.526(d)).
concepts:
- name: One record per person
  description: Every other module refers to a patient only by id. When two records turn out to be the same person, the merge tells every module to point at the one that remains.
- name: History, not overwrite
  description: Names, addresses, phone numbers and identifiers change. The old values are kept and marked old, because documents and other systems still use them.
dataModel:
- name: id
  type: string
  required: true
  description: The record id. It never changes and is never reused (BR-H2).
- name: status
  type: enum
  required: true
  description: Whether the record is in use.
  constraints: active | inactive | merged | entered_in_error
- name: identifiers
  type: identifier[]
  required: false
  description: Each with system, value, type (such as MRN or national health identifier), use (usual, official, temp, secondary or old) and period (FHIR Identifier).
  constraints: system must be in identifier_systems; system plus value unique among records in use (BR-H3)
- name: names
  type: name[]
  required: true
  description: Each with use (usual, official, temp, nickname, anonymous, old or maiden), family name, given names, prefix, suffix and period (FHIR HumanName).
  constraints: at least one usual or official name, or a temp name (BR-H4)
- name: birth_date
  type: date
  required: true
  description: Full date, or year and month, or year only when only that is known.
  constraints: not in the future
- name: birth_date_estimated
  type: boolean
  required: false
  description: Whether birth_date is an estimate, such as from the patient's stated age.
- name: administrative_gender
  type: enum
  required: true
  description: For administration and record keeping (FHIR Patient.gender).
  constraints: male | female | other | unknown
- name: addresses
  type: address[]
  required: false
  description: Each with use (home, work, temp, old or billing), lines, city, district, state, postal code, country and period.
- name: telecoms
  type: contact point[]
  required: false
  description: Phone numbers and email addresses, each with use (home, work, mobile, temp or old), rank and period.
- name: languages
  type: language[]
  required: false
  description: BCP 47 tags, one marked preferred.
- name: interpreter_needed
  type: boolean
  required: false
  description: Whether the patient needs an interpreter (USCDI v6).
- name: multiple_birth
  type: boolean or integer
  required: false
  description: Whether the patient is one of twins, triplets and so on, or their birth order (FHIR Patient.multipleBirth).
- name: deceased
  type: boolean
  required: false
  description: Whether the patient has died (BR-H12).
- name: deceased_at
  type: date or instant
  required: false
  description: When the patient died, as precisely as known.
  constraints: not before birth_date
- name: death_source
  type: string
  required: false
  description: Who reported the death, such as a pronouncement in this hospital or a family member.
  constraints: required with deceased
- name: contacts
  type: contact[]
  required: false

- name: representatives
  type: representative[]
  required: false

- name: confidential_communication
  type: contact point or address
  required: false
  description: Where the patient asked to be contacted instead of their usual details (BR-H16).
- name: general_practitioner
  type: string
  required: false
  description: The patient's nominated primary care practitioner or practice, owned by the Organization module (FHIR Patient.generalPractitioner).
- name: marital_status
  type: code
  required: false
  description: Collected only when listed in collected_fields.
- name: race_ethnicity
  type: code[]
  required: false

- name: gender_identity
  type: code
  required: false
  description: The gender the patient identifies with, or declined. Collected only when listed in collected_fields.
- name: pronouns
  type: code
  required: false
  description: How the patient asks to be referred to. Collected only when listed in collected_fields.
- name: occupation
  type: text
  required: false
  description: Current job and industry (USCDI). Collected only when listed in collected_fields.
- name: confidentiality
  type: enum
  required: true
  description: How much protection the record needs (HL7 confidentiality codes N, R and V).
  constraints: normal | restricted | very_restricted; default normal
- name: accuracy_contested
  type: boolean
  required: false
  description: Set while the patient contests the accuracy of their data and asked for restriction (BR-H17).
- name: replaced_by
  type: string
  required: false

  constraints: set only when status = merged
- name: last_care_at
  type: instant
  required: false

- name: revision

  required: true

businessRules:
- id: BR-H1
  text: Before a new patient is registered, existing records are searched with at least the name and birth date. If the search finds a probable match, the registrar either picks that record or registers a new one with a reason.
  rationale: HL7 FHIR notes that around 2% of registrations are in error, mostly duplicate records. A duplicate splits one person's history in two, so allergies and results on one are missed on the other.
- id: BR-H2
  text: A record's id never changes and is never reused. Numbers people use, such as medical record numbers, are identifiers on the record, not its id.
  rationale: Other modules hold the id for years. FHIR warns that medical record numbers change, for example when duplicates are merged, so they must not be the id.
- id: BR-H3
  text: An identifier's system and value belong to at most one record that is active or inactive.
  rationale: Two people with the same medical record number means a scan or a lookup can open the wrong person's record.
- id: BR-H4
  text: A record has at least one usual or official name, a birth date and an administrative gender. The birth date can be partial or estimated, and the gender can be unknown, when that is all anyone knows.
  rationale: Name and birth date are what every search and every two-identifier check rely on (BR-H1). Allowing partial and estimated values records what is known instead of inventing it.
- id: BR-H5
  text: A change to a name, address, phone number, email or identifier keeps the old value, marked old, with the period it was in use. Nothing is overwritten.
  rationale: USCDI includes previous name and previous address because documents and other systems still use them. Matching a referral letter in a maiden name needs the old name.
- id: BR-H6
  text: Fields listed in collected_fields are optional for the patient. Race and ethnicity and gender identity are recorded as the patient reports them, with declined as an answer, and are never inferred by staff.
  rationale: OMB SPD 15 says race and ethnicity data should be self-reported wherever possible, and allows several categories. USCDI v3.1 removed sexual orientation and gender identity in 2025 while EU law treats racial or ethnic origin as special category data (GDPR Article 9), so each deployment decides what it collects.
- id: BR-H7
  text: 'A merge retires the source record in favour of the target: the source becomes merged and points to the target with replaced_by, its identifiers are copied to the target and marked old, and patient.merged tells every module to move its data to the target. A merge always records who did it, why, and how the two records differed.'
  rationale: This is the FHIR $merge behaviour. Keeping the source, rather than deleting it, lets anyone holding the old id find the right record, and makes a wrong merge reversible.
- id: BR-H8
  text: Two records are merged only by an HIM specialist. Matching proposes duplicates; it never merges on its own.
  rationale: A wrong merge puts two people's care in one record, which is a worse failure than a duplicate. A person confirms every merge.
- id: BR-H9
  text: Reading a merged record returns it with status merged and replaced_by, following the chain to the record in use. A change to a merged record is rejected.
  rationale: FHIR notes the record pointed to may itself have been merged later. Writing to a retired record would put new data where nobody looks.
- id: BR-H10
  text: An unmerge restores the source record to active with the identifiers it had, and emits patient.unmerged. Data recorded on the target since the merge stays there, and every module is told to review it.
  rationale: A wrong merge mixes two people's care. Only the people who recorded care since the merge can tell whose it is, so nothing is moved back automatically.
- id: BR-H11
  text: A record can be marked entered in error only while no care has been recorded for it (last_care_at unset). Once care exists, the record is merged instead.
  rationale: FHIR marks a record created in error as not in use. If care was recorded, discarding the record would hide that care; merging keeps it with the right person.
- id: BR-H12
  text: Recording a death sets deceased, deceased_at and death_source, and emits patient.deceased. The record stays active. A death recorded in error can be retracted only by an HIM specialist, with a reason, which emits patient.death_retracted.
  rationale: FHIR notes a deceased patient's record may stay in use for some time, for results, billing and family. A wrong death record stops care and messages, so retracting it is a controlled correction.
- id: BR-H13
  text: 'A deceased patient''s record keeps the same protection as a living patient''s for as long as the law requires: 50 years after death in the United States.'
  rationale: HIPAA protects a deceased person's health information for 50 years after death (45 CFR 164.502(f)). GDPR does not apply to the dead, but member states may set rules (Recital 27), and medical confidentiality continues.
- id: BR-H14
  text: A representative is recorded with their kind (parent, guardian, other legal representative, or a proxy the patient chose), the scope of their authority, any exclusions, a period, and the evidence seen. They may act for the patient only within that scope and period, and not for an excluded service.
  rationale: HIPAA treats a personal representative as the patient (45 CFR 164.502(g)), and the EHDS lets patients authorise proxies for a limited period and purpose (Article 4(2)). Recording the scope makes every module's question "may this person act?" answerable the same way.
- id: BR-H15
  text: Staff can exclude services or episodes from a representative's authority where the law lets the patient act alone, or where treating someone as the representative could endanger the patient. The exclusion is not shown to that representative.
  rationale: Under HIPAA a parent is not the representative for care a minor could consent to themselves (164.502(g)(3)), and a provider may refuse to treat someone as the representative where there is abuse or danger (164.502(g)(5)). Which care qualifies is set by state or member state law, so staff record it.
- id: BR-H16
  text: A patient can ask to be contacted by other means or at another address, without giving a reason. Every module that contacts the patient asks this one where to.
  rationale: US providers must accommodate reasonable requests for confidential communications and may not ask why (45 CFR 164.522(b)). For some patients an ordinary letter home could be unsafe.
- id: BR-H17
  text: A patient can ask for their data to be corrected. The request is decided within correction_deadline, extendable once by correction_extension with a written reason. An accepted correction is made and linked to the request. A denied one gives the reason in writing, and the patient can add a statement of disagreement, which stays with the record. While the patient contests accuracy, they can ask for the data to be restricted, which sets accuracy_contested.
  rationale: HIPAA gives 60 days with one 30-day extension, and requires a written denial and a right to disagree (45 CFR 164.526). GDPR gives a right to rectification (Article 16) answered within one month, extendable by two (Article 12(3)), and to restriction while accuracy is checked (Article 18(1)(a)). The EHDS adds online requests (Article 6).
- id: BR-H18
  text: A request to erase a patient record is refused while record_retention applies, counted from last_care_at or the date of death. When it ends, identifying fields are removed and the id is kept.
  rationale: HIPAA gives no right to erasure, and US hospitals must keep records for at least 5 years (42 CFR 482.24(b)(1)), longer in many states. GDPR Article 17(3)(c) lets health data be kept for care. Keeping the id keeps every other module's references valid.
- id: BR-H19
  text: Every view and every change of a patient record is logged with who, when, what, and the reason given. Patients can get a report of who accessed their record where the jurisdiction gives that right.
  rationale: HIPAA requires audit controls (45 CFR 164.312(b)). The EHDS gives EU patients information on every access to their health data, kept for at least three years (Article 9).
- id: BR-H20
  text: Opening a restricted or very restricted record needs a reason, which is logged. Every opening of a very restricted record is reported to the privacy officer with patient.restricted_access.
  rationale: Staff and public figures' records are the ones colleagues are tempted to look at. A stated reason and a report deter snooping without stopping care.
- id: BR-H21
  text: Every change sends the revision it read, and a stale revision is rejected.
  rationale: Registrars and HIM specialists edit the same record at once, such as during a merge. Without this, one silently undoes the other.
- id: BR-H22
  text: Search and matching compare every name, address, phone number and identifier the patient has had, including old ones.
  rationale: A patient returning after years, or a referral in a former name, must still find the existing record (BR-H1).
- id: BR-H23
  text: National identifiers and social security numbers are shown in full only to roles that need them, and otherwise masked.
  rationale: GDPR lets member states set conditions for national identification numbers (Article 87), and HIPAA limits use to the minimum necessary (45 CFR 164.502(b)). These numbers enable identity theft.

workflows:
- name: Register a patient
  trigger: A person needs care and may not have a record
  actor: Registrar
  steps:
  - Search with name and birth date, plus any identifier, phone or address the patient gives
  - If a record matches, use it and update what has changed
  - Otherwise register a new record, with a reason if the search found a probable match
  - Emit patient.registered
  - Propose duplicates for review if matching finds any after saving
  outcome: The patient has exactly one record, or a possible duplicate is waiting for review.
- name: Review a possible duplicate
  trigger: Matching proposes two records that may be the same person
  actor: Health information management (HIM) specialist
  steps:
  - Compare the two records side by side, including old names and identifiers
  - 'Decide: the same person, or not'
  - If the same, preview the merge, then merge the newer or less complete record into the other
  - If not, mark the pair not a duplicate so it is not proposed again
  outcome: The pair is resolved, and the decision is recorded.
- name: Merge two records
  trigger: An HIM specialist confirms a duplicate
  actor: Health information management (HIM) specialist
  steps:
  - Check the revisions and that both records are in use
  - Mark the source merged with replaced_by, and copy its identifiers to the target marked old
  - Record who merged, why and how the records differed
  - Emit patient.merged; each module moves its data to the target
  outcome: One record remains, and anyone holding the old id is pointed to it.
- name: Undo a wrong merge
  trigger: An HIM specialist finds a merge joined two different people
  actor: Health information management (HIM) specialist
  steps:
  - Restore the source to active with its identifiers, and remove them from the target
  - Emit patient.unmerged
  - Each module reviews data recorded on the target since the merge and moves what belongs to the source
  outcome: Two records again, with care recorded since the merge checked by the people who recorded it.
- name: Record a death
  trigger: A patient dies in hospital, or a death is reported
  actor: Registrar
  steps:
  - Record deceased_at as precisely as known, and death_source
  - Emit patient.deceased
  - Other modules stop reminders and review future plans
  outcome: Nobody is contacted or booked as if the patient were alive.
- name: Decide a correction request
  trigger: A patient asks for their data to be corrected
  actor: Health information management (HIM) specialist
  steps:
  - Record the request and its due date from correction_deadline
  - If the patient asks, set accuracy_contested while it is checked
  - 'Accept: make the change, link it to the request, and tell the patient'
  - 'Deny: give the reason in writing, and record any statement of disagreement'
  - Clear accuracy_contested
  outcome: Every request is answered in time, and a disagreement stays with the record.
edgeCases:
- id: EC-H1
  situation: Twins born the same day share a family name and birth date, and their mother registers both.
  behaviour: The search proposes the first twin when registering the second. The registrar registers a new record with the reason "twin", and multiple_birth records the birth order.
  covers:
  - BR-H1
  - AC-H1
- id: EC-H2
  situation: A patient registered under her maiden name returns married, with a new family name.
  behaviour: The search finds her through the old name. The new name is added as official and the maiden name is kept with use maiden.
  covers:
  - BR-H5
  - BR-H22
  - AC-H4
- id: EC-H3
  situation: A registrar types the birth date with day and month swapped.
  behaviour: The search may not find the record. If a duplicate is created, matching later proposes the pair, and an HIM specialist merges them.
  covers:
  - BR-H1
  - BR-H8
  - AC-H6
- id: EC-H4
  situation: A patient knows only their age, not their birth date.
  behaviour: birth_date is set to the estimated year, with birth_date_estimated true.
  covers:
  - BR-H4
  - AC-H3
- id: EC-H5

  behaviour: The record is registered with no address. Addresses are optional.
  covers:
  - BR-H4
  - AC-H3
- id: EC-H6

  covers:
  - BR-H21

- id: EC-H7

  covers:
  - BR-H3
  - AC-H2

  - BR-H7

  situation: Record A was merged into B, and B was later merged into C.
  behaviour: Reading A returns replaced_by C, following the chain.
  covers:
  - BR-H9
  - AC-H8
- id: EC-H10
  situation: A merge turns out to have joined two different people, and a lab result was recorded on the target since.
  behaviour: The unmerge restores the source. The result stays on the target and the lab module is told to review it.
  covers:
  - BR-H10
  - AC-H9
- id: EC-H11
  situation: A record created by mistake already has a completed appointment.
  behaviour: Marking it entered in error fails with PATIENT_HAS_CARE. It is merged into the right record instead.
  covers:
  - BR-H11
  - AC-H10
- id: EC-H12
  situation: A death is recorded for the wrong patient.
  behaviour: An HIM specialist retracts it with a reason, and patient.death_retracted lets other modules restore reminders and plans.
  covers:
  - BR-H12
  - AC-H11
- id: EC-H13
  situation: Only the year of death is known, from a family member.
  behaviour: deceased_at holds the year, with death_source family.
  covers:
  - BR-H12
  - AC-H11
- id: EC-H14
  situation: A parent asks about an appointment their 16-year-old consented to alone, where state law allows it.
  behaviour: The service is excluded from the parent's authority, so the answer to "may this person act?" is no.
  covers:
  - BR-H15
  - AC-H13
- id: EC-H15
  situation: A proxy's authorisation ended last month.
  behaviour: They can no longer act for the patient. The answer to "may this person act?" is no.
  covers:
  - BR-H14
  - AC-H12
- id: EC-H16
  situation: A patient asks for erasure of their record in the European Union.
  behaviour: Refused with PATIENT_RETENTION_REQUIRED while record_retention applies, with the reason given.
  covers:
  - BR-H18
  - AC-H15
- id: EC-H17

constraints:
- id: CON-H1
  text: Patient data is encrypted in transit and at rest.
  rationale: GDPR Article 32 lists encryption as a security measure, and HIPAA treats it as an addressable safeguard (45 CFR 164.312).
- id: CON-H2
  text: The access log cannot be changed or deleted by the people it records.
  rationale: A log its subjects can edit cannot show who accessed what.
- id: CON-H3
  text: Language tags follow BCP 47, and dates follow ISO 8601, allowing year or year and month alone.
  rationale: FHIR uses both, and a partial birth date must not be padded into a false full date.

apiConventions:
- topic: Authentication
  text: Every request carries the caller's identity from Identity. Permissions are checked against it (Permission matrix).
- topic: Errors
  text: Errors use problem details with the media type application/problem+json, with a code member carrying the error code, such as PATIENT_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER.
  reference: RFC 9457
- topic: Pagination
  text: 'Lists are cursor-based: a request sends limit and an optional cursor, and the response returns next_cursor until there are no more results.'
- topic: Concurrency
  text: Every change sends the revision it read. A stale revision fails with PATIENT_REVISION_CONFLICT (BR-H21).
- topic: Retries
  text: Every change accepts an Idempotency-Key header. A retry with the same key returns the original result (FR-H1).
  reference: IETF draft-ietf-httpapi-idempotency-key-header
- topic: Rate limits
  text: A caller over its limit gets 429 Too Many Requests with a Retry-After header.
  reference: RFC 6585, RFC 9110
- topic: Dates
  text: Dates are ISO 8601 and may be partial (year, or year and month). Instants are RFC 3339 timestamps in UTC (CON-H3).
  reference: RFC 3339
- topic: FHIR
  text: GET /patients/{id} returns an HL7 FHIR R5 Patient resource when the request asks for application/fhir+json (FR-H12).
- topic: Personal data in URLs
  text: Names, birth dates and identifiers are sent in request bodies or query parameters over TLS, never in paths, and are never written to request logs.
- topic: Versioning
  text: Additive changes don't change the version. A breaking change ships as a new major version, announced with Deprecation and Sunset headers.
  reference: RFC 9745, RFC 8594

deliveryGuarantees:
- id: DG-H1
  text: Events are published at least once. Consumers treat an event with a source and id they have already seen as a duplicate.
  rationale: A relay can publish twice if it crashes after publishing. CloudEvents defines source plus id as the duplicate check.
- id: DG-H2
  text: Events for one patient can arrive out of order. Each carries the revision, and consumers ignore one lower than a revision they have applied.
  rationale: Brokers and retries don't guarantee order. The revision orders every change to a record.
- id: DG-H3
  text: Events carry ids and the names of changed fields, never names, birth dates, addresses or identifiers. Consumers read the record for details.
  rationale: Events fan out to many modules and logs. Keeping demographics out of them follows the minimum necessary standard (45 CFR 164.502(b)) and GDPR data minimisation (Article 5(1)(c)).
- id: DG-H4
  text: Every event carries a unique id, its type, the patient it is about and when the change happened.
  rationale: The id lets consumers drop duplicates (DG-H1). CloudEvents requires id, type and source on every event.

Every clinical module refers to a patient by id. This module keeps that id
pointing at one person, and keeps who that person is accurate over the years.

## One person, one record

A duplicate record splits a patient's history: an allergy on one record isn't
seen on the other. So registration starts with a search (BR-H1), and search
looks at old names and addresses as well as current ones (BR-H22). When a
duplicate still gets through, matching proposes it and an HIM specialist
decides (BR-H8).

## A merge can be undone

Merging keeps the retired record and points it at the one that remains
(BR-H7), so old ids still lead somewhere. If the merge joined two different
people, it can be undone, and every module is told to check what was recorded
since (BR-H10).

## Nothing about a person is overwritten

Names, addresses and identifiers change. The old values stay, marked old,
because referral letters, insurers and other hospitals still use them (BR-H5).

## Death is recorded, not assumed

A death needs a date and a source, and a wrong one can be retracted (BR-H12).
The record stays in use, and stays protected for as long as the law requires
(BR-H13).

## Who may act for the patient is answered in one place

Parents, guardians and proxies are recorded with what they may do and until
when (BR-H14). Where the law lets a patient act alone, such as a minor
consenting to their own care, staff exclude that care from the parent's
authority (BR-H15). Every module asks this one, so the answer is the same
everywhere.
