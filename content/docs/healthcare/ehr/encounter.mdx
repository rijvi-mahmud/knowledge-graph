---
title: Encounters
description: 'The record of each visit or hospital stay: when it started and ended, who cared for the patient and where, its diagnoses, and when its record is complete.'
module: encounter
version: 0.4.0
status: draft
industry: healthcare
domain: ehr
variants:
- id: ambulatory
  title: Ambulatory encounters
  description: Clinic visits, day procedures and virtual visits, where the patient is not in a hospital stay.
  summary: An ambulatory encounter (HL7 class AMB, or VR when virtual) is opened when Appointments reports a check-in or a walk-in. It starts when a practitioner begins care, not when the patient arrives, and ends when the visit ends.
- id: emergency
  title: Emergency encounters
  description: Care in the emergency department, from arrival until the patient leaves, is admitted or is transferred.
  summary: 'An emergency encounter (HL7 class EMER) is opened when the Emergency department module registers an arrival. Its subject status follows the patient: arrived, triaged, receiving care, departed. A patient who leaves before being seen is recorded, not deleted.'
- id: inpatient
  title: Inpatient encounters
  description: A hospital stay, admitted or under observation, from admission to discharge.
  summary: An inpatient encounter (HL7 class IMP, or OBSENC under observation) is opened when the Admission, discharge and transfer module admits the patient. It records the admission, every location and attending practitioner during the stay, and the discharge, and stays open until the record is complete.
purpose: 'Keep the record of each interaction between a patient and the hospital: its class, status and period, the practitioners and locations involved, the reasons and diagnoses, and whether its record is complete. Every clinical module attaches its data to an encounter by id.'
scope:
- Opening encounters from check-ins, emergency arrivals and admissions
- 'The encounter lifecycle: planned, in progress, on hold, discharged, completed, and cancelled, discontinued or entered in error'
- The class of an encounter, and changes to it, such as observation to inpatient
- Participants, such as the attending practitioner, with their periods
- Locations during the encounter, with their periods
- Reasons for the encounter and its diagnoses, with their role and rank
- Encounters grouped under another, such as consultations during a stay
- Record completion and locking, and amendments after locking
- Sensitive encounters, such as at a substance use disorder program
- A department's and a practitioner's open and incomplete encounters
- 'Pre-admission: planned stays before the patient arrives'
- The start of observation, for the US notice deadline
- Links to episodes of care
nonGoals:
- Booking. The Appointments module schedules visits; an encounter records what actually happened.
- Admission orders, beds, transfers and discharge orders. The Admission, discharge and transfer module owns them and reports them here.
- Triage and emergency department flow. The Emergency department module owns them and reports arrivals and triage here.
- Clinical notes, assessments, the history and physical, and discharge summaries. Clinical documentation owns them, attached to the encounter by id.
- Conditions and the problem list. The Conditions module owns them; an encounter refers to them, or carries a coded diagnosis.
- Coding for claims, diagnosis-related groups and charges. Billing owns them, using the diagnoses and class history this module gives.
- Consent to disclose sensitive records. The Consent module decides; this module flags the encounter.
- Episodes of care, such as a course of chemotherapy or a pregnancy. An Episodes module owns them; an encounter only links to them.
actors:
- name: Clinician
  description: A practitioner who cares for the patient during the encounter, such as the attending.
- name: Registrar
  description: Staff who open and correct encounters at check-in and admission.
- name: Health information management (HIM) specialist
  description: Tracks record completion, and corrects or amends locked encounters.
- name: Privacy officer
  description: Reviews who accessed encounters.
- name: System
  description: Another module, such as Appointments or Admission, discharge and transfer, reporting an event.
assumptions:
- id: AS-E1
  text: Appointments, the Emergency department module and Admission, discharge and transfer report check-ins, arrivals, admissions, transfers and discharges as they happen.
  rationale: An encounter records what happened. The modules where it happens tell this one.
- id: AS-E2
  text: Clinical documentation reports when an encounter's documentation is complete.
  rationale: Only the module holding the notes knows whether the record is complete (BR-E7).
- id: AS-E3
  text: The jurisdiction, record_completion_deadline and diagnosis_code_system settings are set with legal advice before go-live.
  rationale: Completion deadlines and code systems differ by US state, by payer and by EU member state.
glossary:
- term: Encounter
  definition: An interaction between a patient and healthcare providers to provide care or assess health (HL7 FHIR). An appointment records what is planned; an encounter records what happened.
- term: Encounter class
  definition: 'The setting: ambulatory (AMB), virtual (VR), emergency (EMER), observation (OBSENC) or inpatient (IMP).'
- term: Subject status
  definition: Where the patient is in the encounter, such as arrived, triaged or receiving care, separate from the encounter's own status.
- term: Attending
  definition: The practitioner responsible for the patient's care during the encounter (HL7 participation ATND).
- term: Record completion
  definition: The point where the encounter's documentation and final diagnosis are complete, after which it is locked.
- term: Amendment
  definition: A change to a locked encounter, recorded with who made it, when and why, keeping what it replaced.
- term: Part 2 program
  definition: A federally assisted substance use disorder program in the United States, whose records have extra protection (42 CFR Part 2).
concepts:
- name: Appointment, then encounter
  description: The appointment is the plan. When the patient checks in, the appointment is fulfilled and linked to the encounter opened for it (FHIR).
- name: Status is not the patient's position
  description: status tracks the encounter; subject_status tracks the patient, such as arrived or triaged. A patient can arrive an hour before the encounter starts.

businessRules:
- id: BR-E1
  text: Every encounter has one patient in use, one class and one responsible department.
  rationale: Every clinical entry is found through its encounter. An encounter without a patient or department is care nobody can find or bill.
- id: BR-E2
  text: 'An ambulatory or virtual encounter is opened when Appointments reports a check-in or a walk-in, at the moment encounter_opens_at sets: at check-in, as planned with subject status arrived, or when a practitioner starts care, in progress. It records the appointment and reports its own id back.'
  rationale: 'FHIR is not consistent on this: the Appointment page says the encounter "is typically created when the service starts, not when the patient arrives", and the Encounter page lets it start as arrived. Practice varies, so it is a setting; either way waiting time and care time stay separate.'
  variant: ambulatory
- id: BR-E3
  text: An emergency encounter is opened, in progress with subject status arrived, when the Emergency department module registers an arrival. Its subject status follows triage, care and departure as that module reports them.
  rationale: In an emergency department the patient's position changes fast and matters for safety and for billing; FHIR tracks it as subject status.
  variant: emergency
- id: BR-E4
  text: An inpatient or observation encounter is opened, in progress, when Admission, discharge and transfer reports an admission. Transfers add locations and close the previous one; leave puts the encounter on hold with subject status on leave; discharge sets discharged with the disposition and destination.
  rationale: The admission, transfers and discharge happen in that module; the encounter records them so the stay is one record from admission to discharge (FHIR admission component).
  variant: inpatient
- id: BR-E5
  text: A change of class, such as observation to inpatient, keeps the same encounter and adds the new class to class_history with the time it took effect.
  rationale: Medicare counts observation as outpatient care until an inpatient order, so the time in each class matters to billing; FHIR keeps class history (EncounterHistory).
  variant: inpatient
- id: BR-E6
  text: An encounter part of another has the same patient, except that a newborn's encounter can be part of its mother's encounter. A part's period is within its parent's, and the parent cannot be completed while a part is still open.
  rationale: FHIR groups encounters with partOf, such as a consultation during a stay, and says a child's encounter should reference the mother's encounter with partOf. A parent closed over an open part leaves care outside any complete record.
- id: BR-E7
  text: A discharged encounter becomes completed only when Clinical documentation reports its documentation complete and a final diagnosis is recorded. It must happen within record_completion_deadline of actual_end; encounters past that are listed as overdue.
  rationale: US hospitals must record the final diagnosis and complete the medical record within 30 days after discharge (42 CFR 482.24(c)(4)(viii)). FHIR uses discharged for care that is over while the record is still being completed.
- id: BR-E8
  text: A completed encounter is locked. Any later change is an amendment that records who, when, why and the previous value, and emits encounter.amended.
  rationale: Hospitals must keep record entries complete and protect their integrity (42 CFR 482.24(b)). Silent changes after completion would change a record others have relied on, including claims.
- id: BR-E9
  text: A planned encounter that never starts is cancelled. An encounter that started but ended early, such as the patient leaving against advice or without being seen, is discontinued with a departure reason.
  rationale: FHIR separates encounters that never happened from those cut short. A patient who left is part of the record and of quality reporting, so nothing is deleted.
- id: BR-E10
  text: An encounter can be marked entered in error only while no clinical data has been recorded against it (has_clinical_data unset). Otherwise its data is moved to the right encounter first.
  rationale: Discarding an encounter that holds notes or results would hide that care. Each clinical module moves its own entries.
- id: BR-E11
  text: An inpatient or observation encounter has exactly one attending practitioner at any time. Every participant holds a role in the department on the date (Organization).
  rationale: Someone must be responsible for an admitted patient at every moment. A practitioner without a role there cannot be accountable for care in it.
  variant: inpatient
- id: BR-E12
  text: An encounter has at most one active location at a time, and each location's period is within the encounter's. When the patient goes somewhere temporarily, such as the scanner or dialysis, their bed becomes reserved and the temporary location active; on return the temporary location completes and the bed is active again.
  rationale: The patient is in one place, but their bed is still theirs. FHIR marks a held location reserved, and IHE tracks temporary transfers to radiotherapy, scanners and dialysis as their own movements.
- id: BR-E13
  text: 'Each diagnosis has a role and a rank, and a code valid in diagnosis_code_system on the right date: the discharge date for inpatient encounters, the encounter date otherwise. A completed encounter has at least one discharge or billing diagnosis.'
  rationale: CMS applies each fiscal year's ICD-10-CM codes to discharges and encounters from October 1 (the FY 2027 files to those from October 1, 2026). A code valid at admission may be retired by discharge.
- id: BR-E14
  text: An encounter carries the sensitivity codes Organization gives for its department, such as SUD for a substance use disorder program. Data attached to it carries the same codes, and disclosure follows the Consent module.
  rationale: Records from US federally assisted substance use disorder programs have extra protection under 42 CFR Part 2, revised with a compliance date of February 16, 2026. Marking the encounter is what lets every module apply it.
- id: BR-E15
  text: A virtual encounter records its connection details and where the patient is.
  rationale: In the United States a practitioner must be licensed where the patient is, as the Clinical appointments rules state.
  variant: ambulatory
- id: BR-E16
  text: length is the time from actual_start to actual_end less time on leave.
  rationale: FHIR defines length as the time the encounter lasted, less time absent. Leave counts toward neither care nor billing time.
- id: BR-E17
  text: When the Patients module merges two patients, their encounters move to the remaining record; when a merge is undone, encounters recorded since the merge are listed for review.
  rationale: An encounter on a retired record disappears from the patient's history. Only a person can tell whose care a post-merge encounter was.
- id: BR-E18
  text: Every view and change of an encounter is logged with who, when and what. Opening an encounter of a restricted patient needs a reason, as the Patients module requires.
  rationale: HIPAA requires audit controls (45 CFR 164.312(b)). An encounter reveals why someone was seen.
- id: BR-E19
  text: Fields the service sets, such as status, class_history, length, documentation_complete, record_due_at, locked_at, amendments, has_clinical_data and revision, cannot be written by clients.
  rationale: A client writing status skips the lifecycle and its guards; one writing locked_at changes what counts as the legal record.
- id: BR-E20
  text: Every change sends the revision it read, and a stale revision is rejected.
  rationale: Registrars, clinicians and HIM staff change the same encounter; without this, one silently undoes another.
- id: BR-E21
  text: A patient has at most one inpatient or observation encounter that is in progress or on hold. A patient merge can break this; BR-E29 handles it.
  rationale: A patient is in one hospital stay at a time. Two open stays send orders, results and charges to the wrong one.
  variant: inpatient
- id: BR-E22
  text: No encounter starts after the patient's recorded death.
  rationale: An encounter after death almost always means the wrong patient. A death recorded in error is retracted in the Patients module first.
- id: BR-E23
  text: 'When an encounter enters observation, encounter.observation_started is emitted with observation_notice_due_at: the start plus observation_notice_deadline. Deciding who must receive the notice, and giving it, belongs to Admission, discharge and transfer.'
  rationale: US hospitals must give Medicare patients who receive observation services as outpatients for more than 24 hours a standardized written notice no later than 36 hours after observation starts, or sooner on transfer, discharge or admission (42 CFR 489.20(y)). The encounter knows when observation began.
  variant: inpatient
- id: BR-E24
  text: A pre-admission from Admission, discharge and transfer opens a planned inpatient encounter with planned_start and the pre-admission id. The admission starts that same encounter; a cancelled pre-admission cancels it.
  rationale: FHIR uses planned encounters for pre-admission, and HL7 v2 sends pre-admit (A05) and cancel pre-admit (A38). Testing and paperwork before an elective stay need an encounter to attach to.
  variant: inpatient
- id: BR-E25
  text: A change of responsible department keeps the encounter and adds the new department to department_history with the time it took over.
  rationale: Who was responsible at each point matters for billing, quality reporting and accountability, the same as class history.
- id: BR-E26
  text: Encounters of a patient under legal hold are never erased or anonymised, even after the Patients module's retention ends.
  rationale: The Patients module stops erasure during litigation, and the encounters are the record in question.
- id: BR-E27
  text: A re-admission is marked on the admission when Admission, discharge and transfer reports it.
  rationale: Readmissions are tracked for quality and payment. FHIR records it as admission.reAdmission.
  variant: inpatient
- id: BR-E28
  text: When the Patients module records a death, the patient's planned encounters are cancelled with the reason patient_deceased. Encounters in progress are discharged by Admission, discharge and transfer or a clinician.
  rationale: A planned stay or visit for someone who has died would stay on lists and schedules. Encounters in progress need a person to record what happened.
- id: BR-E29
  text: After a patient merge, if the remaining patient has two inpatient or observation encounters in progress or on hold, both are kept and encounter.patient_review_needed is emitted for each, so an HIM specialist resolves them.
  rationale: Duplicate records can each hold an open stay for the same admission. Refusing the merge keeps the duplicate; keeping both silently breaks the one-stay rule (BR-E21).
- id: BR-E30
  text: When Appointments undoes a check-in, the planned encounter opened for it is cancelled.
  rationale: The check-in was a mistake, so the visit it opened never happened.
- id: BR-E31
  text: When the Patients module marks a patient entered in error, the patient's planned encounters are cancelled with the reason patient_entered_in_error.
  rationale: The record was created by mistake, so a planned visit or stay for it will never happen. Leaving it planned keeps it on lists and schedules.
- id: BR-E32
  text: When the Patients module retracts a death, the encounters cancelled for it (BR-E28) are listed, and encounter.patient_review_needed is emitted for each so they can be planned again.
  rationale: A death recorded for the wrong patient must not silently cost them a planned admission. A person decides whether each one still stands.
- id: BR-E33
  text: A check-in for a patient who already has an open ambulatory encounter in the same visit_grouping scope adds the appointment to that encounter instead of opening another.
  rationale: FHIR lets one encounter fulfil several appointments. A morning of blood draw, X-ray and consultation is one visit to document and bill, not three.
  variant: ambulatory
- id: BR-E34
  text: Every encounter gets a visit number from visit_number_system when it is opened. It is never reused, and stays with the encounter through merges and amendments.
  rationale: Laboratory, radiology and billing systems match their data to a visit by its number (HL7 v2 PV1-19). A reused number would attach results to the wrong visit.
- id: BR-E35
  text: An encounter records the orders it carries out, and Orders confirms each one is for the same patient.
  rationale: FHIR links an encounter to the request that initiated it (basedOn). Without the link nobody can tell whether an order was carried out.
- id: BR-E36
  text: 'Pending events from Admission, discharge and transfer are recorded as plans: a pending transfer adds a planned location, a pending discharge sets planned_end, and each is removed when cancelled or replaced by the real event.'
  rationale: IHE Patient Administration Management defines pending admit, transfer and discharge (A14, A15, A16, cancelled by A27, A26, A25). Bed management and discharge planning work from who is expected to move.
  variant: inpatient
- id: BR-E37
  text: A transfer recorded by mistake can be cancelled, which completes nothing and makes the previous location active again. An earlier movement is corrected by its entry id; on a locked encounter the correction is an amendment.
  rationale: IHE defines cancel transfer (A12) and identifies each movement so a past one can be corrected (Historic Movement option). Location-based billing and the record of where the patient was depend on it.
  variant: inpatient

stateMachine:
  initial: planned
  states:
  - planned
  - in_progress
  - on_hold
  - discharged
  - completed
  - cancelled
  - discontinued
  - entered_in_error
  transitions:
  - from: planned
    to: in_progress
    trigger: 'start: a practitioner begins care'
  - from: planned
    to: cancelled
    trigger: 'cancel: the encounter never happened'
  - from: in_progress
    to: on_hold
    trigger: leave of absence starts
  - from: on_hold
    to: in_progress
    trigger: leave of absence ends
  - from: in_progress
    to: discharged
    trigger: discharge or end of visit
  - from: in_progress
    to: discontinued
    trigger: the patient left before care was complete
  - from: discharged
    to: completed
    trigger: complete
    guard: documentation complete, a final diagnosis, and no open part (BR-E6, BR-E7)
  - from: discharged
    to: in_progress
    trigger: discharge cancelled
  - from: planned
    to: entered_in_error
    trigger: mark entered in error
    guard: has_clinical_data unset
  - from: in_progress
    to: entered_in_error
    trigger: mark entered in error
    guard: has_clinical_data unset
workflows:
- name: Open at check-in
  trigger: Appointments reports appointment.checked_in
  actor: System
  variant: ambulatory
  steps:
  - Create the encounter as planned, subject status arrived, with the appointment id, class and department
  - Report the encounter id to Appointments
  - Emit encounter.created
  outcome: The visit has a record before care starts, and the appointment points to it.
- name: Start care
  trigger: A practitioner begins seeing the patient
  actor: Clinician
  steps:
  - Set actual_start, status in progress and subject status receiving care
  - Add the practitioner as primary performer or attending
  - Report care to the Patients module
  - Emit encounter.started
  outcome: Waiting and care time are recorded separately.
- name: Follow an emergency visit
  trigger: The Emergency department module reports arrival, triage and departure
  actor: System
  variant: emergency
  steps:
  - Open the encounter in progress with subject status arrived
  - Set subject status triaged, then receiving care, as reported
  - On departure, discharge it, or discontinue it with the departure reason if care was not complete
  - If the patient is admitted, ADT opens an inpatient encounter; the emergency encounter is discharged
  outcome: Every emergency visit is one encounter, including patients who leave.
- name: Pre-admit a stay
  trigger: Admission, discharge and transfer reports a pre-admission
  actor: System
  variant: inpatient
  steps:
  - Open a planned inpatient encounter with planned_start and the pre-admission id
  - On admission, start this encounter instead of opening another
  - On a cancelled pre-admission, cancel it
  outcome: Pre-admission testing and paperwork attach to the stay before it begins.
- name: Follow a stay
  trigger: Admission, discharge and transfer reports an admission, transfer, leave or discharge
  actor: System
  variant: inpatient
  steps:
  - On admission, open the encounter in progress with the admission details and first location
  - On transfer, complete the previous location and add the new one
  - On leave, put the encounter on hold; on return, resume it
  - On a class change, add it to class_history
  - On discharge, set discharged with disposition and destination, and record_due_at
  outcome: The stay is one record from admission to discharge.
- name: Complete the record
  trigger: Clinical documentation reports the documentation complete
  actor: Health information management (HIM) specialist
  steps:
  - Check a final diagnosis is recorded and no part is open
  - Set completed and locked_at
  - Emit encounter.completed with the diagnoses and class history for Billing
  outcome: The record is complete and locked within the deadline.
- name: Amend a locked encounter
  trigger: A locked encounter needs a correction
  actor: Health information management (HIM) specialist
  steps:
  - Record the new value, the previous value, who, when and why
  - Emit encounter.amended
  outcome: The record is corrected and its history kept.

constraints:
- id: CON-E1
  text: Encounter data is encrypted in transit and at rest.
  rationale: GDPR Article 32 lists encryption as a security measure, and HIPAA treats it as an addressable safeguard (45 CFR 164.312).
- id: CON-E2
  text: Coded fields use the code systems named in the data model, and local codes are mapped to them before data leaves this module.
  rationale: Billing, reporting and exchange all assume these code systems.
- id: CON-E3
  text: Times are RFC 3339 instants in UTC.
  rationale: Stays cross daylight saving changes; instants keep length correct.

apiConventions:
- topic: Authentication
  text: Every request carries the caller's identity from Identity. Permissions are checked against it (Permission matrix).
- topic: Errors
  text: Errors use problem details (application/problem+json) with a code member, such as ENCOUNTER_LOCKED.
  reference: RFC 9457
- topic: Pagination
  text: 'Lists are cursor-based: limit and an optional cursor in, next_cursor out.'
- topic: Concurrency
  text: Every change sends the revision it read. A stale revision fails with ENCOUNTER_REVISION_CONFLICT (BR-E20).
- topic: Retries
  text: Every change accepts an Idempotency-Key header; a retry returns the original result (FR-E12).
  reference: IETF draft-ietf-httpapi-idempotency-key-header
- topic: Times
  text: Instants are RFC 3339 timestamps in UTC (CON-E3).
  reference: RFC 3339
- topic: FHIR
  text: GET /encounters/{id} returns an HL7 FHIR R5 Encounter when the request asks for application/fhir+json (FR-E11).
- topic: Versioning
  text: Additive changes don't change the version. A breaking change ships as a new major version with Deprecation and Sunset headers.
  reference: RFC 9745, RFC 8594

deliveryGuarantees:
- id: DG-E1
  text: Events are published at least once; consumers drop one with a source and id they have seen.
  rationale: A relay can publish twice after a crash. CloudEvents defines source plus id as the duplicate check.
- id: DG-E2
  text: Events for one encounter can arrive out of order; each carries the revision, and consumers ignore a lower one.
  rationale: Brokers and retries don't guarantee order.
- id: DG-E3
  text: Events carry ids, codes and changed field names, never diagnoses text, notes or the patient's details. Consumers read the encounter.
  rationale: Events fan out widely. This follows the minimum necessary standard (45 CFR 164.502(b)) and keeps sensitive encounters out of logs.

Every note, order and result in the record belongs to an encounter. This
module keeps that record of each visit and stay: when it happened, who was
responsible, where the patient was, why they came and what was found.

## The appointment is the plan, the encounter is what happened

Checking in opens the encounter, but arrival is not care. The encounter waits,
planned, until a practitioner starts (BR-E2), so the record shows both how long
the patient waited and how long they were seen.

## A stay is one record, whatever it becomes

A patient under observation who is admitted keeps the same encounter, with the
time of each class recorded (BR-E5). Transfers, leave and discharge come from
Admission, discharge and transfer and are recorded as they happen (BR-E4).

## Discharged is not done

Care can be over while the record isn't. An encounter stays discharged until
its documentation and final diagnosis are complete, within the deadline the
law sets: 30 days for US hospitals (BR-E7). Then it locks, and later changes
are amendments that keep what they replaced (BR-E8).

## Codes are checked against the right day

ICD-10-CM changes every 1 October. A stay's diagnoses are checked against the
release in effect on the discharge date, so a stay that crosses the change
isn't rejected for a code that was valid when it ended (BR-E13).
