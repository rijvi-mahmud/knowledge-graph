A clinical appointment carries obligations the [core lifecycle](/docs/core/appointment) does not.
The patient must consent before care is documented, coverage should be confirmed
with the payer, and a completed visit has to become part of the clinical record.

The slot itself behaves exactly as specified in the core module. Everything
below sits around it.

## Consent gates completion, not booking

Consent is collected at check-in, not at the time of booking — a front desk
takes the appointment over the phone weeks earlier. Modelling consent as a
booking requirement would contradict how a clinic actually runs.

So consent blocks the transition to `completed`. An appointment can sit in
`booked` indefinitely with `consent_status` still pending, and only closing it
out requires a recorded decision.

## Eligibility never blocks care

The payer check runs asynchronously after the booking is already held. A failed
result flags billing for manual review; it does not cancel the appointment or
prevent it from being made.

This is deliberate. Payer systems are slow, frequently unavailable, and often
wrong about active coverage. Turning any of those failures into a scheduling
refusal would deny care for an administrative reason, which is a far worse
outcome than a claim that needs manual attention later.

The same reasoning applies to outages: when the eligibility service is
unreachable, the status stays pending and the booking proceeds.

## Completion creates the encounter

A completed appointment is what causes an encounter to exist. Appointment emits
`appointment.completed`, the Encounter service creates the clinical record, and
the resulting `encounter_id` is written back.

Appointment never writes clinical data itself. Keeping that boundary strict
means scheduling storage holds times, participants and consent state — but no
diagnoses, notes or results — which narrows the surface subject to HIPAA
controls to a service that does not handle clinical content at all.

## Duplicate same-day bookings

A patient may not hold two active appointments for the same service type on the
same day. In practice these are almost always double-entry by front-desk staff,
and left in place they produce duplicate claims that have to be reconciled after
submission.
