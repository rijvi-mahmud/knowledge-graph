---
title: Clinical documentation
description: 'Notes, vital signs and assessments recorded during an encounter: who wrote them, when, who signed them, how they are corrected, and when patients can read them.'
module: clinical-documentation
version: 0.2.0
status: draft
industry: healthcare
domain: ehr
purpose: 'Keep the clinical record of each encounter: the notes practitioners write and sign, and the vital signs and assessments they measure. Make every entry attributable, dated, timed and authenticated, keep every version once others can rely on it, track the notes each encounter still needs, tell Encounters when its documentation is complete, and give patients their notes as soon as the law expects. Works in the United States and the European Union.'
scope:
- Writing, signing and cosigning notes, such as progress notes, history and physical notes, operative reports and discharge summaries
- Notes entered by scribes, dictated, or drafted by speech recognition or AI tools, and the author's review before signing
- Addenda, late entries, corrections, discarded drafts and notes entered in error, keeping every version once a note is available for care
- Moving notes to the right encounter, and following encounter and patient merges
- Copied text and its source
- Required notes for each encounter, documentation deficiencies and their deadlines, and reporting to Encounters when documentation is complete
- Releasing notes to patients, holding them back only on the grounds the law allows, and psychotherapy notes in the United States
- Patient-entered information, kept distinguishable from professional entries
- Requests to correct a note, and statements of disagreement
- 'Vital signs: recording, units, plausibility checks, device readings that need validation, derived values and corrections'
- 'Assessments: scored instruments such as pain scores, with the score computed by the service'
nonGoals:
- Orders, including verbal orders and order sets. Orders and results owns them, including their authentication.
- Laboratory, imaging and pathology results and their reports. Orders and results owns them.
- Medication administration records and allergy lists. Medications and allergies owns them.
- Problem lists and diagnoses. Encounters records encounter diagnoses; a problem list is a later module.
- Early warning scores, alerts and escalation such as rapid response calls. Clinical decision support reads the observations this module records.
- Connecting to monitors and other devices. Device integration delivers readings; this module records and validates them.
- Scanned paper and documents from outside the hospital. Document imaging owns them.
- Consent forms and disclosure decisions, including authorizations for psychotherapy notes and 42 CFR Part 2 records. Consent owns them.
- Copies of the record, the accounting of disclosures and the US single-patient export. Release of information owns them.
- Coding notes for claims. Billing owns coding.
- Speech recognition and AI drafting themselves. This module records what such a tool drafted and who reviewed it.
actors:
- name: Clinician
  description: A licensed practitioner who writes and signs notes, such as a physician, advanced practice provider or therapist.
- name: Nurse
  description: A nurse who records vital signs and assessments, validates device readings and writes nursing notes.
- name: Trainee
  description: A resident or student whose notes need a supervising practitioner's cosignature where the deployment requires it.
- name: Scribe
  description: A person who enters documentation for a named practitioner. A scribe never signs for them.
- name: Health information management (HIM) specialist
  description: Tracks deficiencies, moves misfiled notes, marks entries in error and decides correction requests.
- name: Privacy officer
  description: Reviews the access log and holds placed on releases.
- name: Patient
  description: The patient, or a representative within the authority the Patients module records. Reads their notes and adds patient-entered information.
- name: System
  description: 'Other modules and tools: Encounters, Device integration, and drafting tools such as speech recognition or AI scribes.'
assumptions:
- id: AS-E1
  text: Encounters reports when encounters start, are discharged, complete, move to another patient, merge, or are cancelled.
  rationale: Notes open and lock with their encounter. Encounters owns its lifecycle.
- id: AS-E2
  text: Identity can confirm, at the moment of signing, that the person signing is the signed-in user, by asking for a credential again.
  rationale: A signature attests authorship. Hospitals must use an author identification system that protects the integrity of authentication (42 CFR 482.24(b)).
- id: AS-E3
  text: Organization knows each practitioner's roles on a date, which roles may write which note types, and who may supervise whom.
  rationale: Scope of practice and supervision come from state law, licences and medical staff bylaws, which Organization holds.
- id: AS-E4
  text: Medical staff bylaws or hospital policy set the deadlines for signing, cosigning and completing notes, within any limit the law sets.
  rationale: The law sets outer limits, such as 30 days to complete a US hospital record; hospitals set shorter ones in their bylaws.
glossary:
- term: Note
  definition: A clinical document written about a patient for an encounter, such as a progress note or discharge summary (FHIR Composition, HL7 v2 MDM document).
- term: Authentication
  definition: The author's signature on an entry, which identifies them and attests that it is accurate (42 CFR 482.24(c)(1)).
- term: Cosignature
  definition: A supervising practitioner's authentication of a note written by someone who needs supervision, such as a resident.
- term: Addendum
  definition: A new entry linked to an earlier note, adding information that wasn't available or was omitted. The earlier note is unchanged.
- term: Late entry
  definition: An entry recorded well after the care it describes. It shows both times.
- term: Correction
  definition: A new version of a note fixing an error, with a reason. The previous version is kept.
- term: Deficiency
  definition: Something a practitioner still owes the record, such as a missing, unsigned or uncosigned note.
- term: Delinquent record
  definition: A record with a deficiency past the deadline the medical staff bylaws set.
- term: Psychotherapy notes
  definition: In the United States, a mental health professional's notes of a counseling session, kept apart from the rest of the record (45 CFR 164.501).
- term: Vital signs
  definition: Heart rate, respiratory rate, blood pressure, temperature, oxygen saturation, height, weight, head circumference and BMI, as in the FHIR vital signs profiles.
- term: Assessment
  definition: A structured evaluation with defined items and, often, a score, such as a pain score.
concepts:
- name: Available for care
  description: A note becomes available for care when it is signed. Before that it is a draft only its author works on; after it, others may have acted on it, so it never changes in place (HL7 v2 document availability status).
- name: Documentation complete
  description: An encounter's documentation is complete when every required note is signed and cosigned and no deficiency is open. This module reports it; Encounters decides when the encounter completes.

businessRules:
- id: BR-E1
  text: Every note and observation belongs to one encounter of one patient whose record is in use. The encounter must not be cancelled or entered in error. When Encounters reports an encounter cancelled or entered in error, drafts on it are listed for their authors to move or discard.
  rationale: Every entry is found through its encounter (FHIR Composition.encounter, Observation.encounter). An entry on a cancelled encounter documents care that, by the record, never happened.
- id: BR-E2
  text: A draft note is seen and edited only by its author, and by the scribe or drafting tool that created it. It is not part of the record used for care, it is never released to the patient, and it can be edited freely.
  rationale: HL7 v2 allows editing a document in place only while it is unavailable for patient care (MDM T07). Until the author signs, nobody else should act on it.
- id: BR-E3
  text: 'Signing makes a note available for care. From then on its content never changes in place: a change is a correction (BR-E10) or an addendum (BR-E9), and the previous version stays readable.'
  rationale: Others may already have acted on a signed note. HL7 v2 replaces an available document instead of editing it (MDM T09), Medicare wants every change clearly and permanently identified (Program Integrity Manual chapter 3, 3.3.2.5), and HIPAA requires protection against improper alteration (45 CFR 164.312(c)(1)).
- id: BR-E4
  text: Only the responsible author signs a note, with their own credential confirmed again at signing. Nobody signs for someone else, signing can't be delegated or automatic, and a stamped or typed name isn't a signature. Signing records signed_at and needs every required section of the note type to have text.
  rationale: Every entry must be dated, timed and authenticated by the person responsible for it, through an author identification system that protects the integrity of authentication (42 CFR 482.24(b) and (c)(1)). Medicare doesn't accept stamped signatures except for a documented disability (Program Integrity Manual chapter 3, 3.3.2.4).
- id: BR-E5
  text: When cosign_rules say a note type written by an author's role needs a cosignature, the author names the cosigner when signing, signing makes it preliminary and opens an uncosigned deficiency for the cosigner. The cosigner signs it final, optionally with an attestation statement; they can't change the author's text. Changes they want are made by the author as a correction, or by the cosigner as an addendum.
  rationale: Supervising practitioners take responsibility for trainees' notes as medical staff bylaws require. The author's words stay theirs, so the record shows who said what. HL7 v2 marks such a note pre-authenticated until it is authenticated (Table 0271 PA, AU).
- id: BR-E6
  text: A scribe enters a note for a named practitioner. The note records the scribe as data_enterer and the practitioner as author. Only the practitioner signs it, with their own date and time, and the scribe can never sign or cosign.
  rationale: The practitioner's signature affirms that the note documents the care (Medicare Program Integrity Manual chapter 3, 3.3.2.4). The Joint Commission expects the scribe to be identifiable and the practitioner to authenticate the entry.
- id: BR-E7
  text: Content drafted by speech recognition or an AI tool starts as a draft with source and drafting_tool recorded. The author can sign it only by confirming they reviewed it, which sets review_attested, and the provenance stays with the note.
  rationale: Medicare requires the practitioner's concurrence when AI technology captures medical record entries (Program Integrity Manual chapter 3, 3.3.2.4). Drafting tools can invent or misattribute findings, so nothing they write reaches the record unreviewed.
- id: BR-E8
  text: 'Every note and observation keeps two times: when the care or measurement happened (service_at, effective_at) and when it was recorded. Neither can be later than now plus clock_tolerance. An entry recorded more than late_entry_threshold after the care is a late entry: it needs a reason and is shown as late.'
  rationale: Entries must be dated and timed (42 CFR 482.24(c)(1)), and a late entry must be identifiable as such (Medicare Program Integrity Manual chapter 3, 3.3.2.5). Showing both times stops a late entry passing as contemporaneous.
- id: BR-E9
  text: 'Information found or remembered after a note is available goes in an addendum: a new note linked to it by parent_note_id, with its own author, times and signature. The parent doesn''t change. An addendum can''t be added to a draft, cancelled or entered-in-error note.'
  rationale: HL7 v2 gives an addendum its own document id linked to the parent (MDM T05). AHIMA guidance says an addendum bears the current date, its reason and its author.
- id: BR-E10
  text: 'Only the responsible author corrects a signed note, with a reason. The correction is a new version: status becomes corrected, the previous version stays in versions and stays readable, and the correction is signed like the original. If a cosignature was needed, the corrected version needs it again.'
  rationale: A correction must keep the original and show who changed what, when and why (AHIMA amendment guidance; Medicare Program Integrity Manual chapter 3, 3.3.2.5). Only the author can say what they meant.
- id: BR-E11
  text: A draft can be discarded by its author, which cancels it. A note that has been signed can never be cancelled or deleted.
  rationale: HL7 v2 cancels only documents never made available for care (MDM T11); an available one is replaced, so the record shows what others saw.
- id: BR-E12
  text: A signed note created in error, such as on the wrong patient or twice, is marked entered in error by its author or an HIM specialist with a reason. It disappears from views used for care and from the patient's view, but is kept, with its content, for HIM specialists and privacy officers.
  rationale: FHIR marks such a document entered-in-error rather than deleting it. A note shown on the wrong patient may have been read, so what was shown must stay provable.
- id: BR-E13
  text: 'An HIM specialist moves a note or observation filed under the wrong encounter of the same patient, with a reason; the earlier encounter is kept in moved_from. An entry on the wrong patient is never moved: it is marked entered in error (BR-E12) and a copy is started as a draft on the right encounter, keeping service_at and the original author, who must sign it again.'
  rationale: A signature attests content about a particular patient. HL7 v2 removes a document from the wrong patient's record rather than re-pointing it (MDM T11 scenario).
- id: BR-E14
  text: Text copied through the service's copy feature keeps its source note, author and date in copied_text, and is shown as copied. Copying from another patient's record is refused, and copy_paste_policy can block copying into some note types.
  rationale: The Partnership for Health IT Patient Safety recommends making copied material easily identifiable and its source readily available, because copying spreads errors and puts documentation in the wrong patient's chart (Tsou and others, 2017).
- id: BR-E15
  text: Each note type in note_types can be required for some encounter classes, with a deadline. When an encounter of that class starts, or an encounter's class changes to it, a missing_note deficiency is opened for the responsible practitioner, due at the deadline counted from the start or the change.
  rationale: Hospitals must document certain notes for each patient, such as the discharge summary (42 CFR 482.24(c)(4)). Tracking them per encounter is what lets a record be completed on time. A patient admitted from observation becomes an inpatient at the class change, so the clock starts there.
- id: BR-E16
  text: In the United States, an inpatient admission or a procedure needing anesthesia needs a history and physical note signed no more than 30 days before or 24 hours after admission or registration, and before the procedure. A note from before admission needs an update note within 24 hours after admission, and before the procedure. The service answers whether an encounter has a current one.

workflows:
- name: Write and sign a note
  trigger: A clinician documents care
  actor: Clinician
  steps:
  - Start a note on the encounter, from a template or blank, which saves a draft
  - Write and save the draft as often as needed, sending the revision each time
  - 'Sign it: Identity confirms the credential again, required sections are checked, and signed_at is set'
  - The note becomes final, or preliminary when a cosignature is needed, and note.signed is emitted
  - The note is released to the patient unless it is held
  outcome: The note is available for care and to the patient, and any deficiency for it resolves.
- name: Cosign a trainee's note
  trigger: A trainee signs a note whose type and role need a cosignature
  actor: Clinician
  steps:
  - The note becomes preliminary and an uncosigned deficiency opens for the cosigner
  - The cosigner reads it and cosigns, optionally adding an attestation statement
  - The note becomes final and note.cosigned is emitted
  outcome: The supervising practitioner has taken responsibility for the note.
- name: Document with a scribe or drafting tool
  trigger: A practitioner works with a scribe, dictation, speech recognition or an AI scribe
  actor: Clinician
  steps:
  - The scribe or tool creates a draft naming the practitioner as author, with data_enterer or drafting_tool recorded
  - The practitioner reviews and edits the draft
  - The practitioner signs, confirming review, which sets review_attested
  outcome: The note carries the practitioner's signature and shows how it was produced.
- name: Fix a signed note
  trigger: An error or missing information is found in a signed note
  actor: Clinician
  steps:
  - For missing or new information, add an addendum and sign it
  - For an error, the author corrects the note with a reason and signs the new version
  - For a note on the wrong patient or a duplicate, the author or an HIM specialist marks it entered in error, and the author signs a copy on the right encounter
  - note.corrected or note.entered_in_error is emitted so Release of information can tell recipients
  outcome: The record shows what was wrong, what replaced it, who changed it and when.
- name: Complete an encounter's documentation
  trigger: Encounters reports encounter.discharged
  actor: System
  steps:
  - Check the encounter for drafts, preliminary notes, open deficiencies and missing required notes
  - If any remain, keep the deficiencies on the practitioners' lists and mark them delinquent when overdue
  - When none remain, report documentation.completed to Encounters
  outcome: Encounters can complete the encounter within its record completion deadline.
- name: Record and validate vital signs
  trigger: A nurse measures vital signs, or a monitor sends readings
  actor: Nurse
  steps:
  - Enter the values with their units and times, or open the device readings waiting for validation
  - The service checks codes, units and plausibility, flags abnormal values, and computes BMI where it can
  - The nurse accepts the representative readings and rejects artifacts
  - Accepted values become final and observation.recorded is emitted
  outcome: The chart holds validated vital signs with who measured them and when.
- name: Handle a patient's correction request
  trigger: A patient asks for a note to be corrected
  actor: Health information management (HIM) specialist
  steps:
  - Record the request, and set accuracy_contested if the patient asks for restriction meanwhile
  - Check the content with the author or another health professional
  - Accept it, and have the author correct the note or add an addendum, or deny it in writing
  - Emit note.correction_decided; on denial, record any statement of disagreement and rebuttal
  outcome: The decision is made within the deadline and travels with later disclosures.

constraints:
- id: CON-E1
  text: Notes and observations are encrypted in transit and at rest.
  rationale: GDPR Article 32 lists encryption as a security measure, and HIPAA treats it as an addressable safeguard (45 CFR 164.312(a)(2)(iv) and (e)).
- id: CON-E2
  text: A signature is an electronic act of the signer, bound to their identity and the version signed. Stamped, typed or scanned names are never accepted as signatures.
  rationale: Hospitals must protect the integrity of authentication (42 CFR 482.24(b)), and Medicare doesn't accept stamped signatures except for a documented disability (Program Integrity Manual chapter 3, 3.3.2.4).
- id: CON-E3
  text: Stored content of a signed version is never rewritten, including by format conversion; views and exports are generated from it.
  rationale: What the author signed must be provable later (45 CFR 164.312(c)(1)).

apiConventions:
- topic: Authentication
  text: Every request carries the caller's identity from Identity. Permissions are checked against it (Permission matrix). Signing also needs a fresh credential confirmation (BR-E4).
- topic: Errors
  text: Errors use problem details (application/problem+json) with a code member, such as NOTE_NOT_EDITABLE. A request reports every failing field at once.
  reference: RFC 9457
- topic: Pagination
  text: 'Lists are cursor-based: limit and an optional cursor in, next_cursor out.'
- topic: Concurrency
  text: Every change sends the revision it read. A stale revision fails with NOTE_REVISION_CONFLICT or OBSERVATION_REVISION_CONFLICT (BR-E45).
- topic: Retries
  text: Every create accepts an Idempotency-Key header; a retry returns the original result (FR-E12).
  reference: IETF draft-ietf-httpapi-idempotency-key-header
- topic: Times
  text: Instants are RFC 3339 timestamps with an offset; the service stores them in UTC.
  reference: RFC 3339
- topic: FHIR
  text: Notes are returned as HL7 FHIR R5 Composition, and observations as Observation, when the request asks for application/fhir+json (FR-E11).
- topic: Versioning
  text: Additive changes don't change the version. A breaking change ships as a new major version with Deprecation and Sunset headers.
  reference: RFC 9745, RFC 8594

deliveryGuarantees:
- id: DG-E1
  text: Events are published at least once; consumers drop one with a source and id they have seen.
  rationale: A relay can publish twice after a crash. CloudEvents defines source plus id as the duplicate check.
- id: DG-E2
  text: Events about one note or observation can arrive out of order; each carries the revision, and consumers ignore a lower one.
  rationale: Brokers and retries don't guarantee order.
- id: DG-E3
  text: Events carry ids, codes and sensitivity, never note text or values. Consumers read the note or observation.
  rationale: Events fan out widely. This follows the minimum necessary standard (45 CFR 164.502(b)) and GDPR data minimisation (Article 5(1)(c)).
- id: DG-E4
  text: Every event carries a unique id, its type, the entry it is about, when the change happened, and the revision where the entry has one.
  rationale: Consumers order and deduplicate by these. CloudEvents defines id, type and time for every event.

risks:
- id: RISK-E1
  risk: A note is written on the wrong patient.
  impact: Care decisions and disclosures based on someone else's information.
  mitigation: Entered in error with a copy on the right patient, no cross-patient copying, and recipients told (BR-E12, BR-E13, BR-E14, BR-E35).
- id: RISK-E2
  risk: Copied text carries outdated or wrong findings forward.
  impact: Errors spread through the record, and cloned documentation raises billing concerns.
  mitigation: Copied text is labelled with its source and copy_paste_policy can block it (BR-E14); monitoring is a deployment duty.
- id: RISK-E3
  risk: An author signs a drafted note without reading it.
  impact: Invented or misattributed findings enter the legal record.
  mitigation: Review attestation, approved tools only, and provenance kept (BR-E7); auditing review behaviour is a deployment duty.
- id: RISK-E4
  risk: Records stay incomplete.
  impact: Survey findings under the conditions of participation, and encounters that can't be completed or billed.
  mitigation: Deficiencies with deadlines and delinquency (BR-E20), and reporting to Encounters (BR-E21).
- id: RISK-E5
  risk: A patient reads bad news in a note before anyone explains it.
  impact: Distress, and complaints.
  mitigation: Holds only on lawful grounds with review (BR-E27), and the EU delay where member-state law allows it; clinicians plan conversations around release.
- id: RISK-E6
  risk: Device artifacts enter the chart.
  impact: False alerts and wrong trends.
  mitigation: Validation before readings are final (BR-E38), plausibility limits (BR-E37).

  decision: Clinical documentation is an EHR-only module with no core or healthcare page.
  rationale: Notes and vital signs are clinical-record concepts, and the EHR is the only domain specified.
  date: '2026-10-09'
- id: ADR-E2
  decision: A corrected note keeps its id and gains versions, rather than becoming a new note.
  rationale: FHIR keeps a corrected Composition's identity, so links from other modules stay valid. HL7 v2 interfaces send a replacement document (MDM T10) built from the new version.
  date: '2026-10-09'
- id: ADR-E3
  decision: Vital signs and assessments live in this module, not in Orders and results.
  rationale: 'The care team measures them at the bedside, usually without an order, and they share notes'' needs: performer, time, late entry and correction. Laboratory results come from orders and stay there.'
  date: '2026-10-09'
- id: ADR-E4
  decision: A draft isn't part of the record used for care and isn't released.
  rationale: HL7 v2 keeps a document unavailable until it is authenticated, and ONC says notes not yet finalized need not be released unless used to make decisions. Others act only on signed notes, so a draft is never used to make decisions.
  date: '2026-10-09'
- id: ADR-E5
  decision: Device readings need validation by default.
  rationale: Artifact-laden readings filed automatically pollute the record; hospitals that trust a device can turn it off.

technicalNotes:
- id: TN-E1
  title: Make signed versions impossible to change
  text: Insert signed versions into note_versions only, revoke UPDATE and DELETE on it from the application role, and store a SHA-256 hash of the canonical content with the signature, so any later change is detectable.
- id: TN-E2
  title: Confirm the signer at signing
  text: Ask Identity for a fresh authentication before signing, for example an OpenID Connect request with prompt=login or max_age=0, and keep the authentication time with the signature.
- id: TN-E3
  title: Check limits in one unit
  text: Convert each value to its code's canonical UCUM unit with a UCUM library before checking vital_sign_limits, but store the value as entered. A Fahrenheit temperature typed as Celsius then fails the plausibility check.
- id: TN-E4
  title: Compute deficiencies from deadlines
  text: Run a scheduled job over open notes and required note types keyed on due_at; make it idempotent so a rerun opens no duplicate deficiency, and resolve deficiencies in the same transaction as the signature.
- id: TN-E5
  title: Never lose a draft
  text: Autosave drafts to the server with their revision, and keep the client copy until the server acknowledges it.

Every note, vital sign and assessment about a patient is recorded here,
against the encounter it documents. This module keeps them attributable and
provable: who wrote each entry, when the care happened, when it was written,
who signed it, and what it said before anyone changed it.

## A signed note never changes in place

Until its author signs, a note is a draft that nobody else acts on, so it can
be edited freely (BR-E2). Signing makes it available for care. From then on,
others may have read and acted on it, so a fix is a correction that keeps the
earlier version, or an addendum beside it (BR-E3, BR-E9, BR-E10). A note on
the wrong patient isn't moved or deleted: it is marked entered in error and
written again where it belongs (BR-E12, BR-E13).

## The signature is the author's, whoever typed the words

Scribes, dictation, speech recognition and AI tools can all draft a note, but
only the practitioner signs it, after confirming they reviewed it (BR-E6,
BR-E7). Nobody signs for someone else, not even for a practitioner who has
left. Their note is filed as unsigned rather than given a signature they never
made (BR-E47).

## In the United States, the law sets the clock for key notes

A history and physical is due within 24 hours of admission and before
surgery, an operative report straight after surgery, and the whole record
within 30 days of discharge (BR-E16, BR-E18, BR-E20). Each missing or unsigned
note is a deficiency on someone's list. When an encounter has none left,
Encounters is told its documentation is complete (BR-E21).

## Patients read notes when clinicians can

A signed note reaches the patient straight away. Holding it back for a
clinician to review first is likely information blocking in the United States,
and in the European Union patients have a right to immediate access. So a
note is held only on the narrow grounds the law allows, and the patient can
have the decision reviewed (BR-E25, BR-E26, BR-E27).

## A device reading is a suggestion until a nurse accepts it

Monitors record artifacts as readily as vital signs. Readings stay preliminary
until a nurse accepts the ones that represent the patient, and impossible
values, such as a Fahrenheit temperature entered as Celsius, are refused
(BR-E37, BR-E38).
