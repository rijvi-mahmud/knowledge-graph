- id: AS-E4

  rationale: Admitting and discharging patients is its job; this module only needs the answer and the event.

businessRules:
- id: BR-4
  text: A patient cannot have two active appointments whose slots overlap. A practitioner can, only when their department has allow_overbooking on, and with no more than max_overbooked_per_slot appointments overlapping one slot. Slots are half-open.
  rationale: Clinics overbook to make up for no-shows, which research finds range from 3% to 80% between clinics, and no single overbooking policy fits them all. A patient, though, can only be in one place.
- id: BR-E1
  text: A room cannot hold two active appointments whose slots overlap.
  rationale: Two patients cannot be examined in one room at once. The core Appointment specification leaves resources to the layers that need them (ADR-11).
- id: BR-E2
  text: For an outpatient appointment, check-in opens an encounter and the appointment records its encounter_id. A checked-in outpatient appointment completes only once its encounter exists.
  rationale: In FHIR, the appointment is fulfilled and linked to the newly created encounter at check-in. The encounter is where care is documented.
- id: BR-E3
  text: A department with referral_required on accepts a booking only with a valid referral.
  rationale: Specialist clinics often see only referred patients, so the referral is checked before the slot is taken.
- id: BR-E4
  text: An outpatient walk-in gets an appointment that starts now, marked walk_in, and is checked in straight away.
  rationale: Every visit needs an appointment to link its encounter and keep statistics complete.
- id: BR-E5
  text: 'An appointment for an admitted patient is inpatient and belongs to the patient''s current stay: it records the stay''s encounter_id at booking, and check-in opens no new encounter.'
  rationale: HL7 separates inpatient (IMP) from ambulatory (AMB) encounters. Care given during a stay, such as an MRI or theatre slot, is documented on that stay.
- id: BR-E6
  text: An inpatient appointment can be booked only while the patient has an active stay.
  rationale: Without a stay, there is no inpatient encounter to belong to, and the patient may not be in the building.
- id: BR-E7
  text: When a patient is discharged, each of their inpatient appointments still in the future is flagged needs_review. A Scheduler then cancels it or changes it to outpatient.
  rationale: These appointments assumed the patient was on a ward. Some are still needed after discharge, such as follow-up imaging, so neither cancelling nor keeping them silently is safe.

workflows:
- name: Register a walk-in
  trigger: A patient arrives without an appointment
  actor: Front desk
  steps:
  - Verify the patient with two identifiers
  - Book an appointment starting now in the department, marked walk_in
  - Check the patient in, which opens an encounter
  - Emit appointment.booked and appointment.checked_in
  outcome: The patient is in the queue like any other, with an encounter to document the visit.
- name: Link the encounter
  trigger: The Encounter module reports the encounter it opened
  actor: System
  steps:
  - Record encounter_id and increase revision
  - Emit appointment.encounter_linked
  outcome: The appointment and its encounter point to each other.
- name: Book for an admitted patient
  trigger: A ward or department books a service for an inpatient, such as an MRI
  actor: Scheduler
  steps:
  - Ask Admission, discharge and transfer for the patient's active stay
  - Book the appointment as inpatient, with the stay's encounter_id
  - Apply the usual overlap, room and overbooking rules
  - Emit appointment.booked
  outcome: The appointment is part of the patient's stay.
- name: Review after discharge
  trigger: Admission, discharge and transfer announces that a patient was discharged
  actor: System
  steps:
  - Find the patient's inpatient appointments still in the future
  - Flag each one needs_review and increase its revision
  - Emit appointment.review_needed for each
  outcome: A Scheduler decides each one, and none is lost or kept by accident.

Hospital appointments build on the
[clinical appointment rules](/docs/healthcare/appointment), which build on the
[core Appointment specification](/docs/core/appointment). This layer adds how a
hospital schedules care, for outpatients and for patients who are admitted.

## Outpatient or inpatient decides which encounter the appointment belongs to

An outpatient comes in for the appointment and leaves; Medicare counts them as
an outpatient even if they stay overnight. Their check-in opens a new
encounter (BR-E2). An admitted patient already has one encounter for their
whole stay, so an MRI or theatre slot booked for them belongs to that stay and
opens nothing new (BR-E5).

## Discharge doesn't silently cancel what was booked

When a patient goes home with an inpatient appointment still ahead, such as
physiotherapy next week, the appointment is flagged for review (BR-E7). A
Scheduler then changes it to outpatient or cancels it. Booking the admission
itself belongs to the Admission, discharge and transfer module.

## A clinic can overbook a doctor, never a patient

Clinics lose a share of every day to no-shows, and many overbook to make up for
it. How much depends on the clinic, so each department decides whether to
overbook and by how much (BR-4). A patient can still only be in one place.

## The room is booked as well as the doctor

Two patients can't share an examination room at the same time (BR-E1), so a
booking can name a room and the room is checked like a participant.
