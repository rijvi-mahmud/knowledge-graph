title: Clinical encounters
description: 'The record of each interaction between a patient and a care provider: when it happened, who cared for the patient and where, why, what was found, and when its record is complete.'
module: encounter
version: 0.3.2
status: draft
industry: healthcare
variants:
- id: ambulatory
  title: Ambulatory encounters
  description: Clinic visits, day procedures and virtual visits, where the patient is not in a hospital stay.
  summary: An ambulatory encounter (HL7 class AMB, or VR when virtual) is opened when Appointments reports a check-in or a walk-in. It starts when a practitioner begins care, not when the patient arrives, and ends when the visit ends.
purpose: 'Keep the record of each interaction between a patient and a care provider: its class, status and period, the practitioners and locations involved, the reasons and diagnoses, and whether its record is complete. Every clinical module attaches its data to an encounter by id. Works in the United States and the European Union.'
scope:
- Opening encounters when the module where care happens reports a check-in, walk-in, arrival or admission
- 'The encounter lifecycle: planned, in progress, discharged, completed, and cancelled, discontinued or entered in error'
- The class of an encounter, and changes to it
- Participants with their periods
- Locations during the encounter, with their periods
- Reasons for the encounter and its diagnoses, with their role and rank
- Encounters grouped under another, such as consultations
- Record completion and locking, and amendments after locking
- Sensitive encounters, such as at a substance use disorder program
- A department's and a practitioner's open and incomplete encounters
- Links to episodes of care
- Patients' own access to their encounters, and their correction requests
- Marking encounters covered by a self-pay restriction the Consent module agreed
- Moving an encounter recorded on the wrong patient, and merging duplicates
nonGoals:
- Booking. The Appointments module schedules visits; an encounter records what actually happened.
- Clinical notes, assessments, the history and physical, and discharge summaries. Clinical documentation owns them, attached to the encounter by id.
- Conditions and the problem list. The Conditions module owns them; an encounter refers to them, or carries a coded diagnosis.
- Coding for claims, diagnosis-related groups and charges. Billing owns them, using the diagnoses and class history this module gives.
- Consent to disclose sensitive records. The Consent module decides; this module flags the encounter.
- Episodes of care, such as a course of chemotherapy or a pregnancy. An Episodes module owns them; an encounter only links to them.

assumptions:
- id: AS-H1
  text: The modules where care happens, such as Appointments, report check-ins, arrivals, admissions, transfers and discharges as they happen.
  rationale: An encounter records what happened. The modules where it happens tell this one.
- id: AS-H2
  text: Clinical documentation reports when an encounter's documentation is complete.
  rationale: Only the module holding the notes knows whether the record is complete (BR-H7).
- id: AS-H3
  text: The jurisdiction, record_completion_deadline and Terminology's diagnosis_code_system settings are set with legal advice before go-live.
  rationale: Completion deadlines and code systems differ by US state, by payer and by EU member state.
glossary:
- term: Encounter
  definition: An interaction between a patient and healthcare providers to provide care or assess health (HL7 FHIR). An appointment records what is planned; an encounter records what happened.
- term: Encounter class
  definition: 'The setting: ambulatory (AMB), virtual (VR), emergency (EMER), observation (OBSENC) or inpatient (IMP).'
- term: Subject status
  definition: Where the patient is in the encounter, such as arrived, triaged or receiving care, separate from the encounter's own status.
- term: Record completion
  definition: The point where the encounter's documentation and final diagnosis are complete, after which it is locked.
- term: Amendment
  definition: A change to a locked encounter, recorded with who made it, when and why, keeping what it replaced.
- term: Part 2 program
  definition: A federally assisted substance use disorder program in the United States, whose records have extra protection (42 CFR Part 2).
  jurisdiction: us
concepts:
- name: Appointment, then encounter
  description: The appointment is the plan. When the patient checks in, the appointment is fulfilled and linked to the encounter opened for it (FHIR).
- name: Status is not the patient's position
  description: status tracks the encounter; subject_status tracks the patient, such as arrived or triaged. A patient can arrive an hour before the encounter starts.

businessRules:
- id: BR-H1
  text: Every encounter has one patient in use, one class and one responsible department.
  rationale: Every clinical entry is found through its encounter. An encounter without a patient or department is care nobody can find or bill.
- id: BR-H2
  text: 'An ambulatory or virtual encounter is opened when Appointments reports a check-in or a walk-in, at the moment encounter_opens_at sets: at check-in, as planned with subject status arrived, or when a practitioner starts care, in progress. It records the appointment and reports its own id back.'
  rationale: 'FHIR is not consistent on this: the Appointment page says the encounter "is typically created when the service starts, not when the patient arrives", and the Encounter page lets it start as arrived. Practice varies, so it is a setting; either way waiting time and care time stay separate.'
  variant: ambulatory
- id: BR-H6
  text: An encounter part of another has the same patient, except that a newborn's encounter can be part of its mother's encounter. A part's period is within its parent's, and the parent cannot be completed while a part is still open.
  rationale: FHIR groups encounters with partOf, such as a consultation during a stay, and says a child's encounter should reference the mother's encounter with partOf. A parent closed over an open part leaves care outside any complete record.
- id: BR-H7
  text: A discharged encounter becomes completed only when Clinical documentation reports its documentation complete and a final diagnosis is recorded. It must happen within record_completion_deadline of actual_end; encounters past that are listed as overdue. If Clinical documentation reports documentation reopened before then, documentation_complete is cleared.
  rationale: US hospitals must record the final diagnosis and complete the medical record within 30 days after discharge (42 CFR 482.24(c)(4)(viii)). FHIR uses discharged for care that is over while the record is still being completed.
- id: BR-H8
  text: A completed encounter is locked. Any later change is an amendment that records who, when, why and the previous value, and emits encounter.amended.
  rationale: Hospitals must keep record entries complete and protect their integrity (42 CFR 482.24(b)). Silent changes after completion would change a record others have relied on, including claims.
- id: BR-H9
  text: A planned encounter that never starts is cancelled. An encounter that started but ended early, such as the patient leaving against advice or without being seen, is discontinued with a departure reason. Every encounter that ends records its disposition.
  rationale: FHIR separates encounters that never happened from those cut short. A patient who left is part of the record and of quality reporting, so nothing is deleted.
- id: BR-H10
  text: An encounter can be marked entered in error only while no clinical data has been recorded against it (has_clinical_data unset). Otherwise its data is moved to the right encounter first.
  rationale: Discarding an encounter that holds notes or results would hide that care. Each clinical module moves its own entries.
- id: BR-H13
  text: Each diagnosis has a role and a rank, and a code valid in Terminology's diagnosis_code_system on the date Terminology's code_date_rule picks for diagnoses. A completed encounter has at least one discharge or billing diagnosis.
  rationale: CMS applies each fiscal year's ICD-10-CM codes to discharges and encounters from October 1 (the FY 2027 files to those from October 1, 2026). A code valid at admission may be retired by discharge.
- id: BR-H14
  text: An encounter carries the sensitivity codes Organization gives for its department, such as SUD for a substance use disorder program. Data attached to it carries the same codes, and disclosure follows the Consent module.
  rationale: Records from US federally assisted substance use disorder programs have extra protection under 42 CFR Part 2, revised with a compliance date of February 16, 2026. Marking the encounter is what lets every module apply it.
- id: BR-H15
  text: A virtual encounter records its connection details and where the patient is.
  rationale: In the United States a practitioner must be licensed where the patient is, as the Clinical appointments rules state.
  variant: ambulatory
- id: BR-H16
  text: length is the time from actual_start to actual_end.
  rationale: FHIR defines length as the time the encounter lasted, less time absent. Leave counts toward neither care nor billing time.
- id: BR-H17
  text: When the Patients module merges two patients, their encounters move to the remaining record; when a merge is undone, encounters recorded since the merge are listed for review.
  rationale: An encounter on a retired record disappears from the patient's history. Only a person can tell whose care a post-merge encounter was.
- id: BR-H18
  text: Every view and change of an encounter is logged with who, when and what. Opening an encounter of a restricted patient needs a reason, as the Patients module requires.
  rationale: HIPAA requires audit controls (45 CFR 164.312(b)). An encounter reveals why someone was seen.
- id: BR-H19
  text: Fields the service sets, such as status, class_history, length, documentation_complete, record_due_at, locked_at, amendments, has_clinical_data and revision, cannot be written by clients.
  rationale: A client writing status skips the lifecycle and its guards; one writing locked_at changes what counts as the legal record.
- id: BR-H20
  text: Every change sends the revision it read, and a stale revision is rejected.
  rationale: Registrars, clinicians and HIM staff change the same encounter; without this, one silently undoes another.
- id: BR-H22
  text: No encounter starts after the patient's recorded death.
  rationale: An encounter after death almost always means the wrong patient. A death recorded in error is retracted in the Patients module first.
- id: BR-H25
  text: A change of responsible department keeps the encounter and adds the new department to department_history with the time it took over.
  rationale: Who was responsible at each point matters for billing, quality reporting and accountability, the same as class history.
- id: BR-H26
  text: Encounters of a patient under legal hold are never erased or anonymised, even after the Patients module's retention ends.
  rationale: The Patients module stops erasure during litigation, and the encounters are the record in question.
- id: BR-H28
  text: When the Patients module records a death, the patient's planned encounters are cancelled with the reason patient_deceased. Encounters in progress are ended by the module that manages them or by a clinician.
  rationale: A planned stay or visit for someone who has died would stay on lists and schedules. Encounters in progress need a person to record what happened.
- id: BR-H30
  text: When Appointments undoes a check-in, the planned encounter opened for it is cancelled with the reason check_in_undone.
  rationale: The check-in was a mistake, so the visit it opened never happened.
- id: BR-H31
  text: When the Patients module marks a patient entered in error, the patient's planned encounters are cancelled with the reason patient_entered_in_error.
  rationale: The record was created by mistake, so a planned visit or stay for it will never happen. Leaving it planned keeps it on lists and schedules.
- id: BR-H32
  text: When the Patients module retracts a death, the encounters cancelled for it (BR-H28) are listed, and encounter.patient_review_needed is emitted for each so they can be planned again.
  rationale: A death recorded for the wrong patient must not silently cost them a planned admission. A person decides whether each one still stands.
- id: BR-H33
  text: A check-in for a patient who already has an open ambulatory encounter in the same visit_grouping scope adds the appointment to that encounter instead of opening another.
  rationale: FHIR lets one encounter fulfil several appointments. A morning of blood draw, X-ray and consultation is one visit to document and bill, not three.
  variant: ambulatory
- id: BR-H34
  text: Every encounter gets a visit number from visit_number_system when it is opened. It is never reused, and stays with the encounter through merges and amendments.
  rationale: Laboratory, radiology and billing systems match their data to a visit by its number (HL7 v2 PV1-19). A reused number would attach results to the wrong visit.
- id: BR-H35
  text: An encounter records the orders it carries out, and Orders confirms each one is for the same patient.
  rationale: FHIR links an encounter to the request that initiated it (basedOn). Without the link nobody can tell whether an order was carried out.
- id: BR-H39
  text: A patient, or their representative within the authority the Patients module records, can read their own encounters electronically as soon as they are recorded. No policy holds them back for review. A patient can ask for their own access to be delayed. Part of an encounter can be withheld only where a licensed professional decides access is reasonably likely to endanger life or physical safety; the decision is recorded and the patient can have it reviewed.
  rationale: HIPAA gives a right of access and allows denial on narrow reviewable grounds (45 CFR 164.524(a)(3)); a delay is likely information blocking (45 CFR 171.103); GDPR gives a right of access (Article 15), and the EHDS gives EU patients immediate access (Article 3).
- id: BR-H40
  text: A patient, or their representative, can ask for an encounter to be corrected. An HIM specialist decides within the Patients module's correction_deadline, extendable once by correction_extension with a written reason. An accepted correction is made as an amendment linked to the request. A denied one gives the reason in writing, and the patient can add a statement of disagreement, which goes with later disclosures.
  rationale: HIPAA requires a decision within 60 days, with one 30-day extension, a written denial and a right to disagree (45 CFR 164.526). GDPR gives rights to rectification and to restriction while accuracy is checked (Articles 16 and 18).
- id: BR-H41
  text: When the Consent module reports a self-pay restriction it agreed that covers an encounter's services (consent.restriction_decided), health_plan_restriction is set on that encounter, and it is cleared only when the Consent module reports the restriction ended. It is never set here directly. Billing must not disclose a restricted encounter to that health plan for payment or operations unless the law requires it.
  rationale: HIPAA requires a covered entity to agree to this restriction when the care was paid in full and the disclosure is for payment or operations and not otherwise required by law (45 CFR 164.522(a)(1)(vi)); the Consent module owns the restriction, so the encounter only reflects it and the two can't disagree.
- id: BR-H42
  text: When a patient who requests a correction asks for the data to be restricted meanwhile, accuracy_contested is set. Everyone reading the encounter sees the contested part marked, and it is cleared when the request is decided.
  rationale: GDPR gives a right to restriction while the accuracy of personal data is checked (Article 18(1)(a)). Marking it, rather than hiding it, keeps the data available for care while warning everyone it is disputed.
- id: BR-H44
  text: Each correction request is kept and linked to the encounter it concerns, with the request, any denial, any statement of disagreement and any rebuttal. It is kept for as long as the encounter, in the United States for at least 6 years from its decision, and in the European Union for as long as member state law requires.
  rationale: HIPAA requires the request, denial, disagreement and rebuttal to be appended or linked to the record (45 CFR 164.526(d)(4)) and kept for 6 years from creation or when last in effect (45 CFR 164.530(j)(2)).
- id: BR-H47
  text: An encounter recorded on the wrong patient is moved to the right patient by an HIM specialist, with a reason. It keeps its id and visit number; on a locked encounter the move is also recorded as an amendment. The move is refused if the right patient is not in use, or the move would break a rule of the module that manages a stay the encounter belongs to; such an encounter moves only with its stay.
  rationale: A visit with notes on the wrong person is a record overlay, which ONC SAFER Guide 6 counts among the most dangerous identification errors. Entered in error is refused once clinical data exists, so a move is the only fix. HL7 moves a visit between patients (A45). Clinical modules keep their entries on the encounter id, so they follow it.
- id: BR-H48
  text: Two encounters of the same patient for one visit are merged by an HIM specialist, with a reason. The duplicate's appointments, locations, participants and diagnoses join the remaining encounter; the duplicate becomes entered in error with status_reason merged and merged_into set, and its visit number stays on the remaining encounter as an old identifier. encounter.merged tells clinical modules to move their entries. Encounters that belong to stays merge only when their stays merge.
  rationale: Double check-ins and patient merges create two encounters for one visit. HL7 merges visits (A42). Keeping the old visit number lets laboratory and billing messages sent with it still find the visit (BR-H34).
- id: BR-H49
  text: An encounter for care given during an EHR downtime is opened afterwards with the times the care actually happened and downtime_entry set. Check-ins and arrivals replayed from other modules keep their original times, and the record due date counts from the real end of care.
  rationale: Paper records must be entered and reconciled after recovery (REF-H22); using the time of entry would misstate when care happened and shorten the time to complete the record.
- id: BR-H50
  text: A practitioner is added as a participant only while the Practitioners module reports them active on the date. When it reports practitioner.suspended, their participations already recorded stay as they are, but they can't be added to encounters or start one until reinstated.
  rationale: Care by a practitioner whose licence lapsed or who is excluded is unlawful and unpaid; what they already did stays part of the record.
- id: BR-H51
  text: In the European Union, a health professional reads encounters only in the categories Identity's professional_access_rules allows for their professional category, under the rules of the member state where the care takes place. Entries outside them are not shown, and the access log records that they were withheld.
  rationale: Member states must set rules on the categories of personal electronic health data accessible by different categories of health professionals or for different healthcare tasks, and the rules of the member state of treatment apply (EHDS Article 11(3) and (4)); these apply to discharge reports, results and imaging from 26 March 2031 and to patient summaries from 26 March 2029 (Article 105).
  jurisdiction: eu

workflows:
- name: Open at check-in
  trigger: Appointments reports appointment.checked_in
  actor: System
  variant: ambulatory
  steps:
  - Create the encounter as planned, subject status arrived, with the appointment id, class and department
  - Report the encounter id to Appointments
  - Emit encounter.created
  outcome: The visit has a record before care starts, and the appointment points to it.
- name: Start care
  trigger: A practitioner begins seeing the patient
  actor: Clinician
  steps:
  - Set actual_start, status in progress and subject status receiving care
  - Add the practitioner as primary performer or attending
  - Report care to the Patients module
  - Emit encounter.started
  outcome: Waiting and care time are recorded separately.
- name: Complete the record
  trigger: Clinical documentation reports the documentation complete
  actor: Health information management (HIM) specialist
  steps:
  - Check a final diagnosis is recorded and no part is open
  - Set completed and locked_at
  - Emit encounter.completed with the diagnoses and class history for Billing
  outcome: The record is complete and locked within the deadline.
- name: Amend a locked encounter
  trigger: A locked encounter needs a correction
  actor: Health information management (HIM) specialist
  steps:
  - Record the new value, the previous value, who, when and why
  - Emit encounter.amended
  outcome: The record is corrected and its history kept.

  text: Every request carries the caller's identity from Identity. Permissions are checked against it (Permission matrix).

  text: Errors use problem details (application/problem+json) with a code member, such as ENCOUNTER_LOCKED.
  reference: RFC 9457
- topic: Pagination

  text: Every change sends the revision it read. A stale revision fails with ENCOUNTER_REVISION_CONFLICT (BR-H20).
- topic: Retries
  text: Every change accepts an Idempotency-Key header; a retry returns the original result (FR-H12).
  reference: IETF draft-ietf-httpapi-idempotency-key-header
- topic: Times
  text: Instants are RFC 3339 timestamps in UTC (CON-H3).
  reference: RFC 3339
- topic: FHIR
  text: GET /encounters/{id} returns an HL7 FHIR R5 Encounter when the request asks for application/fhir+json (FR-H11).
- topic: Versioning
  text: Additive changes don't change the version. A breaking change ships as a new major version with Deprecation and Sunset headers.
  reference: RFC 9745, RFC 8594
- topic: Dates
  text: Dates are ISO 8601 calendar dates, read as the local date where care is given, never a date converted to UTC.
  reference: ISO 8601
- topic: Personal data in URLs
  text: Names, birth dates and identifiers are sent in request bodies or query parameters over TLS, never in paths, and are never written to request logs.
- topic: Rate limits
  text: A caller over its limit gets 429 Too Many Requests with a Retry-After header.
  reference: RFC 6585, RFC 9110

deliveryGuarantees:
- id: DG-H1
  text: Events are published at least once; consumers drop one with a source and id they have seen.
  rationale: A relay can publish twice after a crash. CloudEvents defines source plus id as the duplicate check.
- id: DG-H2
  text: Events for one encounter can arrive out of order; each carries the revision, and consumers ignore a lower one.
  rationale: Brokers and retries don't guarantee order.
- id: DG-H3
  text: Events carry ids, codes and changed field names, never diagnoses text, notes or the patient's details. Consumers read the encounter.
  rationale: Events fan out widely. This follows the minimum necessary standard (45 CFR 164.502(b)) and keeps sensitive encounters out of logs.
- id: DG-H4
  text: Every event carries a unique id, its type, the encounter it is about, when the change happened, and the revision.
  rationale: Consumers order and deduplicate by these, and need the time of the change rather than the time they received it. CloudEvents defines id, type and time for every event.

risks:
- id: RISK-H1
  risk: Records stay incomplete past the deadline.
  impact: Survey findings under the Medicare conditions of participation, and claims that cannot be coded.
  mitigation: record_due_at, the records-due list and encounter.record_overdue (BR-H7, PERF-H1).
- id: RISK-H2
  risk: A retried check-in opens a second encounter.
  impact: Care split across two records.
  mitigation: Idempotency keys (FR-H12, PERF-H4).
- id: RISK-H3
  risk: Sensitive encounter data is disclosed without the required consent.
  impact: A Part 2 violation and harm to the patient.
  mitigation: Sensitivity codes propagate to every attached entry (BR-H14), and Consent decides disclosure.
- id: RISK-H4
  risk: A locked record is changed silently.
  impact: The legal record and claims no longer match.
  mitigation: Locking and amendments with history (BR-H8).
decisions:
- id: ADR-H1
  decision: Clinical encounters are specified at the healthcare level, with an EHR layer for hospital stays and emergency visits.
  rationale: Clinics, telehealth and other health applications have encounters too; only inpatient stays and emergency flow are hospital work.
  date: '2026-10-09'
- id: ADR-H2
  decision: A class change keeps the same encounter, with class history.
  rationale: FHIR R5 keeps class history in EncounterHistory, and one record keeps notes and results together across the change.
  alternatives: A new encounter per class. Rejected because it splits one stay's care across records.
  date: '2026-10-07'
- id: ADR-H3
  decision: One encounter per visit or stay, with part_of for consultations and sub-stays where a hospital wants them.
  rationale: FHIR notes aggregation varies between organisations and countries. A single stay record with optional parts fits both practices.
  date: '2026-10-07'
- id: ADR-H5
  decision: When an ambulatory encounter opens is a setting (encounter_opens_at).
  rationale: FHIR's Appointment and Encounter pages describe different moments, and providers differ. A setting keeps both correct.
  date: '2026-10-08'

Every note, order and result in the record belongs to an encounter. This
module keeps that record of each interaction between a patient and a care
provider: when it happened, who was responsible, where the patient was, why
they came and what was found.

## The appointment is the plan, the encounter is what happened

Checking in opens the encounter, but arrival is not care. The encounter waits,
planned, until a practitioner starts (BR-H2), so the record shows both how long
the patient waited and how long they were seen.

## Discharged is not done

Care can be over while the record isn't. An encounter stays discharged until
its documentation and final diagnosis are complete, within the deadline the
law or the provider sets (BR-H7). Then it locks, and later changes are
amendments that keep what they replaced (BR-H8).

## Codes are checked against the right day

ICD-10-CM changes every 1 October. Diagnoses are checked against the release
in effect on the right date, so a stay that crosses the change isn't rejected
for a code that was valid when it ended (BR-H13).
