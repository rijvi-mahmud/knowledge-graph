title: Clinical appointments
description: 'What every healthcare application adds to appointments: patients and practitioners, check-in, protected health data and the record it belongs to.'
module: appointment
version: 0.10.3
status: draft
industry: healthcare
extends: core/appointment
purpose: 'Add what every healthcare application needs on top of the core Appointment specification: patients and practitioners, check-in with identity verification, protected health data, communication patients can rely on, and the medical record an appointment becomes part of. Works in the United States and the European Union.'
scope:
- Patients, practitioners and personal representatives as participants
- Check-in with patient identity verification
- Reminders and messages that protect health information
- Access logging, record retention and the limits of erasure
- Language assistance and virtual appointments
- Exchange with other systems as HL7 FHIR Appointment
- Jurisdiction settings for the United States and the European Union
- Disability accommodations
- A patient's right to a copy of their appointment data
- Automated reminder texts and calls in the United States
- Where a virtual appointment may be held
nonGoals:
- The clinical record of the visit itself. That belongs to the Encounter module.
- Insurance eligibility and billing, which differ by country and payer.
- Patient registration, identifiers, contact details and representatives. The Patients module owns them; this layer only asks for them.
- Charging fees for missed or late-cancelled appointments. Billing decides, using appointment.no_show and the late cancellation flag on appointment.cancelled.
- Detecting and reporting data breaches, which belongs to the Security module.
- Maintaining code sets. This layer only stores codes and checks them against the configured code system.
actors:
- name: Patient
  description: The person receiving care. A participant with the role patient.
- name: Practitioner
  description: A clinician providing care. A participant with the role practitioner.
- name: Personal representative
  description: A person allowed to act for the patient, such as the parent of a minor or a legal guardian.
- name: Front desk
  description: Staff who check patients in. A Scheduler with the check-in permission.
- name: Privacy officer
  description: Reviews who accessed appointment data.
assumptions:
- id: AS-H1
  text: The Patients module holds each patient's identifiers, such as name and date of birth, where to contact them, and who may act for them.
  rationale: Check-in verifies against these identifiers (BR-H2), reminders follow the communication preference (BR-H4), and representatives act within what Patients allows (BR-H5).
- id: AS-H2
  text: 'The deployment has a lawful basis to process health data for care: treatment under HIPAA in the United States, and GDPR Article 9(2)(h) in the European Union.'
  rationale: Without it, no appointment data could be held at all. The legal basis is the deployment's responsibility, not this module's.
- id: AS-H3
  text: The jurisdiction setting is set with legal advice before go-live. Record retention is the Patients module's setting.
  rationale: Retention and erasure rules differ by US state and EU member state. Guessing them risks both unlawful deletion and unlawful retention.
glossary:
- term: Active appointment
  definition: 'An appointment that holds its slot: booked, checked in, or pending while pending_holds_slot is on.'
- term: Protected health information
  definition: Health information that identifies a person (HIPAA, 45 CFR 160.103). In the EU, data concerning health is a special category of personal data (GDPR Article 9).
- term: Personal representative
  definition: Someone the law treats as the patient for privacy purposes, such as a parent or guardian (45 CFR 164.502(g)).
- term: Check-in
  definition: Recording that the patient has arrived and their identity is verified.
- term: Entered in error
  definition: An appointment recorded by mistake. It is kept, but never counted as a real appointment.
concepts:
- name: Medical record
  description: The appointment and its history are part of the patient's record, which is kept for a legal minimum period.

  type: enum
  required: true
  description: Current lifecycle state, with the healthcare states.
  constraints: 'pending | booked | checked_in | completed | no_show | cancelled | entered_in_error; changed only through operations; codes: FHIR R5 AppointmentStatus: pending, booked, checked-in, fulfilled, noshow, cancelled, entered-in-error'
- name: service_type
  type: code
  required: false
  description: The service to be performed, coded with service_code_system, such as a cardiology consultation (FHIR Appointment.serviceType).
  constraints: 'codes: service_code_system, such as HCPCS with CPT-4 in the United States'
- name: reason
  type: code or text
  required: false
  description: Why the appointment was booked, coded with diagnosis_code_system. Clinical, so it is health information (FHIR Appointment.reason).
- name: priority

  required: false
  description: From 1 (highest) to 9 (lowest), as in FHIR and iCalendar.
  constraints: 1 to 9
- name: patient_instruction
  type: text
  required: false
  description: What the patient needs to know or do, such as fasting from 8 pm the night before.
- name: virtual_service
  type: connection details
  required: false
  description: How to join a virtual appointment (FHIR Appointment.virtualService).
- name: interpreter_language
  type: BCP 47 tag
  required: false
  description: The language the patient needs an interpreter for at this appointment. Defaults to the language and interpreter need the Patients module holds; set here only when this visit differs.
- name: acting_representative
  type: string
  required: false
  description: The personal representative who booked or changed the appointment, if one did, as the Patients module records them.
- name: checked_in_at
  type: instant
  required: false
  constraints: set when status = checked_in
- name: identity_verified_with
  type: string[]
  required: false
  description: The identifier types used at check-in, such as name and date_of_birth.
  constraints: at least 2 when checked in
- name: accommodation_needs
  type: string[]
  required: false
  description: Disability accommodations the patient needs, such as a sign-language interpreter or an accessible room. Recorded per appointment, because needs differ by visit and place.
- name: patient_location
  type: string
  required: false
  description: 'Where the patient will be during a virtual appointment: a US state or an EU member state.'
  constraints: required for virtual appointments
- name: cancelled_reason
  type: code and optional text
  required: false
  description: 'Why the appointment was cancelled: a code, and text where the code is other or staff add detail.'
  constraints: 'required when status = cancelled; erasure clears the text and keeps the code (BR-11); codes: HL7 appointment-cancellation-reason (FHIR Appointment.cancellationReason), such as pat patient, pat-dec deceased, pat-fb feeling better, prov provider, prov-hosp provider hospitalized, oth-err error, oth-int no interpreter available, oth-weath weather, other; reasons set by the service carry their own code mapped to one HL7 code (BR-H22)'
businessRules:
- id: BR-H1
  text: Every appointment has at least one participant with the role patient, and at least one with the role practitioner.
  rationale: A clinical appointment is care given to a patient by a practitioner. FHIR models both as participants of an Appointment.
- id: BR-H2
  text: At check-in, the patient's identity is verified with at least two identifiers, such as name and date of birth. A room or location is never one of them.
  rationale: The Joint Commission requires at least two identifiers when providing care, treatment and services, and never the room number (NPG.01.01.01 for hospitals from 2026, NPSG.01.01.01 for its other programs). WHO Patient Safety Solution 2 recommends the same before care is given. Verifying at check-in matches the visit, and everything later recorded on it, to the right person.
- id: BR-H3
  text: 'Reminders and other messages to patients contain only what''s needed to attend: the patient''s name, the date, time and place, and the provider''s name and number. Never the reason, diagnosis or a service that reveals a condition.'
  rationale: HHS guidance permits reminders but says providers "should take care to limit the amount of information disclosed", for example only "name and number and other information necessary to confirm an appointment" (FAQ 198). GDPR requires data to be "limited to what is necessary" (Article 5(1)(c)). Messages reach shared phones, postcards and lock screens.
- id: BR-H4
  text: If a patient asked to be contacted by another means or at another address, every appointment message uses it.
  rationale: In the United States, providers must accommodate reasonable requests to communicate by alternative means or at alternative locations, without asking why (45 CFR 164.522(b)). In the EU, GDPR requires appropriate security and confidentiality of personal data (Article 5(1)(f)). For some patients an ordinary reminder could be unsafe.
- id: BR-H5
  text: A personal representative can book and manage appointments for the patient they represent, when the Patients module confirms they may act for the patient for this appointment. Every action they take records them as acting_representative.
  rationale: HIPAA treats a personal representative as the patient (45 CFR 164.502(g)), but not for a minor's care the minor could consent to themselves or that a parent agreed to keep confidential (164.502(g)(3)), and state and member state law add their own rules. That is a judgment about the person, so Identity makes it and this module applies the answer.
- id: BR-H6
  text: When a patient needs an interpreter, the appointment records the language so a qualified interpreter can be arranged at no cost to the patient.
  rationale: In the United States, covered entities must offer qualified interpreters free of charge (45 CFR 92.201). In the EU, language assistance depends on member state law. Recording the need at booking is what makes it possible on the day, everywhere.
- id: BR-H7
  text: Every view and every change of an appointment is logged with who, when and what.
  rationale: HIPAA requires audit controls that record activity in systems holding electronic health information (45 CFR 164.312(b)), and GDPR Article 32 requires security appropriate to the risk. Core history covers changes, not views.
- id: BR-H8
  text: An appointment recorded by mistake is marked entered in error. It is kept, hidden from schedules, and excluded from statistics.
  rationale: Records are never deleted in a medical record. FHIR has the same entered-in-error status.
- id: BR-H9
  text: Connection details for a virtual appointment are shared only with its participants.
  rationale: The link gives access to a session where health information is discussed.
- id: BR-H10
  text: Check-in is allowed from check_in_window before the start until the end.
  rationale: Checking in much earlier would mark someone as present who has not arrived for this appointment.
- id: BR-11
  text: A request to erase a patient's appointment data is refused while the Patients module's record retention for the patient applies, because the appointment and its history are part of the medical record. When retention ends, personal fields are erased as the core Appointment specification describes.
  rationale: HIPAA gives no right to erasure, and US hospitals must keep records for at least 5 years (42 CFR 482.24(b)(1)), longer in many states. In the EU, GDPR Article 17(3)(c) lets health data be kept for care under Article 9(2)(h). Erasing on request would break the record.
- id: BR-H11
  text: When a patient needs a disability accommodation, such as a sign-language interpreter or an accessible room, the appointment records it so it can be arranged at no cost to the patient.
  rationale: In the United States, covered entities must provide auxiliary aids and services at no cost so communication with people with disabilities is as effective as with others (45 CFR 92.202). In the EU this depends on member state law.
- id: BR-H12
  text: 'A patient, or their personal representative, can obtain a copy of their appointment data. The request is answered within access_request_deadline: 30 days in the United States, extendable once by 30 days with written notice; one month in the EU, extendable by two months. In the EU the first copy is free.'
  rationale: HIPAA gives individuals the right to inspect and obtain a copy of their health information (45 CFR 164.524). GDPR gives a right of access and a free first copy (Article 15) within one month (Article 12(3)).
- id: BR-H13
  text: In the United States, automated reminder texts and calls go only to the number the patient provided, name the provider and how to contact them, are limited to one a day and three a week per patient, keep texts to 160 characters and calls to one minute, and offer an opt-out in every message, honoured immediately.
  rationale: These are the conditions under which healthcare messages to mobile phones are exempt from prior consent (47 CFR 64.1200(a)(9)(iv)). Breaking any of them turns a reminder into an unlawful call or text.
  jurisdiction: us
- id: BR-H14
  text: A virtual appointment records where the patient will be. In the United States, the practitioner must be licensed or legally permitted to practise in the state where the patient is located.
  rationale: 'HHS: providers "must ... be licensed or legally permitted to practice in the state where the patient is located". In the EU, telemedicine is considered provided in the member state where the provider is established (Directive 2011/24/EU, Article 3(d)), so the patient''s location is recorded but not checked against a licence.'
- id: BR-H15
  text: reason and service_type are coded with the configured code systems, and a code must be valid in the release in effect on the appointment date.
  rationale: 'HIPAA adopts ICD-10-CM for diagnoses and HCPCS with CPT-4 for services (45 CFR 162.1002). In Europe, coding is set nationally: WHO''s ICD-11 has been the global standard since 1 January 2022, but countries are still moving from ICD-10. Codes are revised over time, so a code is checked against the release in force on the day.'
- id: BR-H16
  text: 'When the Patients module merges two patient records, every appointment of the retired record is moved to the record that remains: the patient participant changes, the appointment keeps its id, and the change is recorded in its history. The overlap rule (BR-4) is not applied to the move: appointments that now overlap are kept, and appointment.patient_review_needed is emitted for each so a Scheduler decides.'
  rationale: After a merge, the retired record is never used again. An appointment left on it would disappear from the patient's schedule and from check-in. Duplicate records often hold the same visit booked twice; refusing the merge would keep the duplicate, and silently cancelling one would lose a real booking.
- id: BR-H17
  text: When a patient dies, their appointments that have not started are cancelled with the reason patient_deceased, and no message about them is sent to the patient. If the death is retracted, the cancelled appointments are listed for a Scheduler to rebook.
  rationale: Reminders and appointment letters to a person who has died distress their family. A death recorded in error is corrected by a person, so rebooking is a person's decision too.
- id: BR-H18
  text: When a merge is undone, appointments moved by it return to the restored record. Appointments booked on the remaining record since the merge stay there, and appointment.patient_review_needed is emitted for each so a Scheduler checks whose they are.
  rationale: Only the appointments the merge moved are known to belong to the restored record. Those booked since could belong to either person, so a person checks.
- id: BR-H19
  text: When the Patients module marks a patient entered in error, their appointments that have not started are cancelled with the reason patient_entered_in_error, and no message is sent.
  rationale: The record was created by mistake, so there is nobody to remind. Keeping the bookings holds slots other patients need.
- id: BR-H20
  text: A checked-in appointment can return to booked, by undoing the check-in, until care has started. Undoing it emits appointment.check_in_undone.
  rationale: Checking a patient in to the wrong appointment is a common front-desk mistake. Without a way back, the right appointment can't be checked in and the wrong visit stays on record.
- id: BR-H21
  text: In the United States, when an appointment is booked or rescheduled at least 3 business days ahead, Billing is told the booking time and the scheduled date, so a good faith estimate can be given to an uninsured or self-pay patient in time.
  rationale: The No Surprises Act requires a good faith estimate for uninsured or self-pay patients within 1 business day of scheduling when the service is 3 to 9 business days away, and within 3 business days when it is 10 or more (45 CFR 149.610). Scheduling starts the clock, so Billing must learn of it at once.
  jurisdiction: us
- id: BR-H22
  text: 'A cancellation records a code from the HL7 appointment cancellation reasons. Reasons the service sets itself keep their own code, mapped to one HL7 code for exchange: patient_deceased to pat-dec and patient_entered_in_error to oth-err.'
  rationale: FHIR Appointment.cancellationReason uses the HL7 appointment-cancellation-reason code system, so other systems and reports can tell a patient cancelling from a provider or an error. A local free-text reason can't be counted or exchanged.
- id: BR-H23
  text: A new appointment cannot be booked, rebooked or rescheduled for a patient the Patients module records as deceased.
  rationale: BR-H17 cancels a deceased patient's appointments, but a booking made afterwards, such as from an old referral, would send reminders to a grieving family and hold a slot nobody will use.
- id: BR-H24
  text: Where no_show_limit is set, a patient, or their representative, cannot book for themselves once the patient has had that many no-shows within no_show_lookback; staff can still book for them. The refusal tells them to contact the hospital.
  rationale: Many hospitals restrict self-booking after repeated missed appointments, so staff can talk to the patient first. No-show rates are higher for patients facing transport, cost or language barriers, so a blanket refusal can cut off care; the limit is off by default and never stops staff booking.
- id: BR-H25
  text: A participant's response is exchanged as FHIR participant.status (accepted, declined, tentative, needs-action), and whether they are required as participant.required.
  rationale: FHIR R5 requires a status for every appointment participant, with the same four values as iCalendar, so the core response maps one to one.

stateMachine:
  initial: booked
  states:
  - pending
  - booked
  - checked_in
  - cancelled
  - completed
  - no_show
  - entered_in_error
  transitions:
  - from: pending
    to: booked
    trigger: confirm
    guard: confirm_by has not passed, revision matches, and the slot is still free when pending_holds_slot is off
  - from: pending
    to: pending
    trigger: reschedule or update
    guard: before the end, revision matches
  - from: pending
    to: cancelled
    trigger: cancel
    guard: cancelled_reason provided, revision matches
  - from: pending
    to: cancelled
    trigger: expire
    guard: confirm_by has passed
  - from: booked
    to: booked
    trigger: reschedule or update
    guard: before the end, new slot is free and in the future, revision matches
  - from: booked
    to: checked_in
    trigger: check_in
    guard: within check_in_window before the start and before the end, identity verified with two identifiers
  - from: booked
    to: cancelled
    trigger: cancel
    guard: before the end, cancelled_reason provided, revision matches
  - from: booked
    to: completed
    trigger: complete
    guard: start_at has passed
  - from: booked
    to: no_show
    trigger: record_no_show
    guard: start_at has passed, at least one absent participant named
  - from: checked_in
    to: completed
    trigger: complete
    guard: the patient was seen
  - from: checked_in
    to: cancelled
    trigger: cancel
    guard: the patient left without being seen, with that as the reason
  - from: pending
    to: entered_in_error
    trigger: mark_entered_in_error
    guard: actor is a Scheduler
  - from: booked
    to: entered_in_error
    trigger: mark_entered_in_error
    guard: actor is a Scheduler
  - from: no_show
    to: completed
    trigger: correct_attendance
    guard: actor is a Scheduler
  - from: checked_in
    to: booked
    trigger: undo check-in
    guard: care has not started, revision matches
workflows:
- name: Check in
  trigger: The patient arrives
  actor: Front desk
  steps:
  - Find the appointment
  - Verify the patient with two identifiers, such as name and date of birth
  - Ask the patient to confirm their details, and record it in the Patients module
  - Record checked_in_at and identity_verified_with, set status to checked_in and increase revision
  - Emit appointment.checked_in
  - Report care to the Patients module
  outcome: The patient is recorded as present and verified, ready to be seen.
- name: Mark entered in error
  trigger: An appointment was created by mistake, for example for the wrong patient
  actor: Scheduler
  steps:
  - Set status to entered_in_error with a reason, and increase revision
  - Withdraw pending reminders and release the slot
  - Emit appointment.entered_in_error
  outcome: The mistake is kept for the record but no longer affects schedules or statistics.
- name: Follow a patient merge
  trigger: Patients emits patient.merged
  actor: System
  steps:
  - Find the appointments whose patient participant is the retired record
  - Change the participant to the remaining record, record it in history, and increase revision
  - Emit appointment.updated for each
  outcome: Every appointment shows on the one record in use.
- name: Follow a death
  trigger: Patients emits patient.deceased
  actor: System
  steps:
  - Find the patient's appointments that have not started
  - Cancel each with the reason patient_deceased, without messaging the patient
  - Emit appointment.cancelled for each
  outcome: No one is booked or reminded after their death.
- name: Follow a patient entered in error
  trigger: Patients emits patient.entered_in_error
  actor: System
  steps:
  - Find the patient's appointments that have not started
  - Cancel each with the reason patient_entered_in_error, without messaging anyone
  - Emit appointment.cancelled for each
  outcome: No slot is held for a record that never existed.

decisions:
- id: ADR-H1
  decision: Check-in is a status between booked and completed.
  rationale: FHIR has checked-in and arrived statuses, and front desks work from the list of who is here.
  alternatives: Record arrival only on the encounter. Rejected because scheduling needs to know who has arrived before an encounter starts.
  date: '2026-10-05'
- id: ADR-H2
  decision: Retention overrides erasure (BR-11) while the medical record must be kept.
  rationale: Both US and EU law protect the medical record from erasure on request.
  date: '2026-10-05'
- id: ADR-H3
  decision: One jurisdiction setting, with explicit settings for anything that varies inside a jurisdiction.
  rationale: Retention differs by US state and EU member state, so the jurisdiction alone cannot decide it.
  date: '2026-10-05'
- id: ADR-H4
  decision: Whether someone may act for a patient, including for minors, is the Patients module's decision; this module asks for each appointment.
  rationale: The rules depend on the patient's age, the kind of care and US state or EU member state law. Deciding them here would copy another module's job.
  date: '2026-10-06'

A clinical appointment is still an appointment: everything in the
[core Appointment specification](/docs/core/appointment) applies. This layer adds what
changes when the participants are a patient and a practitioner and the data is
health information.

## The patient is verified at the desk, not assumed

Check-in uses two identifiers, such as name and date of birth, never a room
number (BR-H2). Two patients with the same name in the same waiting room is an
ordinary day in a hospital, and checking in the wrong one attaches care and
results to the wrong person.

## A reminder says when, never why

A reminder lands on a shared phone, a postcard, a lock screen. So it carries
only what the patient needs to turn up: name, date, time, place and a number to
call (BR-H3). If a patient asked to be contacted somewhere else, every message
goes there (BR-H4).

## The appointment is part of the medical record

Appointments are not erased on request while the record must be kept (BR-11).
US hospitals keep records for at least 5 years, and GDPR lets health data be
kept for care. Mistakes are marked entered in error, never deleted (BR-H8), and
every view and change is logged (BR-H7).

## The United States and Europe, from one setting

The `jurisdiction` setting chooses the legal regime, and the Patients module's record retention is
set per state or member state, with legal advice (AS-H3). Rules that apply in only one
region, such as the US limits on automated reminder texts (BR-H13), are labelled "US only"
or "EU only", and a project in one region can hide the other's.
